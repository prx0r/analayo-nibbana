# The A / B / C structure

This idea works perfectly with your recursive depth system.

## A layer
You just see the thing spinning.

- no explanation
- maybe it appears in different scenes
- maybe close-ups
- maybe its hum becomes part of the soundtrack

The audience just registers:
**there is this ongoing motion**

## B layer
Now we start to notice:

- it is sometimes slowing
- it nearly stops
- then in the next shot it is spinning again
- maybe we hear a tiny sound each time it somehow returns
- maybe the music hints at re-feeding

Now the audience begins to ask:
**wait, what is sustaining this?**

## C layer
At the end of the section:

We finally see the hand.

Not dramatically.
Not villainously.
Almost casually.

A hand reaches in and spins it again.

That reveal is devastating because it retroactively explains the whole section.

And then the deeper implication lands:

> **the suffering process is not self-existing.  
> it is being maintained.**

And the even deeper one:

> **the one who suffers may also be the one re-spinning it.**

That is incredibly strong.

# Possible dangers

## 1. Too metaphorical
If the object is too weird or arty, the audience won't connect it to life.

So keep it simple.

## 2. Too obvious too early
If the hand appears too soon, the mystery is gone.

So delay it.

## 3. Too moralistic
If it feels like "look, idiot human, you do this," it gets preachy.

So the reveal should be gentle, almost sad or compassionate.
