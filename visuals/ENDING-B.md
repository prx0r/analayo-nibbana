Yes — that's **exactly the point**, and I think this may actually be **better** than letting the music box finish audibly.

Because now the ending becomes:

> **the moment before grasping**  
> and the refusal to restart the spell.

That's cleaner.

## Why this works so well

By the end of the film, the viewer has learned:

- **music box turning** = the song returns
- **song returns** = the beloved returns
- **beloved returns** = enchantment returns
- **turning it** = continuing the whole thing

So if the final shot is:

- candle blown out
- man sits at the music box
- the box is still
- his hand could turn it
- but doesn't

then the viewer **feels the urge** for him.

That's brilliant because the audience completes the mechanism internally.

silence.

# This ending is stronger than showing the act of not winding

That's a subtle but important distinction.

## Weaker version:

Why stronger:
- less preachy
- more contemplative
- the audience experiences the wanting
- the scene lets the insight arise rather than declaring it

That's the right move.
