Yes. The narrative is established. Now we need to synchronize the audience's experience with the teaching—not illustrate every sentence literally.

I've checked the actual `analayo-nibbana` assembly manifests. The good news is that they already give us nearly everything required for timing: ordered source clips, narration, pauses and bells.

The key distinction is:

The spoken essay develops the understanding. The visual film develops the anticipation. They meet at carefully chosen moments of recognition.

For example, we don't need Ānālayo to say "craving" at the exact instant someone winds the ballerina music box. Ideally, the viewer has already witnessed the winding, heard the melody and felt the desire for more. The teaching then gives them a way to understand that experience.

## 1. Three clocks, not one

The teaching clock

Fixed by the recorded lectures, your human introductions and the question-and-pause structure. We shouldn't distort source quotations to accommodate a camera movement.

The musical clock

The beloved's recurring melody is progressively revealed. Path 3's winding mechanism explains why the listener keeps receiving another musical cycle. Musical passages generally yield to the lecturer's voice.

The visual clock

The farewell story advances chronologically, while the mirror, candle and music box recur at different levels of understanding. Visual shots can continue quietly through long passages of speech.

The effects will be strongest when these clocks occasionally align—especially at the winding revelation and the final stillness—but they shouldn't be locked together constantly.

## 3. We do need to make room for the music

There's one practical problem in the present audio manifests.

Most of the existing audio is spoken material, separated by 1.5-second breaths and 2.5-second pauses. That's not enough uninterrupted space for Path 2's musical enchantment to develop.

So I would deliberately create protected musical passages between selected teaching beats.

TEACHING

The speaker establishes the question.

MUSICAL EXPERIENCE

Beloved, dance, meadow. The melody develops without speech competing with it.

PAUSE / RECOGNITION

Give the viewer space to notice their response.

TEACHING RESUMES

The source supplies the explanation that deepens or challenges what was just experienced.

This is much more effective than letting the song compete with an hour of lecture audio.

It also means we can save the longest, most satisfying musical passages for later returns. The audience does not receive the entire emotional reward immediately.

Plant. Introduce the beloved, a memorable melody fragment, and the winding sound. Establish the recurring questions with minimal imagery.

Enchant. Let the audience fall in love with the melody and imagery. Repeated movements and returns become familiar.

C

Deepen the teaching, without renewing the story. The final audiovisual coda is black and silent. It supplies no further image or musical reward.

There are two important reconciliation decisions here.

First, your present repository uses L0 Seed → L1 Core → L2 Gold → L3 Full. Its opening Seed is already around ten minutes of teaching, whereas our proposed visual Essay 0 is tiny. We should introduce the short visual seed without silently replacing the existing lecture cut.

Second, C contains many hours of source material. Making all of C silent would remove that teaching. So I'd distinguish the complete C expansion from its final silent coda. The latter is the moment we have locked as black and silence.

The current ending also has a seed reprise and motto bell before its 30 seconds of silence. I'd move the final audible punctuation before the music-box stillness, so nothing rings after the emotional ending.

## 5. The timing principle that will make the ending land

We can compose much of the earlier film against a recurring musical pulse inspired by the music box.

But I'd use that pulse mainly to govern expectation, not mechanically force every cut onto a beat. Beautiful scenes can breathe; the visual returns and significant gestures carry the stronger rhythmic relationship.

As the story advances:

- Path 2 rewards anticipation with another musical phrase.
- Path 3 reveals the winding that enables repetition.
- Path 4 interrupts automatic identification.
- Path 5 introduces deliberate pauses and unforced attention.
- Path 6 stops organizing the world around the song.

Then the man sits before the music box.

We hold the shot beyond the moment when another phrase would normally begin.

Nothing happens.

The audience has been trained by the editing and music to expect a continuation. That anticipation is the final thing we want them to notice.

The important technical next step is therefore not rendering more footage. It's creating a cue sheet against the audio master: each recorded source beat receives a question, visual path, shot ID, soundtrack treatment, entrance cue and exit cue.

Once the complete audio is assembled, those cues can acquire absolute timestamps. Until then, the recorded clip boundaries and pauses provide the reliable anchors.

The big principle I'd preserve is:

The visuals should arrive early enough to create the experience; the teaching should arrive at the moment when it can make that experience intelligible. And the ending should arrive without another experience being supplied.

That gives us the basis for timing the whole film, rather than treating it as illustrated narration.
