> "It leaves a permanent effect on the Arahant, so much so that upon reflection he sees that his influxes are extinct, just as a man with his hands and feet cut off, knows upon reflection that his limbs are gone."

**S01 · Rādha chain (the path to nibbana)**
> "You have gone beyond the scope of your questions... For the holy life is merged in Nibbana, its consummation is Nibbana, its culmination is Nibbana."
> "The holy life gets merged in Nibbana, just as rivers get merged in the sea. Where the holy life is lived out to the full, Nibbana is right there."

**S01 · The purpose question is incoherent**
> "If there is any aim or purpose in attaining Nibbana, Nibbana would not be the ultimate aim. If Nibbana is the ultimate aim, there should no aim in attaining Nibbana. Though it may well sound a tautology, one has to say that Nibbana is the ultimate aim for the simple reason that there is no aim beyond it."
> "It leads one on and on in existence, like the carrot before the donkey."

**S01 · The Vedic dilemma (being and non-being)**
> "Katham asatah sat jayeta: "How could being come out of non-being?" The Vedic confusion between sat and asat."
> "There was neither non-being nor being at the start."

> "Viññāṇaṃ anidassanaṃ, anantaṃ sabbato pabhaṃ, ettha āpo ca paṭhavī, tejo vāyo na gādhati, ettha dīghañca rassañca, aṇuṃ thūlaṃ subhāsubhaṃ, ettha nāmañca rūpañca, asesaṃ uparujjhati, viññāṇassa nirodhena, etth'etaṃ uparujjhati."
> "Where consciousness is signless, boundless, all-luminous — that's where earth, water, fire and air find no footing. There both long and short, small and great, fair and foul — there 'name-and-form' are wholly destroyed. With the cessation of consciousness this is all destroyed."

**S08 · Nibbana = cessation of existence (bhavanirodha)**
> "Nibbana has been defined as the cessation of existence. The Buddha says that when he is preaching about the cessation of existence, some people, particularly the brahmins who cling to a soul theory, bring up the charge of nihilism against him. Not only those brahmins and heretics believing in a soul theory, but even some Buddhist scholars are scared of the term bhavanirodha, fearing that it leads to a nihilistic interpretation."

**S08 · Asmimana eradication = nibbana**
> "Sammamanabhisamaya antam akasi dukkhassa — by rightly understanding conceit, he made an end of suffering."
> "Asmanasamugghatam papunati dittheva dhamme Nibbanam — one arrives at the eradication of the conceit 'am' which in itself is the attainment of Nibbana here and now."

**S08 · The ambrosial deathless**
> "The arahants partake of ambrosial deathlessness. What actually happened in the case of the Venerable arahant Adhimutta was that the bandit chief understood the Dhamma and set him free... But even if he had killed him, Venerable Adhimutta would have passed away, experiencing the ambrosial deathless. Why? Because he can let go before Mara comes to grab."
> "It is that bliss of deliverance, the freedom from the fear of death. Needless to say that it requires no seal of ever-lastingness."

**S12 · Nibbana = nippapanca (one of the 33 epithets)**
> "Nippapanca is regarded as one of the epithets of Nibbana in a long list of thirty-three. Like dukkhu pasama, quelling of suffering, papanca vupasama, 'quelling of prolificity', is also recognized as an epithet of Nibbana. It is also referred to as papancanirodha, 'cessation of prolificity'."

**S12 · The Tathagatas are nippapanca**
> "No track is there in the air, and no recluse elsewhere. This populace delights in prolificity, but 'Thus-gone-ones' are non-prolific."
> "The Tathagatas are free from papanca, that is to say they are nippapanca, whereas the world delights in papanca."

**S12 · The Udana verse**
> "And at that time the Exalted One was seated contemplating his own abandonment of reckonings born of prolific perception."
> "To whom there are no proliferations and standstills, who has gone beyond the bond and the deadlock, in that craving-free sage, as he fares along, the world with its gods sees nothing to decry."

**S17 · The Itivuttaka form/formless verses**
> "Those beings that go to realms of form, and those who are settled in formless realms, not understanding the fact of cessation, come back again and again to existence. Those who, having comprehended realms of form, do not settle in formless realms, are released in the experience of cessation."
> "The formless, or arupa, is like the shadow of form, rupa. When one comprehends form, one also understands that the formless, too, is not worthwhile settling in."

**S17 · The salayatananirodha riddle**
> "Therefore, monks, that sphere should be known wherein the eye ceases and perceptions of form fade away... where the mind ceases and perceptions of mind objects fade away."
> "The Buddha got up and entered the monastery, leaving the monks perplexed."

**S17 · Nibbana as ayatana (sphere)**
> "Āyatana is an allusion to arahattaphalasamadhi. Its significance is psychological."
> "The appatittham, appavattam and anarammanam are suggestive of the three doorways to deliverance: appanihita vimokkha (undirected), sunyata vimokkha (void), animitta vimokkha (signless)."

**S17 · The touching of the deathless**
> "To be free from touch is in itself the 'touching' of the deathless element. What we mean is that, as far as the fear of death is concerned, here we have the freedom from the pain of death and the freedom from the concept of death itself."
> "Nibbana is called a bliss devoid of feeling, avedayita sukha."

### Pass 5 — Similes complete — the full imagery
*Every simile for this question across the whole series, quoted long — fire, whirlpool, mirror, razor, lotus.*

**S01 · The fire simile (the central metaphor)**
> "Fire burns with the help of firewood. "What is burning?" — the wood burns because of the fire, the fire burns because of the wood. This itself shows idappaccayata (this-to-that relatedness). The fire simile has very deep significance."
> "Upadana means BOTH fuel and grasping. The fire grasps the wood; the wood grasps the fire. We call it "firewood." Upadana-paccaya-bhavo: dependent on grasping is existence. Two links in one image."
> "When a fire goes out with exhaustion of fuel, it is absurd to ask in which direction the fire has gone. All one can say: "it comes to reckoned as gone out" (nibbuto tveva sankham gacchati). Just a reckoning, an idiom, a worldly usage — not to be taken too literally."

**S01 · The elevenfold fire**
> "Existence is said to be an eleven-fold fire. So the entire existence is a raging fire. Lust, hate, delusion — all these are fires. Therefore Nibbana may be best rendered by the word 'extinction'."

**S01 · Warming vs cooling (Magandiya simile)**
> "It is an attempt to warm up, not to cool down."
> "It can in no way be compared to the extinction and the cooling down of the Arahants."
> "Buddhaghosa's heretic: if Nibbana is lust-extinction, animals have it too (they extinguish lust by enjoyment). Refuted: animals warm up; arahants cool down. Same word — "extinguishing" — two opposite processes."

**S01 · The exam simile (the certificate)**
> "In the case of an examination, after answering the question paper, one has to wait for the results to get a pass. Here it is different. As soon as you have answered the paper correctly, you have passed immediately and the certificate is already there."
> "One gets the certitude that birth is extinct and that the holy life is lived out to the full"

**S01 · Vacchagotta and the tetralemma (the accusation the fire answers)**
> "The wandering ascetic Vacchagotta, as well as many others, accused the Buddha of teaching a doctrine of annihilation: Sato sattassa ucchedaṃ vināsaṃ vibhavaṃ paññāpeti."
> "Their accusation was that the Buddha proclaims the annihilation, destruction and non-existence of a being that is existent. And the Buddha answered them fairly and squarely with the fire simile."
> "Now if a fire is burning in front of you dependent on grass and twigs as fuel, you would know that it is burning dependently and not independently, that there is no fire in the abstract. And when the fire goes out, with the exhaustion of that fuel, you would know that it has gone out because the conditions for its existence are no more."

**S01 · Upanishad cross-references (fire without fuel)**
> "As the form of fire when latent in its source is not seen and yet its seed is not destroyed, but may be seized again and again in its source by means of the drill"
> "Maitri Upanisad 6.34 (Radhakrishnan): Even as fire without fuel becomes extinct in its own place, even so thought, by the cessation of activity, becomes extinct in its own source."
> "Both are cross-references, not suttas: the fire-out-in-its-own-place intuition predates the Buddha, and Nanananda cites them as a sidelight to the depth of the argument (source sidebars)."

### Pass 6 — Refutations — the counter-arguments answered
*The counter-arguments he answers, in his words: commentators, annihilationism, heretics, first-cause.*

**S01 · The nibbana etymology refuted (section 4 -> Q1)**
> "Common: "Vanasankhataya tanhaya nikkhantatta" — Nibbana is so called because it is an exit from craving which is a form of weaving."
> "To take the element vana in the word to mean a form of weaving is as good as taking nama in nama-rupa as some kind of bending."

**S01 · Buddhaghosa's heretic refuted (section 4 -> Q1)**
> "Visuddhimagga argument: if Nibbana is extinguishing of lust, animals also extinguish their fires of lust through enjoyment."
> "This argument ignores the deeper sense of the word extinction, as it is found in the Dhamma."

**S01 · The commentarial contradiction (section 4 -> Q1)**
> "On one hand: Nibbana as exit from craving (weaving etymology)."
> "it is on seeing Nibbana that craving is destroyed."

**S01 · Annihilationism - Vacchagotta's accusation (section 1) (unrouted->Q1)**
> "Accused of teaching the annihilation, destruction and non-existence of a being that is existent (Sato sattassa ucchedam vinasam vibhavam panapeti). Answered with the fire simile: not preserved, not destroyed - gone out, and the question of which direction it had gone absurd. Full quotes filed in section 1 (Vacchagotta and the tetralemma)."

**S01 · The commentarial agamma periphrasis (section 1)**
> "With an undue grammatical emphasis they would say that it is on coming to that Nibbana that lust and other defilements are abandoned: Nibbanam agamma ragadayo khinati ekameva nibbanam ragakkhayo dosakkhayo mohakkhayo ti vuccati."
> "If they are synonyms, there is no need to make any discrimination with regard to some of them, by insisting on a periphrastic usage like agamma."

**S01 · Khayamattam na nibbanam (section 1)**
> "But the commentator says that destruction alone is not Nibbāna: Khayamattaṃ na nibbānaṃ."
> "The excavation's verdict on this line: a commentarial invention, not a sutta position - the suttas use tanhakkhayo AS nibbana (section 1, Tanhakkhayo IS nibbana)."

> "Monks, I will teach you dependent arising and things that are dependently arisen."
> "Whether there be an arising of the Tathagatas or whether there be no such arising, that elementary nature, that orderliness of the Dhamma, that norm of the Dhamma, the relatedness of this to that does stand as it is."

**S02 · The commentary ignores the two-link sufficiency**
> "The Buddha follows the two-link example with "this is the paṭicca samuppāda" — showing the underlying principle could be understood with just two links. But the commentary's definition takes the dependence in a collective sense (plural "imesam")."

**S02 · Every link is dependently arisen — including ignorance**
> "The Buddha applies the qualifications (anicca, sankhata, paṭicca samuppanna, khayadhamma, vayadhamma, viragadhamma, nirodhadhamma) to each of the twelve links, "even ignorance." **S02 · Birth is born? Death passes away?**
> "Birth itself is an arising. But here we can't help saying that birth 'arises'. It is like saying that birth is born. How can birth get born? Similarly death is a passing away. But here we have to say that death itself 'passes away'. How can death pass away?"

**S02 · Viraga in context = "fading away"**
> "In such contexts as avijjāvirāgā and pītiyā ca virāgā one has to render it by words like 'fading away'. So that avijjāvirāga could be rendered as: 'by the fading away of ignorance', and pītiyā virāgā would mean 'by the fading away of joy'."

**S03 · The abyss = painful bodily feelings**
> "Monks, abyss is a synonym for painful bodily feelings."

**S03 · Salatana = the abyss; craving = the thirst to quell it**
> "The thirst of craving arises in beings in various forms of existence because of painful feeling. The uninstructed worldling, on being touched by painful feeling, delights in sense pleasures, because he knows no way out of painful feeling other than the sense pleasures. **S02 · How a whirlpool is formed (the full simile)**
> "Suppose a river is flowing downward. To flow downward is in the nature of a river. But a certain current of water thinks: 'I can and must move upstream.' And so it pushes on against the main stream. But at a certain point its progress is checked by the main stream and is thrust aside, only to come round and make a fresh attempt, again and again. All these obstinate and unsuccessful attempts gradually lead to a whirling round. As time goes on, the run-away current understands, as it were, that it cannot move forward. But it does not give up. It finds an alternative aim in moving towards the bottom. So it spirals downward, funnel-like, digging deeper and deeper towards the bottom, until an abyss is formed. Here then we have a whirlpool. While all this is going on, there is a crying need to fill up the chasm, and the whirlpool develops the necessary force of attraction to cater to it. It attracts and grasps everything that comes within its reach and sends it whirling down, funnel like, into the chasm."
