Source: Nibbana-30.pdf (BCBS/Dharma Seed, free). 8516 words.

## SECTION 1: WHAT NIBBANA IS

### Perfect Nibbana without clinging (the aim of the holy life)
"Friend it is for the sake of perfect Nibbāna without clinging that the
holy life is lived under the Fortunate One." (Anupādā parinibbāna.)
Analayo parallel: Ñāṇamoli (1995: 242); MĀ 9; SHT VI 1329.

### The inward peace = parinibbāna
"The inward peace, mentioned in the above context, is nothing other
than the clinging-free perfect extinction, anupādā parinibbāna."
"That peaceful one, leaning on none, would hanker no more for
existence."
Analayo parallel: Bodhi (2017: 301) (Māgandiya).

### The sphere the Noble Ones enter
"The sphere that the Noble Ones enter on and abide in is none other
than the sphere alluded to in the famous Sutta on Nibbāna in the
Udāna, beginning with atthi, bhikkhave, tad āyatanaṃ... the very
cessation of the six sense-spheres."
"The three terms appatiṭṭhaṃ, appavattaṃ and anārammaṇaṃ, the
'unestablished', the 'non continuing' and the 'objectless', which we
identified as allusions to the three deliverances."

### Non-identification as the watchword
"Non-identification is the watchword for clinging-free parinibbāna."
"Atammayatā or non-identification is the path to Nibbāna."

## SECTION 2: HOW THE MECHANISM WORKS

### The thirty-six pathways of beings (Saḷāyatanavibhaṅga)
"Chattiṃsa sattapadā, literally 'thirty-six steps of beings'":
six happiness based on household life; six happiness based on
renunciation; six unhappiness household; six unhappiness renunciation;
six equanimity household; six equanimity renunciation — each "six" =
form, sound, smell, taste, tangible, idea.
Analayo parallel: Ñāṇamoli (1995: 1067–1071); MĀ 163.

### The four definitions (paradigm: form)
Household happiness: "When one regards as an acquisition an
acquisition of forms... desirable, charming, agreeable, delightful,
connected with worldly gains, or when one recalls what was formerly
acquired that has passed, ceased and changed, happiness arises."
Renunciation happiness: "When by knowing the impermanence, change,
fading away and cessation of forms one sees as it actually is with
right wisdom... happiness arises. Such happiness... is called
happiness based on renunciation."
Household unhappiness: non-acquisition and lost recollection.
Renunciation unhappiness: "One arouses a longing for the supreme
deliverances thus: 'When shall I enter upon and abide in that sphere
that the Noble Ones now enter upon and abide in?' In one who arouses
such a longing... unhappiness arises conditioned by that longing."
Household equanimity: "Equanimity arises in a foolish infatuated
worldling... such equanimity as this does not transcend form."
Renunciation equanimity: "Equanimity arises... Such equanimity as this
transcends form."

### Longing for nibbana is allowed
"The word pihā (Sanskrit spṛhā, 'longing', 'desire')... shows that
there need not be any hesitation in using words implying desire in
connection with Nibbāna. It is true that such a desire or longing for
Nibbāna makes one unhappy. But that unhappiness is preferable to the
unhappiness based on the household life. That is why it is upgraded
here as unhappiness based on renunciation."

### The maxim: depending on this, abandon this
"Tatra idaṃ nissāya idam pajahatha — 'therein, depending on this,
abandon this'."
"This maxim has some affinity to the paṭicca samuppāda formula 'this
being, this arises'. In fact, this is a practical application of the
same formula. In the context of the path of practice, the dependence
on one thing is for the purpose of abandoning another."

### The four stages
Stage 1: "By depending on and relying on the six kinds of happiness
based on renunciation, abandon and transcend the six kinds of
happiness based on the household life." (Same for unhappiness and
equanimity — household states all left behind.)
Stage 2: within renunciation — unhappiness abandoned by happiness,
happiness abandoned by equanimity. "Happiness is subtler and more
excellent than unhappiness, and equanimity is subtler and more
excellent than happiness, since it is nearer to wisdom."
Stage 3: "There is, monks, an equanimity that is diversified, based on
diversity, and there is an equanimity that is unified, based on
unity." (Diversity = five external senses; unity = four immaterial
spheres.) "The equanimity based on diversity is abandoned and
transcended by the equanimity that is unified."
Stage 4: "By depending and relying on non-identification (atammayatā)
abandon and transcend equanimity that is unified, based on unity."

### Atammayatā explained
"The word tammayo, literally 'of thatness', could be explained with
reference to such usages as suvaṇṇamaya and rajatamaya, 'golden' and
'silver'."
"If, for instance, one who has attained the infinity of space as a
meditative experience identifies himself with it, with the conceit
eso 'ham asmi, 'this am I', there is that tammayatā coming in. It is a
subtle grasping, or in other words a me-thinking, maññanā — imagining
oneself to be one with that experience."
"The subtle conceit 'am', asmi, is that trace of grasping with which
one tries to sit pretty on that which is impermanent and changing. It
is the most fundamental assertion of existence."

### The good man's non-identification (Sappurisa)
"Non-identification even with the attainment of the sphere of
neither-perception-nor-non-perception has been declared by the
Fortunate One in such terms as: 'In whatever way they imagine, thereby
it turns otherwise.' So he takes into account that very
non-identification and neither exalts himself nor disparages others."
"A noble disciple does not look upon his jhānic attainments in the same
way as an ordinary meditator."
Analayo parallel: Ñāṇamoli (1995: 912); MĀ 85.

### The peg and the fine nail (Vitakkasaṇṭhāna)
"Just as, monks, a skilled carpenter or his apprentice might knock
out, draw out and remove a coarse peg by means of a fine one, even so,
when a monk finds that, due to some sign... there arise in him evil
unskilful thoughts connected with desire, with hate and with delusion,
that monk should attend to some other sign in its stead, one that has
to do with the skilful."
"When, for instance, a carpenter... finds that he is driving a blunt
nail, he extracts it with the help of a sharper one."
Analayo parallel: Ñāṇamoli (1995: 211); MĀ 101.

### Kusala/akusala are relative
"'Skilful' presupposes 'unskilful' and gets a value in relation to the
latter. It has no absolute value. We make use of the skilful in order
to push away the unskilful. That done, there is no further involvement
with it, as one's last resort is atammayatā."

### Crossing the flood (Oghataraṇa — the first SN discourse)
"How did you, Sir, cross the flood?" — "Without tarrying, friend, and
without hurrying, did I cross the flood."
"When I, friend, tarried, I found myself sinking; when I, friend,
hurried, I got swept away."
"If a person caught up in a water current tries to stay still, he will
sink. If he simply struggles to escape, he will get swept away. So
like a good swimmer, he has to avoid both extremes... He has to strive
— not struggle."
Analayo parallel: Bodhi (2000: 89); SĀ 1267.

### The vortex and the four perversions (recap)
"It is in the nature of beings in saṃsāra to get drifted by the
current of preparations, saṇkhārā, owing to ignorance, avijjā, and go
on revolving between consciousness, viññāṇa, and name-and-form,
nāmarūpa."
"This ignorance in the form of the four pervert perceptions — the
perception of permanence in the impermanent, pleasure in the painful,
beauty in the repulsive, self in the not-self — gives rise to the
run-away current of water which keeps running round and round."
"If we throw even a small leaf to a spot where a vortex is, it also
keeps revolving."

### Māgandiya's dialogue (purity not by views)
"Not by views, nor by learning, nor by knowledge, nor yet by virtue
and holy vows, they say, can purity come, neither can it come by
without views, learning and knowledge, without virtue and holy vows,
letting go of them all and grasping not one, that peaceful one,
leaning on none, would hanker no more for existence."
Māgandiya's charge: "Well then bewilderment itself, I think, is this
Dhamma, for there are some who claim purity by views."
Analayo parallel: Bodhi (2017: 301).

### The relay of chariots (Rathavinīta)
Seven purifications, each "for the sake of" the next: virtue → mind →
view → overcoming doubt → knowledge of path/not-path → knowledge of
the way → knowledge and vision → "perfect Nibbāna without clinging."
King Pasendi's seven relay chariots run from Sāvatthī to Sāketa,
chariot to chariot; when friends in Sāketa ask whether he came by this
chariot, "he cannot reply in the affirmative" — he has to relate the
whole story of passing from chariot to chariot.
"The key word in this grand finale is yāvad eva. Simply rendered it
means 'just for', that is, the sufficing condition for something else...
a watchword upholding the twin principles of pragmatism and
relativity."
"Here is a series of states, in which one state is to be made use of
for reaching another... but none of which is to be grasped per se."
Analayo parallel: Ñāṇamoli (1995: 242–244); MĀ 9; JCBS 2005, "The
Seven Stages of Purification in Comparative Perspective".

### The two contraries (Puṇṇa Mantāṇiputta's proof)
"If the Fortunate One had designated purification of virtue as perfect
Nibbāna without clinging, he would have designated what is still
accompanied by clinging as perfect Nibbāna without clinging."
"And if perfect Nibbāna without clinging were to be attained without
these states, then even an ordinary worldling would have attained
perfect Nibbāna without clinging, for an ordinary worldling is without
these states."

## SECTION 3: WHAT KEEPS THE MECHANISM RUNNING

### The duality held in fetters and grasping
"These fetters are called saṃyojanāni. A binding or a fetter implies
'two', as when two bulls are tied together. The term upādāna... implies
a holding on to something. There, too, the notion of a duality comes
in — one who holds and the thing held."

### Identification with attainments
(The infinity-of-space example, quoted in full in Section 2: a subtle
me-thinking attaches even to formless attainments.)

## SECTION 4: WHAT THE MECHANISM BUILDS

### The world's viscosity (craving)
"'Crossed the world's viscosity — craving.'" (The deity's verse on
seeing the quenched brahmin.)
"Where there is grasping, there is existence."

## SECTION 5: HOW TO SEE THROUGH IT

### The attention/inattention technique
"In order to eliminate one thing by inattention, attention to some
other thing is recommended. For the purpose of inattention to
something gross, attention to something subtle is taken up. But that is
not the end of it. Even that is expelled with the help of something
subtler."
"Here we have a wonderful technique, based on the twin principles of
pragmatism and relativity."

### The graded reflection (impermanence → renunciation states)
The same reflection on impermanence gives rise to happiness,
unhappiness or equanimity "according to the attitude taken up."

### The path taken like a razor
"The constituents of the path have to be taken up as one takes up a
razor for sharpening, ready to let go. Once the purpose is served,
they have to be given up."

### Middle way as systematic gradual effort
"By means of a mindful and systematic gradual effort, work out his
freedom... he has to strive — not struggle."
"Freedom from it can be won only by a subtle form of striving...
Avoiding the two extremes of stagnation and struggling, one has to
cross the flood going the middle way."

## SECTION 6: WHAT REMAINS

### The assetless Nibbana (nirupadhi)
"This is the distinction between what is called upadhi, or saṃsāric
asset, and nirupadhi, or the assetless Nibbāna."
"One grasps neither purification of virtue, nor purification of the
mind... not even purification by knowledge and vision. Leaving them
all behind and reaching the subtlest of them all, there comes the final
'let go' to attain that perfect extinction without clinging,
anupādā parinibbāna. This is the subtlest truth in this Dhamma."

### Peaceful, leaning on none
"Letting go of them all and grasping not one, that peaceful one,
leaning on none, would hanker no more for existence."

## REFUTATIONS

### The commentary on Māgandiya (wrong/right view reading)
"The commentarial explanation of these two verses seems to go off at a
tangent. It says that the negatives... refer to wrong views, wrong
learning... and that the third and fourth lines refer to right view...
This interpretation misses the subtle point at issue in this dialogue.
If it is as simple as that, there is no ground for Māgandiya's
accusation."
"Here is something more radical concerning sammā diṭṭhi itself.
According to this enlightened approach, views etc. cannot totally be
dispensed with, nor are they to be grasped."

### The variant reading na suddhim āha
"In both these verses, we followed the reading visuddhi, whereas some
editions accept the reading na suddhim āha, where the negative seems
superfluous. Visuddhi seems more meaningful here."

### Oneness as the goal (other religions)
"In other religious systems the question of reality is resolved by
having recourse to unity. Oneness is supposed to be the ultimate goal...
But to grasp the two as one, in some form of oneness, is not the way
out. Instead we have here, as the final solution, atammayatā or
non-identification."

### The two extremes (raft and snake)
"To carry the raft on one's shoulder after crossing is one extreme. To
take the water snake by its tail is the other extreme. The middle path
lies between these two extremes." (Alagaddūpama)

## PASS 3: HOLES AND CONNECTIONS

- **Hole:** The technique of "attention to subtler until subtler is
  abandoned" ends at atammayatā, but HOW non-identification is
  practiced in the last step is not shown — it's a negative
  (don't-identify), the positive content being the whole path before.
- **Hole:** The thirty-six pathways are enumerated for form only; the
  other five sense-objects follow "by paradigm" — acceptable
  compression, noted.
- **Connection to Sermon 28:** Pragmatism/relativity frame and razor
  simile both imported; gratification/danger/escape becomes
  the four-stage abandon-by-higher.
- **Connection to Sermon 29:** Sakkapañha's graded criteria are the
  first statement of what Saḷāyatanavibhaṅga elaborates in full — the
  two sermons are one teaching unit.
- **Connection to Sermon 27:** Chess/game logic inverted — now stages
  of purification are the pieces, none to be grasped (the peaceful
  draw completed).
- **Connection to Sermon 20:** The tetralemma's practical counterpart:
  Māgandiya's "neither by views nor without views" is the same
  neither-nor structure used for practice.
- **Connection to VBT pack:** The peg-and-nail technique IS the VBT
  substitution practice (swap the unskilful sign for a skilful one,
  then drop both); the flood simile maps to the effort-balance note.
