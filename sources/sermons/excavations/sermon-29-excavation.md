# SERMON 29 — EXCAVATION

Source: Nibbana-29.pdf (BCBS/Dharma Seed, free). 7353 words.

---

## SECTION 1: WHAT NIBBANA IS

### The signless gives up the conceit latency (Rāhulasutta)
"Animittañca bhāvehi, mānānusayamujjaha — 'Develop the signless, too,
and give up the latency to conceit!'"
"When signs fade away through the perception of impermanence, latencies
to conceit also lose their hold on the mind."
Analayo parallel: Bodhi (2017: 212).

### Nibbana by giving up craving (Saṃyojana)
"To delight enfettered is the world, thought is its trailing along. It
is that craving, by giving up which, they say, Nibbāna is attained."
Analayo parallel: Bodhi (2000: 219); SĀ 1010.

### Noble silence
"Avitakkasamādhi... is none other than the arahattaphalasamādhi, which
is known as ariyo tuṇhībhāvo, 'noble silence'... because in it the
question of thoughts is fully resolved."

## SECTION 2: HOW THE MECHANISM WORKS

### External element impermanence → this body (MahāHatthipadopama)
"Even of this external earth element, Friends, great as it is, an
impermanence will become manifest... what to say of this ephemeral
body clung to by craving as 'I' or 'mine' or 'am'? On the other hand
there is no justification for such a clinging."
"It is possible to dispel the latencies to conceit leading to 'I'-ing
and 'mine'-ning by penetrating into the impermanence of this puny
internal earth element through the broader perspective of the
impermanence of the vast external earth element."
Analayo parallel: Ñāṇamoli (1995: 279); MĀ 30.

### The blurred water-colour painting
"When dispassion sets in, signs fade away, like in a blurred
water-colour painting. For a meditator, developing the perception of
impermanence, the world might appear like an indistinct water-painting,
with blotches of colour. When the figures in the painting cannot be
distinguished according to colour and shape, there is less room for the
perception of diversity, nānattasaññā. Thereby latencies to conceit...
are also attenuated."

### Two ways to stop the three unskilful thoughts (Khandhasaṃyutta)
"Monks, there are these three kinds of unskilful thoughts: sensual
thoughts, thoughts of ill-will, thoughts of harming. And where do these
cease without remainder? For one who dwells with the mind well attended
by the four foundations of mindfulness, or for one who develops the
signless concentration... it is of great fruit, of great benefit."
"The particle vā, 'or', shows that it is an alternative."
Analayo parallel: Bodhi (2000: 920); SĀ 272.

### Middle path = gradual path (pragmatic and relative)
"The course of practice leading to Nibbāna is in principle both
pragmatic and relative... Each stage has only a relative value, being
of significance in relation to the next stage."
"On an earlier occasion, we happened to mention the simile of the
relay of chariots in the Rathavinītasutta. Like stage coaches, the
chariots run relative to each other. It is an illustration of the
principle of relativity."

### The razor simile (sammasana vs parāmasana)
"If one grabs the razor roughly and moves it up and down the whetstone,
it would get blunt. But if one catches hold of the razor in a relaxed
nonchalant way, and mindfully sweeps the whetstone back and forth, it
would get sharpened."
"Whereas parāmasana means tenacious grasping, sammasana is a
particular way of holding lightly for some subtle purpose."

### The carpenter simile (vipassana/anupassanā)
"The carpenter... might hold up the piece of wood to his left eye, and
with his right eye closed might give it a critical glance... he is
himself playing the role of a critic, in order to find out his
shortcomings as a carpenter."
"In the two terms vipassanā and anupassanā we have a special way of
seeing that is penetrative as well as contemplative. It is... an
objective approach to understand the subjective in one's experience,
with a view to attaining perfection."
"Phrases like kāye kāyānupassī viharati and vedanāsu
vedanānupassī viharati seem to defy translation... That is what the
carpenter is doing. This meditative carpenter, too, has to direct a
critical glance at the preparations in order to find out their
shortcomings. He has to look upon them as alien, parato."
"If he starts off with the prejudice 'this is my body, what is wrong
with this?', there is little chance that he will see its
shortcomings."

### Seeing the aggregates as alien
"They penetrate into that which is subtle, like a horse's hair with an
arrow, who look upon the aggregates five, as something alien, not their
own." (MahāMoggallāna Thera)
"By seeing as alien all preparations, as causally arisen and fragile, I
have given up all influxes, become cool and extinguished." (Sakulā
Therī)
Analayo parallel: Norman (1969: 107); Norman (1989: 181).

### The planing sound (the three nots)
"Na etaṃ mama, na eso 'ham asmi, na me so attā. Na etaṃ mama... Na
etaṃ mama..."
"It is this sharp 'not', na, that cuts away the protruding
defilements."
"These phrases are not mentioned in the discourses for the purpose of
grasping them as some sort of dogmatic formula. They have a pragmatic
and relative value for the meditator in his planing to do away with
those rough edges."

### Ussada — the swellings (Tuvaṭaka)
"Cravings, conceits and views are such swellings or protuberances
which show up from this heap of preparations. These swellings have to
be planed down."
"As in mid-ocean no waves arise, and it is all steady and motionless,
so unmoved and steady let the monk be, let him not form any swelling
anywhere."
"By contrast, insinuates that the worldling's mind is much nearer the
seashore, where ripples turn into waves and furious breakers."
Analayo parallel: Bodhi (2017: 313).

### The graded path (Sakkapañha)
"Desire, O King of Gods, has thinking as its cause... When thinking is
there, desire comes to be; when thinking is not there, desire does not
come to be!"
"Reckonings born of prolific perception (papañcasaññāsaṅkhā), O King
of Gods, is the cause, the arising and the origin of thinking."
"Even happiness, O King of Gods, I speak of as being of two kinds: one
to be pursued, the other not to be pursued..." (Same for unhappiness
and equanimity.)
"Whatever happiness... 'while pursuing this happiness unskilful
thoughts decline and skilful thoughts grow' — that kind of happiness
should be pursued... whatever is not accompanied by thinking and
pondering is the more excellent."
"It is a monk who has thus set out, O King of Gods, who is treading
the path of practice conducive to the cessation of reckonings born of
proliferate perceptions."
Analayo parallel: Walshe (1987: 329); DĀ 14.

### The circularity (no contradiction with Madhupiṇḍika)
"The raw material for thinking is the set of reckonings or worldly
concepts born of prolific perception. Proliferation only aggravates
the situation by further ramification of concepts."
"As in the case of a fermenting agent, used in the preparation of
liquor, there is a circularity in this proliferation, which makes the
confusion in saṃsāra worst confounded."
"It is a forked path, where one has to proceed always keeping to the
right, choosing the skilful in preference to the unskilful, and
intuitive in preference to the ratiocinative."

### Avitakkasamādhi analyzed (Subhūti verse)
"The purpose of samatha is to temporarily suppress thought,
vikkhambhanappahāna. It is the task of insight to dig into the roots of
thinking and clear up the mess... abandonment by eradication,
samucchedappahāna."
"The second line could now be paraphrased as yassa ajjhattaṃ
suvikappitā vitakkā asesā vidhūpitā, 'in whom thoughts, well
constructed within, have been smoked out without residue'."
(Vikappita = built up, from root kḷp; vidhūpita = smoked out.)
"Arūpasaññī means devoid of rūpasaññā, a + rūpasaññī... 'having gone
beyond attachment (to thoughts) and being free from the perception of
form'."
"Catuyogā means the four yokes, namely those of sensuality,
existence, views and ignorance."
Analayo parallel: Ireland (1990: 97) (Subhūti, Udāna Jaccandhavagga).

### Māra's army is our thoughts (Padhāna)
"Sense desires are your first battalion, and boredom is reckoned the
second, hunger and thirst comes as the third, and craving is called
the fourth."
"The word kāmā in this context does not refer to pleasurable objects as
such... Rather, it refers to thoughts about pleasurable objects."
"They are not the pleasures, those charming things in the world, lustful
thought is the pleasure for a man... but it is the desire for them,
that the wise discipline." (SN)
"Hunger and thirst... it is thoughts about them, such as kiṃ su
asissāmi, kuvaṃ vā asissaṃ, 'what shall I eat, where shall I eat'."
"What shall I eat, where shall I eat? Badly have I slept, where shall
I sleep? Such miserable thoughts let the trainee discipline."
Analayo parallel: Bodhi (2017: 227, 319); Bodhi (2000: 111), SĀ 1286.

### Fumigating the hordes of Māra (Bodhivagga)
"When phenomena manifest themselves, to the resolutely meditating
Brahmin, he stands fumigating the hordes of Māra, like the sun
irradiating the firmament."
"It is the reflection on the law of dependent arising in direct and
reverse order that dispels the denizens of darkness of ignorance,
namely thoughts."
"A penetrative reflection into arising and ceasing in a flash deprives
thoughts of their evocative power and renders them nugatory. This is
the smoking out of the army of Māra, the thoughts."
Analayo parallel: Ireland (1990: 13).

### Other avitakkasamādhi witnesses
"The Fully Awakened One's disciple, on attaining to thoughtless
musing, is at once endowed with a silence that is of the ennobling
type." (Sāriputta Thera)
"Seated am I, at the root of a tree, a winner to the thoughtless
state." (Vimalā Therī)
"In body relaxed, in mind well freed, concocting not, mindful,
abode-less, well knowing the Norm, he muses thoughtless." (Dhītaros,
Māra-saṃyutta)
"At that time the Fortunate One was seated reflecting on his
abandonment of reckonings born of prolific perceptions." (Udāna
Cūlavagga)
Analayo parallel: Norman (1969: 92); Norman (1989: 92); Bodhi
(2000: 219).

## SECTION 3: WHAT KEEPS THE MECHANISM RUNNING

### The habit of taking signs
"It is because of our saṃsāric habit of taking in signs that thoughts
and proliferations arise in us."
"It is in the nature of some things that, though they have to be given
up, they cannot be fully dispensed with." (Hence the middle/gradual
path.)

### Delight as the world-fetter
"To delight enfettered is the world, thought is its trailing along."

---

## SECTION 4: WHAT THE MECHANISM BUILDS

### Latencies from past performances
"Where are the actors and actresses who played their part in the early
acts of our drama of life?... Though they are no more, so long as
latencies to perception, amassed through them, persist in us, there is
nothing to prevent us from indulging in thoughts and proliferations."

### The concept-flush cycle
"In the course of thinking proliferation takes over, with the result
that those concepts throw up a flush that tends to overwhelm and
obsess the one who initiated the whole process."

## SECTION 5: HOW TO SEE THROUGH IT

### Project cosmic impermanence inward
"Instead of pervertly exploiting the canonical discourses describing
the impermanence of the world with wrong reflection in order to indulge
in worldly speculation, we should arouse radical reflection and
project them into our own internal world in order to understand the
vanity of this drama of life."
"When we reflect on the impermanence of the entire world, we get
dispassionate about the heap of preparations in our drama of life,
reckoned in terms of objects, events and persons."

### The signless concentration recommended
Pali: "Yāvañcidaṃ, bhikkhave, alam eva animitto samādhi bhāvetuṃ."
English: "So much so that this is reason enough for one to develop the
signless concentration."

### The planing practice
"This meditative carpenter has to plane down the rugged surfaces with
insightful contemplation, until those cravings, conceits and views
that show up are smoothened out and only a pure heap of preparations,
suddhasaṅkhārapuñjo, remains."
"Even a carpenter, while planing a piece of wood, has to adjust his
instrument from time to time... He might even pick up another plane
when the surface gets smoother. The planing down of thoughts and
proliferations... is a gradual process."

### The graded criteria (practical)
Paraphrase of the Sakkapañha criteria: pursue what grows skilful;
avoid what grows unskilful; prefer the happiness and equanimity not
accompanied by thinking and pondering.

## SECTION 6: WHAT REMAINS

### The pure heap of preparations
"Though this is a pure heap of preparations, suddhasaṅkhārapuñjo, as
Venerable Vajirā calls it, there are three protuberances, three rough
edges to be planed down in the form of craving, conceit and views."
(The same "only a pure heap of preparations, suddhasaṅkhārapuñjo,
remains" — the goal image — is quoted in the planing practice, Section
5.)

### The mid-ocean mind
"As in mid-ocean no waves arise, and it is all steady and motionless."

### The world as blurred painting, diversity gone
"Avitakkasamādhi is a term used to denote that state of complete
emancipation of the mind by making all signs fade away, so that the
whole world appears like a blotched water-colour painting, thus
freeing the mind from the perception of diversity, without even
resorting to a perception of unity."

## REFUTATIONS

### Cosmic impermanence used for speculation
"Instead of pervertly exploiting the canonical discourses describing
the impermanence of the world with wrong reflection in order to indulge
in worldly speculation..."

### The commentary's samatha reading of avitakkasamādhi
"According to the commentary, avitakkasamādhi stands for all levels of
concentration... from the second jhāna upwards. This is an
interpretation purely from the samatha or tranquillity standpoint...
But... avitakkasamādhi is not a term that is relevant merely to the
samatha aspect of Buddhist meditation. It is not simply a term that
connotes all jhānas devoid of thought, vitakka. It is a term directly
relevant to insight, vipassanā."

### The commentary's readings of the Subhūti verse
"Suvikappitā as an equivalent of susamucchinnā, 'fully eradicated'.
But it is more likely that the word vikappita basically signifies some
form of 'building up'..."
"The commentary adopts a queer mode of exegesis... trying to establish
the term arūpa as an epithet for Nibbāna rather arbitrarily."
"The attachment meant in this context is attachment to thoughts,
vitakka." (Not "attachment to defilements such as lust".)

### The commentary's vacī kharā = harsh speech
"The commentary takes the word vacī kharā to mean 'harsh speech'. There
is some imbalance between the two terms bhavāsavā and vacī kharā...
Harsh speech could ill afford to get coupled with influxes of
existence... It probably means the sediments or dregs (Sanskrit kṣāra)
of speech, namely the worldly concepts and designations."

### The apparent Madhupiṇḍika/Sakkapañha contradiction
"Apparently there is a contradiction... But actually there is no
contradiction, since the raw material for thinking is the set of
reckonings... born of prolific perception."
