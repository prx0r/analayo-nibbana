# SERMON 21 — EXCAVATION

Source: Nibbana-21.pdf (BCBS/Dharma Seed, free). 8436 words.

---

## SECTION 1: WHAT NIBBANA IS

### Tanhakkhayo hi nibbanam (Rādha, Sand castles)
"For, the destruction of craving, Rādha, is Nibbāna."
"Suppose some little boys and girls are playing with sand castles. So
long as their lust, desire, love, thirst, passion and craving for those
things have not gone away, they remain fond of them... But when they
have outgrown that craving, they scatter them with their hands and feet,
demolish them, dismantle them and render them unplayable."
Analayo parallel: SĀ 122.

### The Anurādha catechism (Tathagata not found)
"Is form the Tathagata? No. Is the Tathagata in form? No. Is the
Tathagata apart from form? No... Do you regard the Tathagata as one
who is without form? No."
"When the Tathāgata is not apprehended by you as real and actual here
in this very life, is it fitting for you to declare that he describes
him apart from these four propositions?"
Analayo parallel: SĀ 106, SĀ 101.

### The Tathagata freed from reckoning
"That form by which one designating the Tathagata might designate him,
that has been abandoned by him, cut off at the root, made like an
uprooted palm tree. The Tathagata is free from reckoning in terms of
form, deep, immeasurable and hard to fathom, like the great ocean."

### The satta redefinition (Radha)
"Rādha, that desire, that lust, that delight, that craving in form...
with which one is attached and thoroughly attached to it, therefore is
one called a 'being'."
Analayo parallel: SĀ 122.

## SECTION 2: HOW THE MECHANISM WORKS

### The vortex and the ocean
"If a vortex in the ocean comes to cease, can one ask where the vortex
has gone? It will be like asking where the extinguished fire has gone."
"The vortex has now become the great ocean itself... In reality, the
vortex is merely a certain pervert state of the ocean itself."

### The four perversions
"Perceiving permanence in the impermanent. Perceiving pleasure in the
painful. Perceiving beauty in the foul. Perceiving a self in the
not-self."
"The samsaric individual tries to forge ahead in existence, misled by
these four pervert views."

### Tathagata = return to suchness
"Tathā, 'even so', is the correlative of yathā. At whatever moment it
becomes possible to say 'as is the ocean, so is the vortex now', then
it is the state of tathāgata."
"Tathata is a term for the law of dependent arising. Tathāgata could
be explained as a return to that 'thusness', by comprehending it fully."

### The Pahārāda ocean refutation
"Just as however many rivers may flow into the great ocean, no decrease
or increase is apparent — even so, although many monks attain parinibbana,
no decrease or increase is apparent in the Nibbana element."
"It is a ridiculous idea that arahants find some place of refuge which
never gets overcrowded."

## SECTION 3: WHAT KEEPS THE MECHANISM RUNNING

### The internal/external vortex (Hatthipadopama)
"The four elements are to be found both internally and externally. Both
are simply earth element. 'This is not mine, this I am not, this is
not my self.'"
"This so-called individual is in fact a vortex, formed out of the same
kind of primary elements that obtain outside of it."

### Ignorance as a coil
"Ignorance is a sort of going round, in a winding pattern, as in the
case of a coil. Each round seems so different from the previous one,
a peculiar novelty arising out of the forgetting trait."

### The four perversions as the engine
"The four pervert views are what impel the vortex. Without them, the
current would simply join the main stream."

## SECTION 4: WHAT THE MECHANISM BUILDS

### The Vajira-Mara challenge
"What do you mean by a 'being', Mara? Isn't it a bigoted view? This is
purely a heap of preparations, mind you. No being is to be found here
at all."
Analayo parallel: SĀ 1202 (Selā instead of Vajirā).

### The Purabhedasutta
"Not for him are sons and cattle, he has no field or site to build.
In him there is not to be found anything that is grasped or given up."
"The words attam and nirattam are suggestive of the dichotomy from
which the arahant is free."

### The sand castles (Radha)
"The boys scatter them with their hands and feet, demolish them,
dismantle them and render them unplayable. Even so, Rādha, you all
scatter form, feeling, perception, preparations, consciousness.
Practise for the destruction of craving."

## SECTION 5: HOW TO SEE THROUGH IT

### The sand castle practice
"Scatter form, demolish it, shatter it, put it out of play. Practise
for the destruction of craving."

### The internal/external reflection
"The four elements are to be found both internally and externally.
Both are simply earth element. This is not mine, this I am not, this
is not my self."

## SECTION 6: WHAT REMAINS

### The vortex ceases
"When the vortex has ceased, there is actually nothing to identify
with, for purposes of designation. The most one can say is to refer
to it as the place where a vortex has ceased."

### The Dvayatanupassana inversion
"What others may call bliss, that the ariyans make known as pain.
What others may call pain, that the ariyans have known to be bliss."

---

## REFUTATIONS

### The Pahārāda passage used for eternalism
"Quite a number of scholars draw upon this passage when they put forward
the view that arahants, after their death, find some place of refuge
which never gets overcrowded. It is a ridiculous idea, utterly misconceived."

### The upalabbhati misreading
"Some prefer to render upalabbhati to mean the Tathagata does exist,
only that he cannot be traced. But the context makes it clear that
upalabbhati definitely means 'not to be found'."

## PASS 2 FINDS

- The sand castles simile is the most practical instruction in the
  series — scatter, demolish, dismantle, render unplayable. That's the
  practice.
- The Anurādha catechism is the most rigorous philosophical move —
  the Tathāgata is not found in any of four modes, even here and now.
- The Vajira-Mara challenge ("no being is to be found here at all") is
  the most defiant statement on not-self in the series.
- The satta redefinition (attached = being) turns the concept itself
  inside out.
- The Pahārāda refutation demolishes the eternalist reading of the
  ocean simile.

## PASS 3: HOLES AND CONNECTIONS

- **Hole:** The sand castle practice is given but the method for
  "scattering the aggregates" is only implied. The satipatthana
  four modes (Sermon 4) is the method.
- **Connection to Sermon 20:** The fire catechism is continued. The
  palm stump and the ocean simile are the same insight from the
  Anurādha angle.
- **Connection to Sermon 18:** The lotus practice is the same as the
  sand castle — things used without grasping.
- **Connection to VBT pack:** The sand castle practice IS the VBT
  concept-deconstruction practice. The four perversions map to the
  VBT practice of seeing the sign as a sign.
