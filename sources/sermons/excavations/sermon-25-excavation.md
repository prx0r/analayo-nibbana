# SERMON 25 — EXCAVATION

Source: Nibbana-25.pdf (BCBS/Dharma Seed, free). 8631 words.

---

## SECTION 1: WHAT NIBBANA IS

### The Bahiya chain to the end of suffering
"And when to you, Bāhiya, there will be in the seen just the seen, in
the heard just the heard, in the sensed just the sensed, in the
cognized just the cognized, then, Bāhiya, you are not by it. And when
you are not by it, then you are not in it. And when you are not in it,
then you are neither here nor there nor in between. This, itself, is
the end of suffering."
"In other words, you would have realized voidness, suññatā."
Analayo parallel: Ireland (1990: 20f).

### The plane of the void (suññatābhūmi)
"The very absence of maññanā, or 'egoistic imagining', is to be
understood by suññatābhūmi, or 'the plane of the void'."
"This is the plane of the void... the perfect realization of the
corelessness or essencelessness of the seen, the heard, the sensed and
the cognized."

### The plane of the such (tādibhūmi)
"Thus, monks, the Tathāgata, being such in regard to all phenomena,
seen, heard, sensed and cognized, is such. Moreover than he who is
such there is none other higher or more excellent, I declare."
Analayo parallel: Bodhi (2012: 411).

## SECTION 2: HOW THE MECHANISM WORKS

### The five declarations as a staged tetralemma
1. "Whatever is seen, heard, sensed and cognized... that I know" —
   yet "the Tathāgata has not taken his stand upon it."
2. "If I were to say 'all that I do not know', it would be a falsehood
   in me."
3. "If I were to say 'I both know it and do not know it', that too
   would be a falsehood in me."
4. "If I were to say 'I neither know it, nor am ignorant of it', it
   would be a fault in me."
"The Tathāgata has no stance, not because he is ignorant, but due to
the very fact that he knows full well and has understood the nature of
the sum total of sensory data."
"So it seems, in this context too, we have the negation of the first
alternative, as is usual in the case of a tetralemma, only that the
negation is expressed here in a very peculiar way."

### The reading dispute (na jānāmī vs jānāmī)
"The commentary has followed the reading tam ahaṃ 'jānāmī'ti
vadeyyaṃ... But as we have pointed out earlier, this reading is not
meaningful. That is probably why the Chaṭṭhasaṅgīti-piṭaka edition has
followed the variant reading tam ahaṃ 'na jānāmī'ti vadeyyaṃ."

### The eel-wriggler rejected (Sañjaya Belaṭṭhiputta)
"This attitude of wriggling out had already assumed the status of a
philosophy in itself in Sañjaya Belaṭṭhiputta, a contemporary of the
Buddha... he would respond with such a series of negations like 'I do
not say it is, I do not say it is thus, I do not say it is otherwise,
nor do I say it is neither'... The attempt here is to evade the issue
by a sort of 'eel-wriggling'. That school of philosophy, which resorted
to such an evasive legerdemain, came to be known as
amarā-vikkhepa-vāda. The Buddha refuses to subscribe to such tactical
sophistry."

### The five planes (commentarial analysis)
"The commentary analyses the main theme of the discourse into five
planes... the plane of omniscience, sabbaññutabhūmi... the plane of
the influx-free one, khīṇāsavabhūmi... the third plane of truth,
saccabhūmi... the plane of the void, suññatābhūmi... tādibhūmi, the
'plane of the such'."
Analayo parallel: Anālayo 2014, The Dawn of Abhidharma, pp. 117ff.

### The dog on the plank (the full simile)
"When a dog, while crossing a stream, stops halfway on the plank and
starts wagging its tail and peeping curiously down, the reason is the
sight of its own image in the water. It imagines a dog there, a
'water-dog'. The dog thinks that there is something worthwhile seeing,
apart from the seen. It is unaware of the fact that it is seeing what
it sees because it is looking. It thinks that it is looking because
there is something out there to be seen. The moment it realizes that
it is seeing because it is looking, it will stop looking at its own
image in the water."
"The simile, of course, is not canonical, but of fable origin."
"Due to the very ignorance of the fact that it is seeing because it is
looking, the dog imagines another dog, there, in the water. What is
called maññanā is an imagining of that sort."
"Everything is dependently arisen, phassapaccayā, says the
Brahmajālasutta, 'dependent on contact'."

### The music simile
"One imagines a thing called 'music' and with the idea of listening to
the same music goes to the place where the music is going on. One is
not aware of the fact that at each step in that direction one is
hearing a different music."
"He will realize the extent of his delusion if he happens to put his
ear to the musical instrument. When he does so, he will hear not a
music, but a set of crude vibrations."
"There is no person hearing, but only a state of affairs dependent on
the ear and sound, a conditioned arising dependent on contact."
"Music is just a word. By taking seriously the concept behind that
word, one imagines a thing called 'music'."

### The four negations explained (diṭṭhā daṭṭhabbaṃ...)
"Diṭṭhā daṭṭhabbaṃ diṭṭhaṃ na maññati — 'does not imagine a sight
worthwhile seeing as apart from the seen', that there is nothing
substantial in the seen."
"Sutā sotabbaṃ sutaṃ na maññati — 'does not imagine a worthwhile
hearing apart from the heard'."
"Daṭṭhāraṃ na maññati — 'does not imagine a seer'. Here we have the
direct expression of voidness — the voidness of a self or anything
belonging to a self."
"There is no seer, apart from the phenomenon of seeing, according to
the Tathāgata... For the worldling, the bare act of seeing carries
with it a perception of 'one who sees'."
"The Tathāgata does not even imagine a thinker apart from thought."

### Two kinds of relationship: duality vs concatenation
"The worldling takes seriously the subject-object relationship in the
grammatical structure... Because there is something to be seen, there
is someone who sees... To understand the law of dependent arising is to
be free from this duality. It is the ability to see a concatenation of
conditions... an assemblage instead of a bifurcation."

### The high-speed engine
"We may compare this seeing of the cessation of the six sense-bases to
an exposure of the inner mechanism of a high-speed engine by removing
the bonnet."
"The totality of percepts are made up or 'prepared', saṅkhata...
If we take the six-fold sense-base as a high-speed machine, productive
of perceptions, the Buddha has revealed to us the workings of its
intricate machinery. Each and every part of this machine is unstable,
evanescent, impermanent."

### The magic show (Pheṇapiṇḍūpamasutta)
"Form is like a mass of foam, and feeling but an airy bubble,
perception is like a mirage, and formations a banana trunk,
consciousness is a magic show, a juggler's trick entire, all these
similes were made known by the kinsman of the sun."
"Suppose, monks, a magician or a magician's apprentice should hold a
magic show at the four cross-roads and a keen-sighted man should see
it, ponder over it and reflect on it radically... he would find it
empty, he would find it hollow, he would find it void of essence. What
essence, monks, could there be in a magic show?"
Analayo parallel: SĀ 265; Bodhi (2000: 952).

## SECTION 3: WHAT KEEPS THE MECHANISM RUNNING

### The dogmatic assertion
"Worldings in general are in the habit of asserting dogmatically 'I
know, I see, it is verily so', jānāmi passāmi tath' eva etaṃ, when
they have a special knowledge or understanding of something or other."
"The dictum idam eva saccaṃ, mogham aññaṃ — 'this alone is true, all
else is false'."

### Leaning taken as stability
"The worldling, on the other hand, thinks that to lean on or to rely on
something is the mark of stability."
"Now I am leaning on the wall, if someone does damage to the wall, I
would get shaken."

### Philosophical in-breeding (sayasaṃvuta)
"The term carries insinuations of philosophical in-breeding, which
often accounts for dogmatic adherence to views."
"The three expressions paccekasacca, sakaṃ sakaṃ sammutim and
sayasaṃvutesu convey more or less the same idea... narrow viewpoints to
which they are individually confined, or remain closeted in."
Analayo parallel: Anālayo 2016, Ekottarika-āgama Studies, pp. 222ff.

## SECTION 4: WHAT THE MECHANISM BUILDS

### The truth/falsehood dyad from logic (Cūḷaviyūha)
"There are no several and various truths, that are permanent in the
world, apart from perception. It is by manipulating logic in
speculative views, that they speak of two things called 'truth and
falsehood'."
Analayo parallel: Bodhi (2017: 308).

### Each school calls its own true (Mahāviyūha)
"They call their own system of thought perfect, and the other system
of thought inferior. Thus they quarrel and dispute."
"Sakaṃ sakaṃ sammutim — 'each his own viewpoint'."
Analayo parallel: Bodhi (2017: 310).

### The barb
"He declares that he has seen well in advance 'the barb on which
mankind is hooked impaled'. The barb is none other than the dogmatic
assertion, 'I know, I see, it is verily so'."

## SECTION 5: HOW TO SEE THROUGH IT

### Reflect radically on consciousness (yoniso upaparikkha)
"A keen-sighted man should see it, ponder over it and reflect on it
radically. Even as he sees it, ponders over it and reflects on it
radically, he would find it empty, he would find it hollow, he would
find it void of essence."
"Even so, monks, whatever consciousness... a monk sees it, ponders
over it and reflects on it radically... he finds it empty, he finds it
hollow, he finds it void of essence."

### The dog's liberation-moment
"The moment it realizes that it is seeing because it is looking, it
will stop looking at its own image in the water."
"Only a looking is there, nothing worth looking at. Only a seeing is
there, nothing to be seen. Apart from the bare act of hearing, there
is nothing to be heard."

### The Bāhiya training restated
"In the seen there will be just the seen, in the heard there will be
just the heard, in the sensed there will be just the sensed, in the
cognized there will be just the cognized."
"One has to stop at just the seen, without discursively imagining that
there is some-'thing' seen, some-'thing' substantial behind the seen."

## SECTION 6: WHAT REMAINS

### Tādī = atammayatā (not-made-of-that)
"In one sense, the term tādī stands for the understanding of the norm
called tathatā. The other implication is the abstinence from the
tendency towards identification or acquisition, meant by tammayatā.
This exemplary trait is called atammayatā."
"Tammayo means 'made of that', as in suvaṇṇamaya, 'made of gold',
and rajatamaya, 'made of silver'. To be free from this tammayatā, is
to be tādī, 'such', that is to say, not to be of that stuff."
"The attitude of not leaning on or grasping is meant by it."
"Generally, one associates firmness, immovability or stability with
holding on or leaning on. But here we have just the contrary. Not to
hold on to anything, is to be 'such'."

### The revolving swing (non-rigid resilience)
"We may compare it to a revolving swing in a children's playground...
The seats are hung in such a way that they also turn with the
revolving motion of the swing. Had they been rigidly fixed, one seated
there would fall off the seat when it goes up. It is that kind of
resilience that is characteristic of the quality of tāditā."
"Though the term is sometimes rendered by the word 'steadfast', it does
not stand for any rigidity. Instead, it carries implications of a
non-rigid resilience."

### The alms-food verse (adaptability)
"Suppose I got it, well and good, suppose I didn't get, that's fine
too, in both circumstances he is such, and comes back like one who
walks up to a tree."
Analayo parallel: Bodhi (2017: 278).

### The Mahāmaṅgalasutta line
"Phuṭṭhassa lokadhammehi, cittam yassa na kampati — 'whose mind
remains unshaken, when touched by worldly vicissitudes.'"
"This quality of being unshaken... is the result of not grasping...
It is to one who rests on or leans on something that there is
dislodgement or instability."
Analayo parallel: Bodhi (2017: 200).

### The couplet (grand finale)
"I know, I see, 'tis verily so, no such clinging for the Tathāgatas."
"Being such, he does not categorically label any of those views as true
or false. He penetrates into and analyses the psychological background
of all those dogmatic views and understands them as such."

## REFUTATIONS

### The commentary's shallow reading of diṭṭhaṃ na maññati
"According to it, what is meant is that the Tathāgata does not imagine
by way of cravings, conceits and views that he is seeing just what the
common people have seen. This is an oversimplification, a rather
shallow interpretation... The commentary, it seems, has gone at a
tangent, bypassing the deeper sense."

### The commentary's tāditā explanation
"The commentary explains it by the phrase tāditā nāma ekasadisatā...
'as he is in regard to gain etc., so is the Tathāgata in regard to
loss etc.'... But this explanation is rather misleading, as it ignores
a certain deep dimension of the meaning of the term tādī... However,
the commentator happens to quote from the Mahāniddesa another
explanation, which is more to the point... 'he is such in gain as well
as in loss...' That is the correct explanation."
Analayo parallel: Mp II 273.

### The commentary's sayasaṃvutesu reading
"According to the commentary, the term sayasaṃvuta refers to persons
who hold dogmatic views. But we interpreted it as a reference to such
views themselves." (Supported by the Aṭṭhakavagga verses.)

### The commentary's paraṃ daheyyaṃ
"Another lapse in the commentary... It takes the word paraṃ in the
sense of 'supreme', uttamaṃ katvā, whereas in this context it means
'the other'... 'That by which one dubs the other a fool, by that
itself one calls oneself an expert.'"
Analayo parallel: Bodhi (2017: 308).

### The Burmese absolutive substitution
"The Burmese Chaṭṭhasaṅgīti edition goes a step further in
substituting sutvā, mutvā and viññatvā rather arbitrarily... Probably
the editors thought that in this context the terms diṭṭha suta muta
and viññāta could not be interpreted as they are."

## PASS 2 FINDS

- The dog on the plank simile gets its fullest treatment here — the
  complete logic (seeing because looking, not looking because seen)
  plus the explicit "not canonical, of fable origin" admission. It is
  the sermon's central illustration of maññanā.
- The music simile is a second full illustration of dependent arising
  in sensory experience (each step = different music; ear to the
  instrument = crude vibrations).
- Sañjaya Belaṭṭhiputta and amarā-vikkhepa-vāda (eel-wriggling) —
  named teacher + school, the fourth declaration's target.
- The five-plane commentarial analysis with Anālayo 2014 reference —
  recorded as the structure of the discourse.
- The reading dispute (na jānāmī vs jānāmī) is a textual-criticism
  argument won on logic — the Buddha already said he knows.
- Four separate commentarial refutations this sermon (shallow
  diṭṭha reading, misleading tāditā, persons-not-views sayasaṃvuta,
  paraṃ=supreme) — the densest refutation count in the series so far.
- The Aṭṭhakavagga support verses (Cūḷaviyūha, Mahāviyūha, Pasūra)
  for sayasaṃvuta/paccekasacca — a whole inter-textual line Nāṇananda
  opens up.
- The revolving swing as the image of non-rigid steadfastness — new
  visual material for the "what remains" section.

## PASS 3: HOLES AND CONNECTIONS

- **Hole:** The formal equivalence between the Kāḷakārāma five
  declarations and the four-proposition tetralemma is argued by
  analogy ("bears some affinity") — Nāṇananda never maps them
  one-to-one. Note for the arguments pack.
- **Hole:** The dog simile's "moment it realizes" — the actual
  meditative moment of seeing-through is described as a realization,
  not a technique. The technique lives in Bahiya + radical reflection
  (yoniso upaparikkha).
- **Connection to Sermon 24:** Direct continuation — Kāḷakārāma taken
  apart plane by plane; Dvayamasutta recap; the four negations from
  Sermon 24's opening now explained.
- **Connection to Sermon 15:** The Bahiya instruction as practice
  manual (per Sermon 15) returns here as the RESULT formulation
  (na tena → na tattha → end of suffering).
- **Connection to Sermon 6:** The dog on the plank (originally used
  for nama-rupa) is reused for the seer/seen — same simile, new
  application. Nāṇananda flags the reuse himself.
- **Connection to Sermon 22:** The voidness-discourse category and
  the "commentary scared" pattern continue (four refutations here).
- **Connection to VBT pack:** The music simile IS the VBT practice of
  watching sensory experience change — verifiable: walk toward a
  sound, notice it is never the same music. The dog simile maps to
  the observer-not-found practice.
