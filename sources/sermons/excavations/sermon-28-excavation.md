# SERMON 28 — EXCAVATION

Source: Nibbana-28.pdf (BCBS/Dharma Seed, free). 7393 words.
(First sermon of the "elixir" half: path and practice.)

---

## SECTION 1: WHAT NIBBANA IS

### Right view includes cessation
"Right view, the first factor of the noble eightfold path, is defined
as the knowledge of all the four noble truths, namely that of
suffering, its arising, its cessation, and the path leading to its
cessation. This is a pointer to the fact that some understanding of
cessation, or Nibbāna, is essential for the practice of the path."

### Two conditions for right view
"Monks, there are these two conditions for the arising of right view.
Which two? Hearing from another and radical reflection."
Analayo parallel: Bodhi (2012: 178); "Yonisomanasikāra",
Encyclopaedia of Buddhism 8.3: 809–815.

### Elements seen as void
"Dhātuyo suññato passa — 'look upon the elements as void'."

## SECTION 2: HOW THE MECHANISM WORKS

### Yoniso manasikāra = attention by way of source
"Strictly speaking, yoniso manasikāra, or 'radical reflection', is
attention by way of source or matrix."
"The bodhisatta Vipassī went on reflecting from the very end of the
formula of paṭicca samuppāda, of dependent arising, in reverse order
and gradually arrived at the true source."
"Kimhi nu kho sati jarāmaraṇaṃ hoti, kiṃ paccayā jarāmaraṇaṃ?
Jātiyā kho sati jarāmaraṇaṃ hoti, jātipaccayā jarāmaraṇaṃ — 'What
being there, does decay and death come to be? Conditioned by what, is
decay-and-death? Birth being there does decay-and-death come to be,
conditioned by birth.'"
"In this way, he directed his radical reflection gradually upwards...
and at last came to the saṃsāric vortex between consciousness and
name-and-form."
"This is an illustration of the deepest sense of yoniso manasikāra as
an attitude essential for seeing the law of dependent arising within
one's own experience." (Mahāpadānasutta)

### The Sūciloma recap: from within
"This ito, 'hence', means from within one's self. This is clear from
the term attasambhūta, 'self-begotten'."
"They are revealed only to radical reflection, to attention by way of
source or matrix. That is why the Buddha emphasizes the need for
discerning the true source."
"And they that know wherefrom it springs, they dispel it... they
cross this flood so hard to cross, never crossed before, to become no
more."
Analayo parallel: Bodhi (2017: 201).

### The true source is ignorance, not craving
"The commentary takes the term yatonidānaṃ in this verse as a
reference to the second noble truth of craving... But it is actually an
allusion to ignorance. The true source is non-understanding. That is
why the Buddha... went beyond craving and placed ignorance at the head
of the series of twelve links."
"When one probes into the conditions for craving, one discovers
ignorance as its root."

### Attasambhava = the self from duality
"The notion of a self is to be traced to an interrelation between two
conditions, that is, the reciprocal relationship between consciousness
and name-and-form... 'dependent on consciousness is name-and-form',
'dependent on name-and-form is consciousness'."
"Here is a vortex, a turning round. The delusion or ignorance is the
non-understanding of the reciprocal relationship between these two."
"So ignorance is the non-understanding of the fact that the basis of
this attasambhava or springing up from within, namely, the dichotomy,
is in fact a mutual interrelation between two conditions."

### The tulam atulaṃ verse (the armour of self)
"That preparation for becoming, the Sage gave up, whence arise an
'equal' and an 'unequal', inwardly rapt and concentrated, he split
like an armour the origin of self."
Nāṇananda's reading: "The Sage renounced the preparations for
becoming, which give rise to a distinction between equal and unequal...
Inwardly content and concentrated he broke up the point of origin of
self like an armour."
"This breaking up of the armour happened not at the moment he uttered
this verse, but at the moment he attained perfect enlightenment."
"The armour of Māra, the armour of self-origin, attasambhava, has been
broken down already and as such he is not within the clutches of
Māra."
Analayo parallel: Bodhi (2012: 1215); also AN Eights, Udāna.

### Bhavasankhara vs ayusankhara
"The Buddha renounced the preparations pertaining to the life span,
āyusaṅkhārā, after several requests to that effect by Māra... The
The former, that is bhavasaṅkhārā, are preparations productive of
existence, which go to build up a bhava. These the Buddha had already
done away with by breaching the saṃsāric vortex between viññāṇa and
nāmarūpa. Chinnaṃ vaṭṭaṃ na vattati, 'the whirlpool cut off whirls no
more'."
"That renouncing is compared in that Sutta itself to a vomiting. The
Buddha tells Ānanda that it is not in the nature of a Tathāgata to
take in what he has already vomited, even for the sake of life."
Analayo parallel: Bodhi (2000: 1943) comment quoted.

### Atta/niratta = taking in/throwing out (not "self")
"It is derived from dā prefixed by ā, giving ādatta, which by
syncopation becomes ātta... Niratta is derived from nirassati."
"These two terms, suggestive of a duality, remind us of the water
pump... Due to these two aspects in the mechanism of a water pump, we
call it a unit... In this very concept of a unit, one can discern the
delusion involved."
"Though we regard them as two functions, the taking in and throwing
out are simply two aspects of the same function." (Greeds/hate = two
feelers from the apex delusion.)

### The sage without grading (verses)
"The sage does not grade himself, among equals, inferiors or
superiors, being at peace and with selfishness gone, he neither takes
up nor throws away." (Attadaṇḍa)
"Let the monk inwardly calm himself, let him not seek peace from
outside, to one who is inwardly calm, there is nothing taken up or
rejected." (Tuvaṭaka — incentive to upasamānussati)
"To that Brahmin... there is neither a farther shore nor a hither
shore nor both." (Dhammapada Brāhmaṇa Vagga)
Analayo parallel: Bodhi (2017: 312, 317).

## SECTION 3: WHAT KEEPS THE MECHANISM RUNNING

### Non-understanding of the interrelation
"The ignorance which gives rise to those preparations that go to
create the vortex between consciousness and name-and-form is the
non-understanding of the mutual interrelation implicit in this vortical
interplay."
"Craving finds something to hold onto precisely because one presumes
that there actually exists a thing to be grasped."

### The friends forget their friendship
"It is as if the two friends, who amicably prepared the chess board,
forgot their friendship when they confronted each other as two sides.
This duality is a very subtle problem."

### Latencies to perception
"These are the latencies to perception, which in the Madhupiṇḍikasutta
find mention in the expression saññā nānusenti, 'perceptions do not
lie latent'. Whereas the arahant does away with these latencies, the
non-arahant entertains them to some extent or other."
"Signs taken up in the far distant past in one's saṃsāra can come up
again and again as attachments and aversions to perpetuate one's
saṃsāric existence."

## SECTION 4: WHAT THE MECHANISM BUILDS

### Samsara as a chess game between organic and inorganic
"Our entire saṃsāric existence is a chess game between the organic,
upādiṇṇa, and the inorganic, anupādiṇṇa. The four elements within this
body, the grasped par excellence... and the four elements as nutrition
and atmosphere are always in conflict in their game of chess."
"These chess game... vicissitudes: the disturbances of the three
humours wind, bile and phlegm, on the physical side, and greed, hate
and delusion on the mental side."

### The perception of form built on the four primaries
"Because of these four primaries we have a perception of form, which
enables us to take signs. All the four are actually impermanent, but
the perceptual data we have gathered dependent on them are indelibly
imprinted on our minds."
"The thoughts and prolific concepts arise out of this perception of
form."

## SECTION 5: HOW TO SEE THROUGH IT

### The element meditation (MahāHatthipadopama, Sāriputta)
"Whatever is internal... hard, solid and clung to, that is, head
hairs, body hairs, nails, teeth, skin, flesh, sinews, bones...
"Now whatever is the internal earth element and whatever is the
external earth element, both are simply the earth element; and that
should be seen as it actually is with right wisdom thus: 'This is not
mine, this I am not, this is not my self.'"
"Having seen it as it actually is with right wisdom, one becomes
disenchanted with the earth element, becomes dispassionate towards the
earth element."
Analayo parallel: Ñāṇamoli (1995: 279); MĀ 30.

### Viraga as fading away and decolouration
"Paṭhavīdhatuyā cittaṃ virājeti seems to imply something like 'he
makes the earth element fade away from his mind'."
"The function of disenchantment, nibbidā, is to see that whatever
colour the earth element had infused in the mind is made to fade away.
It is a detachment as well as a decolouration."

### Why resolve internal/external (the purpose)
"The purpose is the breaking up of the foundation for cravings,
conceits and views. For 'me' to acquire some object out of craving
that object has to exist apart from 'me'... The statement 'this is
mine' presupposes a duality between 'me' and 'mine'."
"For instance, one gazing at a mirror is imperceptibly involved in
this duality when he tries to compare his face with its reflection."
"What fosters this opposite point of view is the very absence of the
distinction between the internal and the external. The fundamental
basis for acquisition or measuring is gone."
"It is as if the unending game of chess with all its vicissitudes has
ended in a peaceful draw."

### Equanimity through elements (butcher simile)
"Just as a skilled butcher or his apprentice, having killed a cow
were seated at the crossroads with it cut up into small pieces, so a
monk reflects on this same body... as consisting of elements."
"When a butcher... cuts it into small pieces and sits at the
crossroads ready to sell the meat, he is no longer particular about
the cow from which it came. He is conscious of it merely as a heap of
meat. Similarly, the contemplation by way of elements inculcates an
equanimous attitude."
"Among the parts of our body, there are some we pride on and cherish,
some others, like excreta and urine, we abhor and detest. When
regarded as mere elements, attachment and revulsion give place to
equanimity."
Analayo parallel: Ñāṇamoli (1995: 148); MĀ 98; EĀ 12.1.

### Not atomistic — body as it stands
"It is noteworthy that the monk is instructed to reflect on this same
body as it stands and as it is disposed, imam eva kāyaṃ yathāṭhitaṃ
yathāpaṇihitaṃ... they do not imply an atomistic or microscopic
analysis."
"In this way he abides contemplating the body as a body internally, or
... externally, or ... both internally and externally." (Ajjhatta/
bahiddhā = one's own/another's dichotomy.)

### From diversity to unity
"Yathā idaṃ tathā etaṃ, yathā etaṃ tathā idaṃ — 'just as this, so is
that; just as that, so is this'."
"Our minds are obsessed by the perception of diversity, nānattasaññā...
The reflection by way of elements... could even be appreciated as a
step towards the perception of unity, ekattasaññā... It tends to
relaxation and unification of the mind."

### The Buddha's research: gratification, danger, escape
"I went in search of the gratification in the earth element... I went
in search of the danger... I went in search of the stepping out...
that have I found out; whatever is the range... I have well discerned
with wisdom."
Definitions: assāda = "bodily pleasure and mental happiness, sukhaṃ
somanassaṃ"; danger = "impermanent, suffering and changing nature";
stepping out = "the disciplining and abandonment of desire for it,
chandarāgavinayo chandarāgappahānaṃ."
Analayo parallel: Bodhi (2000: 646) (Nidānasaṃyutta).

### The four requisites practiced (Ariyavaṃsa)
"On getting alms food he partakes of it without greed, uninfatuated,
unenslaved, being aware of the danger in it, with the wisdom in
stepping out."
"Nissaraṇapañño, 'with the wisdom in stepping out', in the highest
sense is taking food with the deeper idea of abandoning food in
accordance with the cryptic dictum āhāraṃ nissāya āhāraṃ pajahati,
'gives up food depending on food'."
Analayo parallel: Bodhi (2012: 415).

### Erasing the perception of form (the true purpose)
"All the meditation techniques the Buddha has taught are directed
towards the fading away of this perception of form."
"He preached about the impermanence of the four elements to expose the
hollowness and vanity of this drama of existence to erase the
perception of form, productive of this drama, from the minds of
beings."

### The earth story as disenchantment device
Aggaññasutta: "After billions and billions of years, a savoury earth
spread itself over the waters, like the tissue that forms over hot
milk as it cools... Some being of a greedy nature, exclaiming: 'Ah!
What can this be?', tasted this savoury earth with his finger. Craving
arose in him as a result of it... their subtle bodies became gross...
The environment changed in unison, becoming grosser and grosser." (The
perilous aspect.)
Sattasuriyasutta: "As the holocaust draws near, a second orb of the sun
appears, and then a third, a fourth, a fifth, a sixth and a seventh.
The great earth in its entirety, together with its mountains and rocks,
goes ablaze... like in a spot where oil or ghee had burnt."
"So here we have no room for any atomism."

### The conclusion verse
"So impermanent, monks, are preparations, so unstable, so
unsatisfying. So much so, monks, this is enough to get disenchanted
with preparations, this is enough to get dispassionate with them, this
is enough to get released from them."
Analayo parallel: Bodhi (2012: 1071); MĀ 8.

## REFUTATIONS

### The commentary's craving reading of yatonidānaṃ
"The commentary takes the term yatonidānaṃ as a reference to the
second noble truth of craving... But it is actually an allusion to
ignorance."

### The tulam atulaṃ commentarial quandary
"The commentators themselves are in a quandary. They advance
alternative interpretations..." (Four listed: sense-sphere vs
fine-material; or else immaterial; karmic result small/great;
tula = comparing, atula = Nibbāna — plus Nettippakaraṇa's
saṅkhāradhātu/nibbānadhātu.)
"It seems, however, that we have to approach the whole problem from a
different angle altogether. The twin term tulam atulaṃ most probably
represents the principle of duality."

### Atta as "self" in attaṃ nirattaṃ
"Very often scholars interpret the term attaṃ in this context as
'self', which in our opinion is incorrect."

### The atomistic turn in later Buddhism
"Some Buddhist sects indulged in academic subtleties which seem to
obsess the mind with the four elements with concepts about them,
instead of the simpler reflection on elements characteristic of the
suttas. Originally the purpose was to erase the four elements from the
mind... It ended up in hair-splitting analyses even literally,
painting for instance the earth element all the more vividly in the
mind."
"It is no exaggeration to say that all this tended to obscure the
path to Nibbāna in the course of time."

## PASS 2 FINDS

- **Miss 1:** Sattasuriya quote lacked the source lead-in "As the
  holocaust draws near" and had an editorial label inside the quote —
  both corrected.
- **Miss 2:** Bhavasankhara quote dropped "that is" and "which go to
  build up a bhava" — restored.
- **Miss 3:** Vipassī passage had a colon-splice joining Nāṇananda's
  narration to the Pali quote — separated.
- **Miss 4:** Aggañña quote had "Aggaññasutta:" inside the quotation
  marks (editorial label as source text) — moved outside; "tasted...
  Craving arose in him" restored to source tense/punctuation.
- **Miss 5:** Voice chess line had dropped the Pali upādiṇṇa/
  anupādiṇṇa terms — restored.
- The tulam atulaṃ verse gets a full editorial history — four
  commentarial interpretations plus Nettippakaraṇa, then Nāṇananda's
  duality reading — one of the densest textual-criticism passages in
  the series.
- The bhavasankhara/ayusankhara distinction (existence-preparations vs
  life-span preparations; the vomiting simile) with Bodhi's agreeing
  comment quoted — a named scholar's position recorded.
- The water-pump simile for atta/niratta (taking in/throwing out as
  one unitary function) — new image for the duality mechanism.
- The "peaceful draw" ending of the chess parable — the resolution
  image that completes Sermon 27's chess game.
- The Buddha's threefold element research (gratification/danger/
  escape) with technical definitions — the systematic practice frame.
- The Ariyavaṃsa almsfood formula and āhāraṃ nissāya āhāraṃ
  pajahati — food as both support and object of relinquishment.
- The Aggañña/Sattasuriya earth-story pair — cosmology deployed for
  disenchantment, explicitly "no room for any atomism."
- Sāriputta as the preacher of the element meditation (named role).

## PASS 3: HOLES AND CONNECTIONS

- **Hole:** The mirror-gazing duality illustration is gestured at
  ("one gazing at a mirror...") without the full Punna Mantaniputta
  treatment (Sermon 10) — cross-reference rather than new content.
- **Hole:** The two conditions for right view (hearing + radical
  reflection) are stated; the practice of each is distributed across
  the remaining sermons — track delivery in 29–33.
- **Connection to Sermon 27:** Direct continuation — Sūciloma resumed
  where it stopped; chess parable resolved; yoniso manasikāra as the
  promised path-prerequisite now unpacked.
- **Connection to Sermon 3/18:** Vipassī's reverse reflection and the
  vortex between consciousness and name-and-form return as the
  mechanism behind attasambhava.
- **Connection to Sermon 4:** Element contemplation (four modes:
  internal/external/both) is the satipatṭhāna method the scaffold
  Section 5 needs — now given with purpose and refutations.
- **Connection to Sermon 19 (Dhatuvibhanga):** the element technique
  cross-referenced in-source ("we have already mentioned this").
- **Connection to VBT pack:** The element practice maps directly to
  the VBT body/elements practice; the "fading away/decolouration"
  reading of virāga gives the practice its felt quality.
