# SERMON 27 — EXCAVATION

Source: Nibbana-27.pdf (BCBS/Dharma Seed, free). 6704 words.

---

## SECTION 1: WHAT NIBBANA IS

### Hunger-less and fully appeased (Dvayatānupassana)
"Whatever suffering that arises, all that is due to consciousness.
With the cessation of consciousness, there is no arising of suffering.
Knowing this peril... by calming down consciousness, a monk is
hunger-less and fully appeased."
"Nibbāna is unfalsifying in its nature, that they understood as the
truth, and, indeed, by the higher understanding of that truth, they
have become hunger-less and fully appeased."
Analayo parallel: Bodhi (2017: 283, 288).

### The pivot: purgative done, elixir to come
"Up to now, we have been administering a purgative, to dispel some
deep-rooted wrong notions. If it has worked, it is time now for the
elixir."
"So the few sermons that will follow, might well be an elixir to the
minds of those meditators striving hard day and night to realize
Nibbāna."

### The aim restated (six qualities of the Dhamma)
"It is to pave the way for an attainment of this goal, by
rediscovering the intrinsic qualities of this Dhamma that is well
proclaimed, svākkhāto, visible here and now, sandiṭṭhiko, timeless,
akāliko, inviting one to come and see, ehipassiko, leading one onwards,
opanayiko, and realizable personally by the wise, paccattaṃ veditabbo
viññūhi."

### Surrogate Nibbāna warned against
"Many of those who aspire to Nibbāna today, aim not at the cessation
of existence, but at some form of quasi existence as a surrogate
Nibbāna. If the aiming is wrong, will the arrow reach the target?"

## SECTION 2: HOW THE MECHANISM WORKS

### The first week under the Bodhi tree (Bodhivagga)
"After the attainment of enlightenment, the Buddha spent the first
week in the same seated posture under the Bodhi tree, and on the last
night of the week he reflected on the Law of Dependent Arising in the
direct order in the first watch of the night, in the reverse order in
the second watch, and both in direct and reverse order in the last
watch."
"These last-mentioned reflection, both in direct and reverse order, is
like a compromise between the deluded point of view and the discerning
point of view."

### Seeing the arising of the bases liberates (Soṇasutta)
"The mind of one who is fully attuned to renunciation and mental
solitude... on seeing the arising of sense-bases, is fully
emancipated."
"To see how the sense-bases arise is to be released in mind."
Analayo parallel: Bodhi (2012: 936); MĀ 123; SĀ 264.

### Magic consciousness becomes non-manifestative
"Magic consciousness subsides. In other words, it is transformed into
a non-manifestative consciousness, which no longer displays any
magic."

### Perception as the source (saññā)
"The term saññā has connotations of a 'mark', a 'sign', or a
'token'... It is as if a party going through a forest is blazing a
trail for their return by marking notches on the trees with an axe. The
notion of permanence is therefore implicit in the term saññā."
"So it is this saññā that gives rise to papañcasaṅkhā, reckonings
through prolificity."
"Saññāṃ pariññā vitareyya oghaṃ — 'comprehend perception and cross the
flood'." (Guhaṭṭhakasutta)
Analayo parallel: "Nimitta", Encyclopaedia of Buddhism 7.1: 177–179.

### Lust is a maker of signs (Mahāvedalla)
"Lust, friend, is something, hate is something, delusion is something.
They have been abandoned in an influx-free monk, uprooted, made like a
palm tree deprived of its site, made extinct and rendered incapable of
sprouting again."
"Lust, friend, is significative (nimittakaraṇo), hate is significative,
delusion is significative."
"It is only so long as there are things like lust, hate and delusion
that signs are significant... why the Tathāgata declared that there is
no essence in the magic show of consciousness is because there is
nothing in him that signs or symbols can signify or symbolize."
"The term akiñcana, literally 'thing-less', is an epithet for the
arahant. He is thing-less not because he no longer has the worldly
possessions of a layman, but because the afore-said things lust, hate
and delusion are extinct in him."
Analayo parallel: Ñāṇamoli (1995: 395).

### The three deliverances explained
"Animitta, the signless, is that stage in which the mind refuses to
take a sign or catch a theme in anything. Where lust, hate and
delusion are not there to give any significance, signs become
ineffective."
"Paṇidhi means 'direction of the mind', an 'aspiration'. In the
absence of any aspiration, there is nothing 'essence-tial' in
existence."
"Appatiṭṭhaṃ means 'unestablished'. Mind gets established when there
is desire or aspiration... Contemplation on the suffering aspect,
dukkhānupassanā, eliminates desire. Contemplation on not-self,
anattānupassanā, does away with the notion of substantiality...
Contemplation of impermanence, aniccānupassanā, ushers in the
signless, animitta, state of the mind that takes no object,
anārammaṇaṃ."

### Sense pleasures are Māra's realm (Āneñjasappāya)
"Impermanent, monks, are sense pleasures, they are empty, false and
deceptive by nature, they are conjuror's tricks, monks, tricks that
make fools prattle... they all are within the realm of Māra, they are
the domain of Māra, the bait of Māra, the beat of Māra."
Analayo parallel: Ñāṇamoli (1995: 870); MĀ 75.

### The chess game parable (full)
"Two friends are seen drawing something together on a board with two
kinds of paints... They are painting a chess board."
"The game of chess demands two sides — the principle of duality... A
hazy idea of victory and defeat, another duality, hovers above them.
But they are playing the game just for fun."
"A happy-go-lucky benefactor comes by and offers a handsome prize...
Now that the prospect of a prize has aroused greed in them, the
innocuous game becomes a tussle for a prize. Worthless pieces dazzle
with the prospect of a prize."
"There comes a pervert killjoy, who shows a threatening weapon and
adds a new rule... The winner will get the prize all right, but the
loser he will kill... The sportive spirit is gone. It is now a
struggle for dear life... It is no longer a game, but a miserable
struggle to escape death."
"Those worthless chunks of wood... received special recognition once
they took on the paint... With the prospect of a prize, they got
animated... due to cravings, conceits and views in the minds of the
two players... especially after the death knell sounded, the whole
chess board became the world for these two friends."
"The world, in fact, is a chessboard, where an unending chess game
goes on."

### Adhimutta facing the bandits
"When one sees with wisdom, this world as comparable to grass and
twigs, not finding anything worthwhile holding onto as mine, one does
not grieve, saying: 'O! I have nothing!'"
"Venerable Adhimutta's fearless challenge to the bandit chief was
extraordinary: You may kill me if you like..."
Analayo parallel: Norman (1969: 14).

### Victory and defeat (Dhammapada)
"Victory breeds hatred, in sorrow lies the defeated, the one serene is
ever at peace, giving up victory and defeat."
"These two go as a pair. This recognition of a duality is a distinctive
feature of this Dhamma. It gives, in a nutshell, the essence of this
Dhamma."
Analayo parallel: Norman (2004: 30).

### The two reflections (Dvayatānupassana)
"Whatsoever is pondered over as 'truth', that by the ariyans has been
well discerned with right wisdom, as it is, as 'untruth'. This is one
mode of reflection. Whatsoever is pondered over as 'untruth', that by
the ariyans has been well discerned with right wisdom, as it is, as
'truth'. This is the second mode of reflection."
"In whatever way they imagine, thereby it turns otherwise, that itself
is the falsity, of this puerile deceptive thing."
Analayo parallel: Bodhi (2017: 288).

### The reed-fruit verse
"Greed and hate and delusion too, sprung from within work harm on him
of evil wit, as does its fruit on the reed for which the bark is pith."
"The three defilements — greed, hatred and delusion — spring up from
within, that they are attasambhūta, self-begotten."
"It is generally believed that greed, hatred and delusion originate
from external signs... The Buddha points out that they arise from
within an individual and destroy him as in the case of the fruit of a
reed or bamboo."
Analayo parallel: Bodhi (2000: 167).

### The Madhupiṇḍika chain
"Dependent on eye and forms, friend, arises eye-consciousness; the
concurrence of the three is contact; because of contact, feeling; what
one feels, one perceives; what one perceives, one reasons about; what
one reasons about, one proliferates; what one proliferates, owing to
that, reckonings born of prolific perceptions overwhelm him in regard
to forms cognizable by the eye relating to the past, the future and the
present."
"Radical reflection is undermined when proliferation takes over. The
true source, the matrix, is ignored... tantamount to an entanglement
within and without, anto jaṭā bahi jaṭā."
Analayo parallel: Ñāṇamoli (1995: 203); MĀ 115.

### Sūciloma's questions and the Buddha's answer
Q: "Lust and hate, whence caused are they? Whence spring dislike,
delight and terror? Whence arising do thoughts disperse, like children
leaving their mother's lap?"
A: "It is hence that lust and hate are caused... moisture-born and
self-begotten, like the banyan's trunk-born runners they cleave to
diverse objects of sense, like the māluvā creeper entwining the forest.
And they that know wherefrom it springs, they dispel it... they cross
this flood so hard to cross, never crossed before, to become no more."
Analayo parallel: Bodhi (2017: 201); SĀ 1324; Enomoto 1989: 27.

### The banyan and the creeper
"Just as the runners of a banyan tree, once they take root would even
conceal the main trunk, which gave them birth, so the thoughts in the
mind, attached to external objects of sense, would conceal their true
source and origin."
"The māluvā creeper is a plant parasite... it overspreads the tree,
which gave it nourishment. Both similes illustrate the nature of non
radical reflection. Conceptual proliferation obscures the true source,
namely the psychological mainsprings of defilements."
"There is possibly a pun on the word sneha. Children are affection-
born and self-begotten, from a mother's point of view."

## SECTION 3: WHAT KEEPS THE MECHANISM RUNNING

### Signs find something within to signify
"Signs become significant due to greed, hate and delusion in our own
minds. Perceptions pander to these emotive tendencies."
"They become significant because they find something within that they
can signify and symbolize."

### Proliferation takes over from radical reflection
"The process is somewhat similar to the destruction of the reed by its
own fruit. It shows how non-radical reflection comes about."
"Non radical reflection could easily come in... The result is the
pursuit of a mirage, spurred on by non-radical reflection."

## SECTION 4: WHAT THE MECHANISM BUILDS

### The chessboard world
"Especially after the death knell sounded, the whole chess board
became the world for these two friends. Their entire attention was on
the board — a life and death struggle."
"Worthless pieces dazzle with the prospect of a prize."
"But this is only one aspect of our illustration. The world, in fact,
is a chessboard, where an unending chess game goes on."

### Thoughts concealing their source
"Those defiling thoughts, arisen from within, once they get attached
to sense objects outside, obscure their true source."

## SECTION 5: HOW TO SEE THROUGH IT

### See how the trick is done
"Now, in a magic show to see how the magic is performed, is to get
disenchanted with it, to make it fade away and cease, to free the mind
from its spell. By seeing how a magician performs, one gets disgusted
with what he performs."
"Similarly, seeing the arising of the six bases of sense-contact is the
way to get disenchanted with them, to make them fade away and cease,
to transcend them and be emancipated."

### Comprehend perception
"When perception is understood inside out, disenchantment sets in as a
matter of course, since delusion is no more."
"One has to comprehend perception for what it is, in order to become
dispassionate towards it."

### Radical reflection as path-prerequisite
"Now this is where the question of radical reflection, yoniso
manasikāra, comes in. What the Buddha brings up in this particular
context, is the relevance of that radical reflection as a pre-requisite
for treading the path."

### The two reflections as practice
"From this, one can well imagine what a great difference, what a
contrast exists between the two stand-points."

## SECTION 6: WHAT REMAINS

### The arahant's chessboard view
"Now, for the arahant, the whole world appears like a chessboard."
"Not finding anything worthwhile holding onto as mine, one does not
grieve."

### At peace having given up both
"The arahant is at peace, having given up victory and defeat. Isn't it
enough for him to give up victory? Why is it said that he gives up
both victory and defeat? These two go as a pair."
"This recognition of a duality is a distinctive feature of this
Dhamma."

### Detached from percepts (Māgandiya)
"To one detached from percepts there are no bonds, to one released
through wisdom there are no delusions, those who hold on to percepts
and views, go about wrangling in this world."
Analayo parallel: Bodhi (2017: 302).

## REFUTATIONS

### The commentary's crow simile (Sūciloma reading)
"We are forced to depart from the commentarial trend... The
commentary... alludes to a game among village lads, in which they tie
the leg of a crow with a long string and let it fly away so that it is
forced to come back and fall at their feet."
"The commentary rather arbitrarily breaks up the compound term
manovitakkā... The commentator was forced to contradict himself in the
present context, because he wanted to justify the awkward simile of the
game he himself had introduced."
"We take it as one word, whereas the commentary breaks it up into two
words. It is queer to find the same commentator analyzing this
compound differently in another context."
"We adopted the variant reading kumārakā vaṃkam iv' ossajanti, found
in some editions... 'even as children leave the lap'."
Analayo parallel: SĀ 1324 (依倚於乳母); Yogācāra quote, Enomoto 1989: 27.

### The external-origin belief
"It is generally believed that greed, hatred and delusion originate
from external signs. The magic show and the chess game have shown us
how signs become significant."

### Surrogate Nibbāna (aiming wrong)
"Many of those who aspire to Nibbāna today, aim not at the cessation
of existence, but at some form of quasi existence as a surrogate
Nibbāna."

## PASS 2 FINDS

- The chess game parable is Nāṇananda's own extended composition
  (paint → pieces → prize → death rule) and the second great
  narrative illustration after the magic show — extracted in full.
- The pivot statement (purgative → elixir) marks the structural
  midpoint of the whole 33-sermon series: sermons 1–27 clear wrong
  views about the goal; 28–33 teach the path. Record for the series
  graph.
- The surrogate-Nibbāna warning is the sermon's contemporary polemic
  — aimed at present-day meditators.
- The Mahāvedalla sign-maker passages (kiñcano / nimittakaraṇo) are
  the doctrinal key of this sermon: signs are significant only while
  defilements remain — the mechanism behind the magic show.
- Adhimutta appears again (from Sermon 26's verse) now in his full
  bandit story — named person with role.
- The commentary's crow simile refutation with the compound-
  inconsistency argument (manovitakkā split in one place, whole in
  another) — a philological catch.
- Saññā notch-marking trail simile (blazing a trail by marking trees)
  — new image for perception in Section 2.
- The three deliverances mapped onto the three contemplations
  (dukkha→appatiṭṭha, anattā→anārammaṇa... anicca→animitta) —
  a doctrinal crosswalk not usually stated this compactly.

## PASS 3: HOLES AND CONNECTIONS

- **Hole:** The "elixir" for the following sermons is promised but
  not given here — the actual path instructions are deferred to 28–33.
  The excavation for those must track what is delivered against this
  promise.
- **Hole:** The chess parable's mapping is only sketched (paint =
  name/form, prize = craving, death = Māra) — the explicit mapping is
  left to the reader.
- **Connection to Sermon 26:** Direct continuation — the magic show
  motif extended via consciousness→non-manifestative transformation;
  Bodhivagga's three watches answer how the Buddha resolved the two
  viewpoints.
- **Connection to Sermon 1/21:** Dvayatānupassana (Sermon 21's "what
  others call bliss") returns as the two reflections — now framed as
  practice.
- **Connection to Sermon 10:** The tangle verse (Jaṭā) returns with
  the paṭigha/rūpasaññā pair.
- **Connection to Sermon 12-13:** The Madhupiṇḍika chain is the
  papnca mechanism those sermons analyze; here it's used for the
  yoniso manasikāra argument.
- **Connection to VBT pack:** The chess parable maps to the VBT
  practice of watching significance arise — pieces worthless until the
  prize appears; the sign-maker analysis IS the concept-deconstruction
  practice's doctrinal ground.
- **Analayo note:** SĀ 1324 preserves the mother's-lap reading in
  Chinese (依倚於乳母) — supports Nāṇananda's variant against the
  commentary's crow.
