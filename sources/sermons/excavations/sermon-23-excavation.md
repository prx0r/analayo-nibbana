# SERMON 23 — EXCAVATION

Source: Nibbana-23.pdf (BCBS/Dharma Seed, free). 10472 words.

---

## SECTION 1: WHAT NIBBANA IS

### The arahant still says "I" (Arahantasutta)
"He might still say: 'I speak', he might also say: 'They speak to me',
being skilful in knowing the worldly parlance, he uses such terms
merely as a convention."
"The fact that the arahant, who has done away with the latencies to
conceits of 'I' and 'mine', still continues to use even the words 'I'
and 'mine', only as a concession to worldly conventions and common
parlance."
Analayo parallel: Bodhi (2000: 102).

### Arahant = worthy of gifts (the paradox)
"The irony arising from these statements is already implicit in the
term arahant... the basic sense is 'to be worthy of gifts'. In fact,
it is being worthy of receiving everything. It is by giving up all
that one becomes worthy of all."
"To become an arahant is to let go of everything. Craving has to be
fully abandoned. It is when all desires are gone, when everything is
given up, that one becomes worthy of receiving everything."

### Nibbana = cessation of the six sense-spheres
"All those measuring rods and scales lose their applicability with the
cessation of the six sense-spheres. That is why Nibbāna is defined as
the cessation of the six sense-spheres, saḷāyatananirodho Nibbānaṃ."
"The cessation of the six sense-spheres is Nibbāna."

### Nibbana is serene awareness, not a void
"Therefore, Nibbāna is not an experience as dry as a log of wood, but
a state of serene awareness of its true significance. It is a
transcendence of the world by realization of its cessation."

### Perception of nibbana in the fruit-attainment
"To the question: 'Of what is he percipient?', once Venerable Sāriputta
gave the answer that the perception is of Nibbāna as the cessation of
existence, bhavanirodho nibbānaṃ."
"What a monk attends to while in that attainment could be summed up in
the stereotyped phrase: Etaṃ santaṃ, etaṃ paṇītaṃ, yadidaṃ
sabbasaṅkhārasamatho... taṇhakkhayo virāgo nirodho nibbānaṃ."

## SECTION 2: HOW THE MECHANISM WORKS

### The fumigating verb (vidhūpeti / sandhūpeti)
"The Buddha also had to stage a similar fire ordeal in the Dhammayāga,
or the 'Dhamma-sacrifice', he administered to exorcize the malignant
personality view, sakkāyadiṭṭhi, ingrained in the minds of worldlings."
"The word vidhūpeti, derived from the word dhūpa, 'incense', is
suggestive of 'fumigating' or 'smoking out'."
Bodhivagga (Udāna): "When dhammas manifest themselves, to the
resolutely meditating Brahmin, he stands fumigating the hordes of Māra,
like the sun irradiating the firmament."
"While some fragrant kinds of incense are used for propitiating
benevolent spirits, certain caustic types are utilized for exorcising
evil spirits... the phrase could be rendered as 'he exorcises and does
not propitiate'." (Khajjanīyasutta)

### Flotsam after the vortex ceases
"The ordinary worldling's mode of recognition of the Tathāgata is
comparable to the recognition of a vortex that has already ceased with
the help of the flotsam and jetsam lightly floating around it. Even
after the vortex has ceased, flotsam and jetsam could still go on
rotating, giving the wrong impression that the vortex is still there."
"If one understands that the vortex has actually ceased deep down at
its centre, and that what remains there, now, is the great ocean...
one can get rid of the unfounded fear arising from the statement that
there is no Tathāgata in truth and fact."
"The cessation of the puny centre of the whirlpool is equivalent to
inheriting an expansive great ocean. To give up the limitations of a
vortex, is to inherit the limitless ocean."

### The six sense-bases as six measuring rods
"There are six modes of measuring in accordance with the conceit 'am',
asmimāna. What is known as saḷāyatana, or the six sense-bases,
comprise the six scales of measurement, asserting the conceit 'am'."
"At whatever point of time the measuring, evaluating and assessing
done by the six sense-bases... ceases, the person concerned thereby
becomes immeasurable, invaluable and boundless."
"The only way of becoming immeasurable and boundless is to abandon all
those scales of measurement."
"The subtlest scale of measurement, however, is that based on the mind.
It is in this mode of measuring and reckoning that concepts and
designations play their part."

### The world as thought-construct
"The above definition of the world brings out the 'prepared',
saṅkhata, nature of the world. It is a thought-construct. This does
not amount to a negation of the role of materiality. All we mean to say
is that the concept of the world is actually an outcome of these six
sense bases."
"The world arises in the six sense-bases. It is at the same time the
arising of suffering... The law of dependent arising is an explanation
of the way a concept of the world comes about. This is an extremely
subtle point."

### The duality of existence/non-existence (Kaccāyanagotta)
"This world, Kaccāyana, for the most part, bases its views on two
things: on existence and non-existence. Now, to one who with right
wisdom sees the arising of the world as it is, the view of non-existence
regarding the world does not occur. And to one who with right wisdom
sees the cessation of the world as it really is, the view of existence
regarding the world does not occur."
Analayo parallel: SĀ 301 (CBETA T02 no.99 p.85).

### The circularity of time
"The furthest limit of the forenoon is the nearest limit of the
afternoon, and the furthest limit of the afternoon is the nearest limit
of the forenoon... A similar time cycle is to be found even in one
moment. Rise and fall occur as a cycle even within a single moment."
"World systems go on contracting and expanding... Therefore it is
impossible to find any beginning or end. The very question of a first
beginning is ill conceived. It is like an attempt to find a starting
point in a cycle."

### The Lokāyata circularity (Pūraṇa Kassapa vs Nigaṇṭha)
Pūraṇa Kassapa: "I dwell knowing and seeing an infinite world with an
infinite knowledge." Nigaṇṭha Nāthaputta: "I dwell knowing and seeing
a finite world with a finite knowledge."
"You see the world as infinite because your knowledge lacks finality...
you are seeing the world as finite because your knowledge is limited...
So here, too, we have a circle, or rather a circularity of argument."
"Enough, brahmins, let that question be... I shall preach to you the
Dhamma." (Alaṃ brāhmaṇā, tiṭṭhat' etaṃ... Dhammaṃ vo desissāmi.)
"The expression used here is suggestive of the fact that the question
belongs to the category of unexplained points."

### The four walkers simile
"Four persons endowed with the highest ability to walk... stand in the
four directions... Each of them tells himself: 'I will reach the end of
the world by walking' and goes on walking for hundred years... only to
die on the way without reaching the end of the world."
"Brahmins, it is these five strands of sense pleasures that in the
Noble One's discipline are called 'the world'."
"When one attains to the first jhāna, one is already far removed from
that world of the five sense-pleasures. But about him, the Buddha makes
the following pronouncement: 'This one, too, is included in the world,
this one, too, has not stepped out of the world.'"
"Only: a monk, having completely transcended the sphere of
neither-perception-nor-non-perception, attains to and abides in the
cessation of perceptions and feelings, and in him, having seen with
wisdom, the influxes are made extinct. This one is known as one who,
on reaching the end of the world, is dwelling at its very end."
Analayo parallel: Bodhi (2012: 1303-1304).

### The Rohitassa declaration
"It is in this very fathom-long physical frame with its perceptions
and mind that I declare lies the world, the arising of the world, the
cessation of the world, and the path leading to the cessation of the
world."
Pali: "Api c'āhaṃ, āvuso, imasmiṃ yeva byāmamatte kaḷevare sasaññimhi
samanake lokañca paññāpemi lokasamudayañca lokanirodhañca
lokanirodhagāminiñca paṭipadaṃ."
Rohitassa's story: the swift seer who "died on the way, without
reaching the end of the world" — and approved: "How well this was
said."
Analayo parallel: SĀ 1307 (赤馬天子, Chinese).

### Papañca's primary meaning is linguistic
"The primary significance of papañca is traceable to the linguistic
medium. We have already shown how the network of grammar spreads as
soon as the peg 'am' is driven down to earth, as it were. The reality
in the first person in grammar beckons a second and a third person to
complete the picture."
"The 'world' is precariously perched on a fictitious network of grammar
and logic."
"Usually, the term is glossed over by explaining it with reference to
taṇhā, māna and diṭṭhi... To rattle off the three instances is not a
fit answer to the question 'what is papañca'."

### The is/is-not as proliferation (Mahākoṭṭhita–Sāriputta)
Four questions: "With the remainderless fading away and cessation of
the six spheres of sense-contact, is there something left?" — "Do not
say so, friend." (nothing / both / neither — same reply.)
"Whatever is the range of the six spheres of sense-contact, that itself
is the range of conceptual proliferation, and whatever is the range of
conceptual proliferation, that itself is the range of the six spheres
of sense-contact. By the remainderless fading away and cessation of the
six spheres of sense-contact, there comes to be the cessation and
appeasement of conceptual proliferation."
"Here, then, we see the fistfuls of inflammable incense powder the
Buddha had directed towards language and logic, setting all that
ablaze."
Analayo parallel: SĀ 249 (Ānanda/Sāriputta variant).

### The recording machine
"This recording machine before us 'is there'. So also are the things
which we presume to exist. We ourselves do exist, do we not? One could
say 'I am'. Out of the two rapid processes going on within us every
moment, namely arising and passing away, we are most of the time
dwelling on the side of arising."
"Not only 'is' and 'is not', but also the entire logical structure
connecting these two postulates is founded on these six spheres."

### The physician breaches the vortex
"Like an expert physician, who treats the germ of a disease and
immunizes the patient, the Buddha effected a breach in the saṃsāric
vortex by concentrating on its epicycle within this fathom-long body."
Naḷakalāpīsutta: "Out of the two bundles of reeds left standing,
supporting each other, when one is drawn the other falls down. Even so,
with the cessation of consciousness comes the cessation of name-and-form."

## SECTION 3: WHAT KEEPS THE MECHANISM RUNNING

### The perception of the compact (ghanasaññā)
"The worldlings under the sway of defilements, which thrive on the
perception of the compact, ghanasaññā, have the habit of grasping
everything... he regards the world as a unit due to his perception of
the compact, as he takes cognizance only of the arising aspect,
ignoring the decaying aspect."

### Delirium of views (diṭṭhipariḷāha)
"With reference to speculative views... the Buddha uses the term
diṭṭhipariḷāha, 'delirium of views'. Pariḷāha means 'delirious fever'.
Patients in delirium cry out for water. The worldlings, in general, are
in high delirium."
"Their views, based on wrong reflections, were mere hallucinations.
They kept on raising such questions, because they had no insight into
the nature of saṅkhāras, or 'preparations'."
"The worldlings spend their whole lifetime running in search of the
world's end. All that is papañca, conceptual proliferation."

### Wrong attention at the root
"The fallacy involved here, that is, the inability to understand that
their concept of the world is the outcome of wrong attention, we
illustrated by the simile of the magic kettle."

## SECTION 4: WHAT THE MECHANISM BUILDS

### The world each species measures
"Dependent on that contact, feelings and perceptions arise, creating a
visual world. The visual world of the humans differs from that of
animals. Some things that are visible to animals are not visible to
humans. That is due to the constitution of the eye-faculty."
"Those six measuring rods have measured out a world for us."

### The world of papnca
"This world, which is a product of the spheres of sense-contact, is a
world of papañca, or 'proliferation'."
"The entire world is built up on the two concepts 'is' and 'is not'.
Being unaware of the saṅkhata, or 'prepared', nature of these concepts,
we are accustomed to say 'this is' as occasion demands."

## SECTION 5: HOW TO SEE THROUGH IT

### Stop walking, look here (Rohitassa as practice)
"The cycle cannot be discovered by travelling. It is not out there, but
in this very stream of consciousness within us. We have already
described it as the vortex between consciousness and name-and-form."
"There is only one solution, that is, to break the cycle. That is what
the term vaṭṭupaccheda means."
"This, then, is a level of experience realizable here and now."

### Cessation of perception-and-feeling and the three contacts
"This fact comes to light in the following answer of Venerable
Dhammadinnā Therī to the question raised by the lay-follower Visākha,
her former husband, in the Cūḷavedalla Sutta." (Visākha's role: the
lay questioner; Dhammadinnā's role: the teaching nun.)
"When a monk has emerged from the attainment of the cessation of
perceptions and feelings, three kinds of contact touch him: voidness
contact, signless contact, desireless contact." (Dhammadinnā Therī,
Cūḷavedalla)
"When a monk has emerged... his mind inclines to seclusion, slants to
seclusion, tends to seclusion."
Analayo parallel: MĀ 211, MĀ 210. Commentary: "'voidness' means the
attainment of the fruit of arahant-hood"; "what is called seclusion is
Nibbāna."

### Two ways of emergence (Vism 708, Griffith)
"The emergence comes about in two ways thus: by means of the fruition
of non-return in the case of the non-returner, or by means of the
fruition of arahantship in the case of the arahant." (Ñāṇamoli 1991:
737, trans. Vism 708)
"Griffith 1986/1991: 35 (On Being Mindless), suggests 'to take
Buddhaghosa's mention of the occurrence of the "fruits" as descriptive
of the first mental events that occur upon leaving the attainment of
cessation'."
Analayo parallel: AN 5.166 at AN III 194 (Bodhi 2012: 779).

### The fruit-attainment as extraordinary perception
"While discussing the significance of arahattaphalasamāpatti, also
known as aññāphalasamādhi, and aññāvimokkha, we had occasion to bring
up such quotations as the following."
"Could there be, Lord, for a monk such an attainment of concentration
wherein he will not be conscious of earth in earth, nor of water in
water... whatever is seen, heard, sensed, cognized... even of that he
will not be conscious — and yet he will be conscious?"
"It is Nibbāna, then, that one attends to while in that attainment."
Analayo parallel: Bodhi (2012: 1557f).

### The non-manifestative consciousness verse
"Consciousness, which is non-manifestative, endless, lustrous on all
sides, here it is that earth and water, fire and air no footing find.
Here it is that long and short, fine and coarse, pleasant, unpleasant,
and Name-and-form are cut off without exception, when consciousness has
surceased."
Parallel: Walshe (1987: 179); Sanskrit fragment Zhou 2008: 9; Chinese
DĀ 24.

## SECTION 6: WHAT REMAINS

### The end of proliferation
"By the remainderless fading away and cessation of the six spheres of
sense-contact, there comes to be the cessation and appeasement of
conceptual proliferation."

### The lotus on the rubbish heap (the positive side of void)
"As on top of a rubbish heap, dumped by the highway side, there
blossoms forth a lotus, pure in fragrance and charming. So amidst the
worldlings blind, the Fully Awakened One's disciple outshines them in
marked contrast, in point of wisdom bright."
"The dawn of that wisdom which sees the voidness of a self... marks the
efflorescence as well as the fruition of the saṃsāric existence of a
being."
"So, then, the arahant is that charming lotus, arising out of the
cesspool of saṃsāra. Surely there cannot be anything frightful about
it."

### The ocean without delimitations
"It is where a vortex ceases that the great ocean prevails
unhindered."
"The attention of one who has been looking at it turns towards the
depth, immeasurability and boundlessness of the great ocean."

## REFUTATIONS

### The Rohitassa misreading
"This momentous declaration... has been misinterpreted by some who have
not grasped its true significance. They say that according to this
discourse the cessation of the world is not here and that only the
other three are to be found in this fathom-long body."
"Such misinterpretations are the result of taking seriously various
far-fetched speculations of later origin about Nibbāna. According to
them, Nibbāna is some mysterious non-descript place of rest for the
arahants after their demise."

### The commentator's missed opportunity
"The commentary in this context simply observes that the four noble
truths are to be found not in grass and twigs outside, but in this body
consisting of the four elements. It has nothing more to add."
"A certain modern scholar has rightly pointed out that the commentator
has missed a great opportunity for exegesis. The reason... is probably
his predilection for these later speculations on Nibbāna."

### The Mahākoṭṭhita misreading
"The commentator gives the following explanation...: 'is there even a
little defilement left after the cessation?' But the question is
obviously not about the remaining defilements, in which case even a
categorical negative could have been the correct answer. The question
here is about the very usage of the expressions 'is' and 'is not'."

### The fear of void answered
"This line of reflection might even enable one to get a glimpse of an
unworldly beauty in this philosophy of the void, which drives an
unfounded fear into the minds of the worldlings."
"There is nothing to get scared about this prospect."

## PASS 3: HOLES AND CONNECTIONS

- **Hole:** The relation between the cessation attainment
  (saññāvedayitanirodha) and nibbāna is handled carefully — the sermon
  says influxes are extinct "on rising from it" by vision of wisdom,
  but the exact meditative sequence for a beginner is not laid out. The
  practice layer is the Rohitassa move: stop travelling, look at the
  measuring itself.
- **Hole:** Papañca's "primary linguistic meaning" is asserted and
  illustrated (grammar peg 'am') but the full derivation lives in
  Sermon 12-13 territory — cross-pack, note for the scaffold Section 3.
- **Connection to Sermon 22:** Direct continuation. The world riddle
  (SĀ 234), magic kettle, and six-bases definition recur; Sermon 23
  takes them into the Rohitassa declaration and papnca.
- **Connection to Sermon 21:** The vortex/flotsam simile completes the
  Anurādha problem — how to "see" someone whose vortex has ceased.
- **Connection to Sermon 19-20:** The tetralemma returns as the
  Mahākoṭṭhita four questions — now applied to what remains after the
  six spheres cease.
- **Connection to VBT pack:** "Break the cycle (vaṭṭupaccheda)" maps
  to the VBT direct-experience practice — the cycle is in the stream of
  consciousness, verifiable now. The six measuring rods map to the
  concept-deconstruction practice.
- **Analayo note:** SĀ 249 assigns the tetralemma dialogue to Ānanda
  asking Sāriputta (reversed from the Pali Mahākoṭṭhita framing) —
  worth recording for the evidence layer.
