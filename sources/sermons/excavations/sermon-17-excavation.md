# SERMON 17 — EXCAVATION

Source: Nibbana-17.pdf (BCBS/Dharma Seed, free). 7915 words.

---

## SECTION 1: WHAT NIBBANA IS

### The Itivuttaka form/formless verses
"Those beings that go to realms of form, and those who are settled in
formless realms, not understanding the fact of cessation, come back
again and again to existence. Those who, having comprehended realms of
form, do not settle in formless realms, are released in the experience
of cessation."
"The formless, or arupa, is like the shadow of form, rupa. When one
comprehends form, one also understands that the formless, too, is not
worthwhile settling in."

### The twig-fire simile (Sariputta's own experience)
"One perception arises in me: 'cessation of existence is Nibbana', and
another perception fades out in me... Just as, friend, in the case of
a twig fire, when it is burning one flame arises and another flame fades
out."
"The true significance of the simile is that Sariputta was attending to
the cessation aspect of preparations."
"Worldlings see only a process of burning in a flame. To the insight
meditator it can appear as an intermittent series of extinctions."

### The salayatananirodha riddle
"Therefore, monks, that sphere should be known wherein the eye ceases
and perceptions of form fade away... where the mind ceases and
perceptions of mind objects fade away."
"The Buddha got up and entered the monastery, leaving the monks perplexed."
Ananda: "It is with reference to the cessation of the six sense-spheres
that the Exalted One has preached this sermon."
Analayo parallel: SĀ 211.

### Nibbana as ayatana (sphere)
"Āyatana is an allusion to arahattaphalasamadhi. Its significance is
psychological."
"The appatittham, appavattam and anarammanam are suggestive of the
three doorways to deliverance: appanihita vimokkha (undirected),
sunyata vimokkha (void), animitta vimokkha (signless)."

### The touching of the deathless
"To be free from touch is in itself the 'touching' of the deathless
element. What we mean is that, as far as the fear of death is concerned,
here we have the freedom from the pain of death and the freedom from
the concept of death itself."
"Nibbana is called a bliss devoid of feeling, avedayita sukha."

## SECTION 2: HOW THE MECHANISM WORKS

### The arahant does not come back
"They, the arahants, do not formulate or put forward views, they do not
subscribe to any views, the true Brahmin is not liable to be led astray
by ceremonial rites and ascetic vows, the Such-like One, who has gone
to the farther shore, comes not back."
"The steadfastness associated with the epithet tadi is reinforced by
the simile of the firm post at the city gate."

### The upstream struggler
"Even with pain, even with displeasure, with tearful face and crying he
leads the holy life in its fullness and perfection."
"The second type, the upstream bound person, refrains from indulgence
in sense pleasures and from evil deeds."

### The four types of persons
1. Downstream bound: indulges, commits evil, bound in samsara.
2. Upstream bound: refrains, struggles, leads the holy life.
3. Stationary: non-returner, goes to Brahma world, ends suffering there.
4. The Brahmin standing on dry ground: crossed over, gone beyond, stands
   on the farther shore.

## SECTION 3: WHAT KEEPS THE MECHANISM RUNNING

### Perception of the compact
"The worldling who attends to the arising aspect and ignores the
cessation aspect is carried away by the perception of the compact."
"With that steadied mind the arahant attends to the cessation of
preparations."

### The fuel/grasping pun
"Upadana can mean 'grasping' as well as 'fuel'. The totality of
existence is sometimes referred to as a fire. The fuel for the fire of
existence is grasping itself."

---

## SECTION 4: WHAT THE MECHANISM BUILDS

### Formless as shadow
"The formless is like the shadow of form. When one comprehends form,
one also understands that the formless is not worthwhile settling in."

### Bhavanirodho nibbana (the equation)
"Bhavanirodho nibbanam — cessation of existence is Nibbana. The dictum
clearly shows that Nibbana is the cessation of existence."

---

## SECTION 5: HOW TO SEE THROUGH IT

### Attending to cessation
"How does one realize cessation? By attending to the cessation aspect
of preparations. The worldlings pay attention to the arising aspect.
The Buddhas have seen the cessation of existence in a subtle way."

### The steadied mind
"Thitam cittam vippamuttam, vayanca anupassati — the mind steadied and
released contemplates its own passing away."

### The Sariputta revelation
"Sariputta came out with his own experience, revealing that he himself
once attained to such a samadhi, when he was at Andhavana in Savatthi."

---

## SECTION 6: WHAT REMAINS

### The three doorways
"Appatittham = appanihita vimokkha (undirected, through extirpation of
craving). Appavattam = sunyata vimokkha (void, negation of continuity).
Anarammanam = animitta vimokkha (signless, no object)."

### The Paramatthaka close
"The Such-like One, who has gone to the farther shore, comes not back."

## REFUTATIONS

### The commentary on the ayatana passage
"It paraphrases ayatana by karana, meaning reason... The commentary
conjures up a place where there is no sun and no moon, a place that is
not a place. Such confounding trends have crept in probably due to the
very depth of this Dhamma."

### The asanthita/susanthita variant
"Some modern editions replace asanthita with susanthita. But the two
readings contradict each other. We accepted asanthita for some valid
reason and not simply because it accords with the commentary."

## PASS 2 FINDS

- The twig-fire simile is the most precise description of how the arahant
  sees existence — intermittent extinctions, not a continuous process.
- The ayatana passage is the most controversial in the canon — Nanananda
  resolves it as the arahant's concentration, not a destination.
- The three doorways mapped to appatittham/appavattam/anarammanam is
  a major interpretive move.
- The upstream struggler (tearful face, crying, holy life) is the most
  human image of practice in the series.
- The "gone beyond does not come back" is the definitive statement on
  the arahant's finality.
