# SERMON 8 — EXCAVATION

Source: Nibbana-08.pdf (BCBS/Dharma Seed, free). 9540 words.

---

## SECTION 1: WHAT NIBBANA IS

### Nibbana = cessation of existence (bhavanirodha)
"Nibbana has been defined as the cessation of existence. The Buddha says
that when he is preaching about the cessation of existence, some people,
particularly the brahmins who cling to a soul theory, bring up the charge
of nihilism against him. Not only those brahmins and heretics believing
in a soul theory, but even some Buddhist scholars are scared of the term
bhavanirodha, fearing that it leads to a nihilistic interpretation."

### The dhuva/sassata refutation
"They sometimes make use of the word sassata in defining Nibbana. This
is a word that should never be brought in to explain the term Nibbana.
According to them, Nibbana is a permanent and eternal state."
"The true significance of this synonym [dhuva] has not been understood.
It means stable or immovable... In the sense that the experience of
Nibbana is irreversible. That is why it is referred to as acalam sukham,
'unshakeable bliss'."
"All other such deliverances are shakeable, or irritable. Even if they
are unshaken during one's life time, they get shaken up at death. The
final winning post is the pain of death. Before the pain of death, all
other deliverances of the mind fall back defeated."

### Asmimana eradication = nibbana
"Sammamanabhisamaya antam akasi dukkhassa — by rightly understanding
conceit, he made an end of suffering."
"Asmanasamugghatam papunati dittheva dhamme Nibbanam — one arrives at
the eradication of the conceit 'am' which in itself is the attainment
of Nibbana here and now."
"Some seem to think that the eradication of the conceit 'am' is one
thing, and Nibbana another. But along with the eradication of the
conceit 'am', comes extinction."

### The ambrosial deathless
"The arahants partake of ambrosial deathlessness. What actually happened
in the case of the Venerable arahant Adhimutta was that the bandit chief
understood the Dhamma and set him free... But even if he had killed him,
Venerable Adhimutta would have passed away, experiencing the ambrosial
deathless. Why? Because he can let go before Mara comes to grab."
"It is that bliss of deliverance, the freedom from the fear of death.
Needless to say that it requires no seal of ever-lastingness."

## SECTION 2: HOW THE MECHANISM WORKS

### The rope/cord simile (the full mechanism)
"Suppose a rope or a cord is being made up by winding some strands from
either end by two persons. For the strands to gather the necessary
tension, the two persons have to go on winding in opposite directions.
But for the sake of an illustration, let us imagine a situation in which
a third person catches hold of the strands in the middle, just before
the other two start their winding. Oddly enough, by mistake, those two
start winding in the same direction. Both are unaware of the fact that
their winding is at the same time an unwinding."
"Let us suppose that the man in the middle suddenly lets go of his hold
with a 'twang'. Now what happens to the cord? The windings in the same
direction from both ends, which made it a cord, immediately get
neutralized and the cord ceases to be a cord!"
"No cord at all is made up, even if the two at either end go on winding
for aeons and aeons. Why? Simply because they are winding in the same
direction."
"The impermanence of the world does not affect us so long as there is
no grasping on our part. All windings in this world get unwinded
immediately. This is the nature of the world."
"It is the grasp in the middle that accounts for the cord, or rather,
for whatever has the semblance of a cord."

### The sewing machine simile
"When a sewing machine goes into action, it sews up two folds of cloth
together. But supposing suddenly the shuttle runs out of its load of
cotton. One might even mistake the folds to be actually sewn up, until
one discovers that they are separable."
"For the arahant, the shuttle refuses to put in the knot. For him,
preparations are ineffective in producing a prepared. He has no cravings,
conceits and views. For knots of existence to occur, there has to be an
attachment in the form of craving, a loop in the form of conceit, and a
a tightening in the form of views."
"The arahant's mind works like a sewing machine with the shuttle run
out of its load of cotton."

### The ice-cube melting
"The ordinary worldling clings to the perception of earth in a piece of
ice because of its hardness. But when we heat it up to a certain degree,
its watery quality reveals itself. Further heating would bring up its
fiery nature. Continuous heating will convert it into vapour, revealing
its air quality."
"Even Brahma-hood is subject to 'liquidation', like an ice-cube."

### The cardboard houses
"In order to produce a tragic scene on the screen, the film producers
adopt subtle devices and camera tricks. Sometimes an awe-inspiring scene
of conflagration... is produced with the help of cardboard houses.
Cardboard houses are set on fire, but the audience is hoodwinked into
thinking that a huge mansion is on fire."
"If any member of the audience truly understands at that moment that
these are cardboard houses and toys toppled from hill tops, he sees
something comic in the apparently tragic."

### The puppet show
"Beings who enjoyed high positions come down to the level of hungry
ghosts, petas, or even to lower realms in their very next birth."
"Beings, who are born in accordance with their karma, entertain the
conceit 'I am a god', 'I am a Brahma'. Once their karma is spent up,
they get destroyed and are reborn somewhere or other. It is the same
with those items used in a drama, such as the stool and the footstool."

### The stool/footstool
"What in one scene serves as a sitting-stool, could be improvised as a
footstool in another scene, and as a table in yet another."
"The same piece of wood, which in one act serves as a walking stick to
lean on, could be seized as a stick to beat with, in the next act."

### The gem and the pebble
"A man is hastily walking along a jungle path. Suddenly his foot strikes
against a stone. 'Oh, it is so painful!' He kicks the stone with a
curse. A few more steps, and another stone trips him. This time it is
even more painful. He turns round, quietly, picks up the stone, cleans
it carefully, looking around, wraps it up in his handkerchief and slips
it into his pocket. Both were stones. But why this special treatment?
The first one was a mere pebble, but the second one turned out to be a
gem!"

### The dumb show / mime
"He opens a non-existing door, sits on a non-existing chair, and so on."
"An actor rides a no-bike, climbs a no-hill, meets a no-friend and has
a no-chat with him. Or else he may sit on a no-chair by a no-table and
writes a no-letter with a no-pen."
"When the basis of the factors of the form group is removed, those in
the name group are reduced to purposeless activations."

### The anthill (Vammikasutta)
"This body has been compared to an anthill in the Vammikasutta. This
bodily frame, made up of the four elements, procreated by parents and
built up with food and drink, is metaphorically conceived as an anthill.
The discourse says: 'Take the knife, oh wise one, and dig in.' The world
has the obsession that there is a real cobra of a self inside this
anthill. But once it is dug up, what does one find? One discovers an
arahant, who has realized selflessness, a selfless cobra, worthy of
honour."

## SECTION 3: WHAT KEEPS THE MECHANISM RUNNING

### Grasping in the middle
"It is the grasp in the middle that accounts for the cord. For him, it
is extremely difficult to let go. Until a Buddha arises in the world and
proclaims the Dhamma, the world stubbornly refuses to let go."
"One hangs on, because one is afraid to let go. One thinks that to let
go is to get destroyed."

### The three fetters gone at stream-entry
"Personality view, doubt and dogmatic adherence to rules and observances,
are gone. An unwinding has occurred to some extent. The strands of the
cord are less taut now."

### Saṃvega (anguish / sense of urgency)
"Both these words, tragic and comic, can be accommodated within the
highly significant term samvega, anguish, sense of urgency."
"The ordinary worldling sees only the tragic side of the drama of
existence, and that because of his ignorance. But the arahant sees in
this drama of existence a comic side as well."
"This spontaneous smile seems to be the outcome of an insight into the
comic aspect of this existential drama."
"'What a pity that we are subject to such a state of affairs! What a
pity that we do not understand it because of the power of influxes and
latencies and thereby heap up defilements!'"

### The Adhimutta verses (the four)
1. "There is no mental pain to one with no expectations. All fears have
   been transcended by one whose fetters are extinct."
2. "It does not occur to me 'I was', nor does it occur to me 'I will be'.
   Mere preparations get destroyed. What is there to lament?"
3. "To one who sees as it is, the arising of pure dhammas and the
   sequence of pure preparations, there is no fear."
4. "When one sees with wisdom, this world as comparable to grass and
   twigs, not finding anything worthwhile holding on as mine, one does
   not grieve: 'O! I have nothing!'"

### Pure phenomena (sudda dhamma)
"Why are the phenomena qualified by the word pure? Because the
mind-objects, which are generally regarded as dhamma by the world, are
impure. Why? Because they are 'influenced' by influxes. Now here we
have 'uninfluenced' or influx-free phenomena."
"To the arahant's mind the objects of the world occur free of influxes.
That is to say, they do not go to build up a prepared. They are
quasi-preparations. They do not go to build up a film show."
"The film reel is just being played — that is the way it occurs to him."

### The butterfly / no-bike dumb show
"When the basis of the factors of the form group is removed, those in
the name group are reduced to purposeless activations. He feels as if
he is performing a drama with non-existing objects."
"Seeing the reciprocal relationship between name-and-form, he is
disinclined to dabble in concepts or gulp down a dose of prescriptions.
If one sits down to draw up lists of concepts and prescribe them, it
would only lead to a mental constipation. Instead of release there will
be entanglement."

### The fan (dependent on grasping is becoming)
"What I am now holding in my hand has become a fan because I am using
it as a fan. Even if it is made out of some other material, it will
still be called a fan. But if it were used for some other purpose, it
could become something else."
"Becoming is something that goes on from moment to moment."

### Gold and silver are born
"How can gold and silver be born? How can they grow old? They are born
because of craving, conceit and views. They come into existence. They
are born. Because of birth, they grow old."
"All these assets, monks, are of the nature to be born." (Analayo: MĀ
204 does not mention birth.)

## SECTION 4: WHAT THE MECHANISM BUILDS

### The worldling takes concepts seriously
"They are actually comparable to things temporarily improvised to depict
a particular scene in a drama or a film show. That is why we compared
the four elements to ghosts."

### Deposits become aggregates of grasping
"It is such 'deposits' that become our aggregates of grasping, or
'assets', which we take along with us in this samsara in the form of
likes and dislikes. Loves and hates contracted in the past largely
decide our behaviour in the present."

## SECTION 5: HOW TO SEE THROUGH IT

### The monodrama (four postures)
"When walking, he understands: 'I am walking'; when standing, he
understands: 'I am standing'; when sitting, he understands: 'I am
sitting'; when lying down, he understands: 'I am lying down'. While
keeping one's postures in this manner, one sees in outline one's own
form as if one were acting in a monodrama."
"He gets an opportunity to witness a monodrama, free of charge."

### Letting go before Mara comes
"He can let go before Mara comes to grab. This is the idea expressed in
the first verse [Adhimutta]."

### The immediate understanding
"If one gives up the tendency to grasp, at least for a short while by
developing the noble eightfold path at its supramundane level, and lets
go even for one moment, then one understands as one grasps again that
now there is less stress and tension."

## SECTION 6: WHAT REMAINS

### What remains when the cord unwinds
"One realizes, 'as-it-is', that no real cord existed at all."
"The cessation of existence is experienced then and there, dittheva
dhamme."

### Adhimutta's verse 2: nothing to lament
"It does not occur to me 'I was', nor does it occur to me 'I will be'.
Mere preparations get destroyed. What is there to lament?"

## REFUTATIONS

### The nihilism charge
"Even some Buddhist scholars are scared of the term bhavanirodha,
fearing that it leads to a nihilistic interpretation... The main reason
behind this is the misconception regarding existence — that there is an
existence in truth and fact."
"Before the advent of the Buddha, the world conceived of existence in
terms of a perdurable essence as 'being'. So the idea of destroying
that essence of being was regarded as annihilationism."
"According to the law of dependent arising, existence is something that
depends on grasping. It is due to grasping that there comes to be an
existence. This is the pivotal point."

### The sassata definition
"They sometimes make use of the word sassata in defining Nibbana. This
is a word that should never be brought in to explain the term Nibbana."

### The "purpose" question (revisited)
"What is the use of a well, if water is there all the time? Once the
thirst is quenched forever, why should one go in search of a well?"

## PASS 2 FINDS

- The rope/cord simile is the most complete mechanical explanation of
  dependent origination in the series — the two winders, the grasping
  in the middle, the twang. This is the visual grammar for the twelve-
  link machine.
- The sewing machine simile is the arahant's mind in one image — the
  shuttle runs out of cotton, the knots stop forming.
- The gem and the pebble — both stones, different treatment. This is
  the dependent origination of value in one parable.
- The Adhimutta verses are the most direct teaching on fearlessness in
  the series. The bandit chief context makes them visceral.
- The dhuva/sassata refutation is a specific exegetical move — Nanananda
  shows how the commentators corrupted a synonym of nibbana.
- The dumb show / mime is the practice method — the monodrama of four
  postures where the form group fades and the name group becomes
  purposeless activations.

## PASS 3: HOLES AND CONNECTIONS

- **Hole:** The saṃvega teaching (tragic + comic = anguish) is introduced
  but not fully worked through as a practice. That's the contemplation
  on death and impermanence, which connects to the maranasati practices.
- **Hole:** The relationship between the rope/cord and the twelve links
  is established but the mapping is not made explicit (which strand is
  which link?).
- **Connection to Sermon 7:** The anidassana vinnana analysis is continued.
  The Brahmanimantanika passage is the anchor.
- **Connection to Sermon 6:** The cinema simile is continued and deepened.
  The cardboard houses, the puppet show, the stool/footstool — all extend
  the movie metaphor.
- **Connection to Sermon 5:** The theme verse is mapped again, now through
  the Adhimutta verses and the rope/cord.
- **Connection to VBT pack:** The monodrama (four postures) maps to the
  VBT body-awareness practices. The rope/cord maps to the gap practice —
  the grasping in the middle is the craving that fills the gap.
