# SERMON 32 — EXCAVATION

Source: Nibbana-32.pdf (BCBS/Dharma Seed, free). 8719 words.

---

## SECTION 1: WHAT NIBBANA IS

### The path concentration (Mahācattārīsaka)
"That unification of mind equipped with these seven factors is called
noble right concentration with its supports and requisites" — right
view, intention, speech, action, livelihood, effort, mindfulness.
"So right concentration itself is the path. The singular number is
used to denote the fact that it is accompanied by the requisite
factors... a unification of all the forces built up by the
participative factors of enlightenment."
Analayo parallel: Ñāṇamoli (1995: 934); MĀ 189; Anālayo 2010, JCBS
8: 59–93.

### Cessation of existence is Nibbana
"The cessation of existence is Nibbāna, bhavanirodho nibbānaṃ."

### The path as highest prepared thing
"Monks, whatever prepared things there are, the noble eightfold path is
called the highest among them."
"It is by means of this prepared noble eightfold path that the Buddha
clears the path to the unprepared. This is an extremely subtle truth,
which only a Buddha can discover and proclaim to the world."
Analayo parallel: Bodhi (2012: 422) (Aggappasāda); EĀ 21.1.

## SECTION 2: HOW THE MECHANISM WORKS

### The five faculties completed (recap with precision)
Faith: "the firm faith characteristic of the stream-winner."
Energy: "that element of energy weathered and reinforced by its
fourfold application as a base for success."
Concentration: "defined as the first four jhānas, based on which one
can develop insight and attain Nibbāna... a noble disciple gains
concentration... having made the release of Nibbāna its object. The
term vossagga connotes Nibbāna as a giving up or relinquishment."
Wisdom: "pertaining to the understanding of the four noble truths...
the 'noble penetrative wisdom of rise and fall', udayatthagāminī
paññā ariyā nibbedhikā."

### The mindfulness faculty as balancer
"It maintains the balance between the two sets of pair-wise faculties,
by equalizing faith with wisdom and energy with concentration."
"Now in the context of the four foundations of mindfulness, the role of
mindfulness is the simple task of being aware of the appropriate
object... But here in this domain of faculties, mindfulness has
attained lordship."

### The rock balancing (the danger of the last heave)
"If excessive energy is applied in that last heave, one would be thrown
off head over heels after the rock. If insufficient energy is applied
the rock would roll back and one would get crushed. That is why a
balancing is needed before the last spurt. Right endeavour has to be
preceded by a balancing."

### The Caṅkī formula (strive, balance, endeavour)
"Having aroused a desire or keen interest, he strives; having strived,
he balances; having balanced or equalized, he endeavours; and with that
endeavour he realizes the highest truth by his body and penetrates into
it with wisdom."
Analayo parallel: Ñāṇamoli (1995: 782) (Caṅkīsutta).

### The log of wood (finding the centre of gravity)
"Without lifting up a log of wood and putting one's shoulder to it, one
cannot get to know the art of balancing. If the log of wood is thick at
one end and thin at the other end, one cannot locate the centre of
gravity at a glance. So one puts one's shoulder to one end and goes on
lifting it up. It is when one reaches the centre of gravity that one is
able to balance it on one's shoulder and take it away."

### Enduring effort (ussa ussoḷhi, maraṇasati)
"Ussahati is suggestive of enduring effort with which one bears up...
the Buddha uses the term ussoḷhī to designate that steadily enduring
effort."
"There are in me unabandoned evil unskilful states which could spell
danger to me if I die today... then... that monk should arouse a high
degree of desire, effort, striving, enduring effort, unremitting
effort, mindfulness and full awareness."
Analayo parallel: Bodhi (2012: 1222) (maraṇasati, AN Eights).

### The gabled hall (wisdom faculty as roof peak)
"Just as, monks, in a gabled hall, so long as the roof peak has not
been raised, the rafters are not conjoined, the rafters are not held in
place, even so, as long as the noble knowledge has not arisen in a
noble disciple, the four faculties are not conjoined, the four faculties
are not held in place."
"The five faculties are to be found only in the eight noble persons...
In others, they are weak... In the worldling they are not at all to be
found, in any way, sabbena sabbaṃ sabbathā sabbaṃ n'atthi."
Analayo parallel: Bodhi (2000: 1222, 1675) (Mallikāsutta);
Spk III 236 (lokuttara-only reading).

### Five powers: faculties enthroned
"Faith, energy, mindfulness, concentration and wisdom were elevated to
the position of a king or lord. They have attained sovereignty. So now
they are exercising their power. For what purpose? To put down the
evil unskilful mental states that rise in revolt against Nibbāna."

### Seven enlightenment factors: the marshalled squad
"The constituents are: sati, mindfulness; dhammavicaya, investigation
of states; viriya, energy; pīti, joy; passaddhi, calmness; samādhi,
concentration; upekkhā, equanimity."
"Sati leads the way and at the same time marshals the squad. Three
members of the group, namely dhammavicaya, viriya and pīti are by
nature restless, while the other three, passaddhi, samādhi and
upekkhā are rather slack. They have to be marshalled and properly
aligned, and sati comes to the forefront for that purpose."

### Any meditation subject develops them
"When one develops a particular meditation subject, whether it be
mindfulness of breathing, ānāpānasati, or even one of the four divine
abidings... one can arouse these enlightenment factors."
"A monk develops the enlightenment factor of mindfulness imbued with
loving kindness, based upon seclusion, dispassion and cessation,
maturing in release."
Analayo parallel: Bodhi (2000: 1609); SĀ 744.

### The bojjhaṅga sequence
Chain (paraphrase) with source phrases: "dwelling thus mindfully, he
investigates that mental state with wisdom" → "energy is stirred up and
not inert" → "there arises a joy of the spiritual type" → body calms,
mind concentrated → "he rightly looks on with equanimity at the mind
thus concentrated".
"Once the mind is concentrated, there is no need to struggle or
strive. With equanimity one has to keep watch and ward over it."
"It is the proper basis for the knowledge of things as they are,
yathābhūtañāṇa. The neutrality that goes with equanimity not only
stabilizes concentration, but also makes one receptive to the knowledge
of things as they are."
Analayo parallel: Bodhi (2000: 1783–1784) (Ānandasutta); SĀ 810.

### Right view twofold
"There is right view, monks, that is affected by influxes, on the side
of merit and maturing into assets, and there is right view, monks, that
is noble, influx-free, supramundane, a factor of the path."
"The ten postulates: 'There is an effectiveness in what is given, what
is offered and what is sacrificed, there is fruit and result of good
and bad deeds, there is this world and the other world, there is
mother and father, there are beings who are reborn spontaneously...
recluses and Brahmins who have realized by themselves by direct
knowledge...'"
"The path right view: 'that wisdom, that faculty of wisdom, that power
of wisdom, that investigation of states enlightenment factor, that path
factor of right view in one whose mind is noble...'"
"Four qualifiers: 'right view which is based upon seclusion,
dispassion and cessation, maturing in release.'"
Analayo parallel: Ñāṇamoli (1995: 935); Bodhi (2000: 1524); SĀ 768.

### The path at the moment of arising
Speech, action and livelihood at the path moment: "It is the very
thought of abstaining that represents the three factors at the path
moment and not their physical counterparts. That is to say, the act of
refraining has already been accomplished."
Three circling factors — right view, effort, mindfulness — "running
around and circling around" (anuparidhāvanti, anuparivattanti).
The VIP escort simile: "One ushers him in with his vanguard, another
brings up the rear with his bandwagon while yet another is at hand as
the bodyguard-cum-attendant. So also at the path moment right view
shows the way, right effort gives the boost, while right mindfulness
attends at hand."

### The ten-factor chain
"Therein, monks, right view comes first... In one of right view, right
intention arises...
right speech... right action... right livelihood... right effort...
right mindfulness... right concentration... right knowledge... right
deliverance arises. Thus, monks, the disciple in higher training
possessed of eight factors becomes an arahant when possessed of the ten
factors."
Analayo parallel: Ñāṇamoli (1995: 935); MĀ 189.

### The seed similes (wrong view / right view)
Wrong: "In the case of a person with wrong view... all those mental
states conduce to unwelcome, unpleasant, unwholesome, disagreeable and
painful consequences. Why is that? The view, monks, is evil."
"Just as, monks, in the case of a margosa seed or a bitter gourd seed,
or a long gourd seed thrown on wet ground, whatever taste of the earth
it draws in, whatever taste of the water it draws in, all that conduces
to bitterness, to sourness, to unpleasantness. Why is that? The seed,
monks, is bad."
Right: "...all those mental states conduce to welcome, pleasant,
wholesome, agreeable and happy consequences. Why is that? The view,
monks, is good."
"Just as, monks, in the case of a sugar cane seedling or a sweet paddy
seed, or a grape seed thrown on wet ground, whatever taste of the earth
it draws in, whatever taste of the water it draws in, all that conduces
to sweetness, agreeableness and deliciousness. Why is that? The seed,
monks, is excellent."
Analayo parallel: Bodhi (2012: 118); EĀ 17.5, 17.6.

### View precedence = personality transformation
"View has to come first in any total transformation of personality...
A view gives rise to thoughts, thoughts issue in words, words lead to
actions, and actions mould a livelihood."

### The middle path's true depth
"It is not such a piecemeal solution... Instead of grafting half of one
extreme to half of the other, the Buddha rejected the wrong views
behind both those pursuits."
"One inclines to the pursuit of sense pleasures by taking one's stance
on the annihilationist view... It inculcates a nihilistic outlook."
"Where the nihilist found an excuse for indulgence in sensuality by
negating, the Buddha applied a corrective by asserting."
"Beings have kamma as their own, they are inheritors of kamma, kamma is
their matrix, kamma is their relative."
"The right view that takes kamma as one's own, kammasakatā sammā
diṭṭhi, on the other hand is oriented towards arising, samudaya."
"This noble influx-free right view... is oriented towards cessation,
nirodha."
"It is this orientation towards Nibbāna that paves the way for the
signless, animitta, the undirected, appaṇihita, and the void,
suññata."

### The Ākāsā winds simile (path contains all)
"Just as, monks, various winds blow in the sky, easterly winds,
westerly winds... so too, when a monk develops and cultivates the
noble eightfold path, for him the four foundations of
mindfulness go to fulfilment by development, the four right efforts...
the four bases... the five faculties... the five powers... the seven
factors of enlightenment..."

### The MahāSaḷāyatanika setup (suffering through the six bases)
"Not knowing and not seeing the eye as it actually is... one gets
lustfully attached to the eye, to forms, to eye-consciousness, to
eye-contact... the five aggregates of grasping get accumulated for the
future and his craving, which makes for re-becoming... increases, his
bodily stresses increase, his mental stresses increase... and he
experiences bodily and mental suffering."
Analayo parallel: Ñāṇamoli (1995: 1137); SĀ 305. (Discussion of the
cessation half deferred to Sermon 33.)

## SECTION 3: WHAT KEEPS THE MECHANISM RUNNING

### The evil seed
"The view, monks, is evil... The seed, monks, is bad."

### The nihilistic excuse
"One inclines to the pursuit of sense pleasures by taking one's stance
on the annihilationist view... no rebirth... indulge in sense pleasures
unhindered by ethical considerations... a nihilistic outlook."

---

## SECTION 4: WHAT THE MECHANISM BUILDS

### The aggregates accumulated, the fevers rising
"For him, who is lustfully attached, fettered, infatuated,
contemplating gratification, the five aggregates of grasping get
accumulated for the future... his bodily stresses increase, his mental
stresses increase, his bodily torments... fevers increase, and he
experiences bodily and mental suffering."

### Assets ripening (the lower right view)
"That right view is on the side of merit... which mature into saṃsāric
assets, upadhivepakka, in the form of the conditions in life conducive
even to the attainment of Nibbāna."

## SECTION 5: HOW TO SEE THROUGH IT

### Balance before the last spurt
"Right endeavour has to be preceded by a balancing... one has to be
cautious and mindful."
"One has to put one's shoulder to it. By merely looking at the rock,
without trying to lift it up, one cannot say how much effort is needed
to topple it."

### Marshal the squad
"Sati leads the way and at the same time marshals the squad...
They have to be marshalled and properly aligned."

### Develop any subject
"Whether it be mindfulness of breathing... or even one of the four
divine abidings... one can arouse these enlightenment factors."

## SECTION 6: WHAT REMAINS

### The three doors open
"When the mind is weaned away from the habit of grasping signs, from
determining and from the notion of self-hood, the three doorways to
deliverance, the signless, the undirected and the void, would open up
for an exit from this saṃsāric cycle."

### Prepared clears the path to the unprepared
"Whatever is prepared is not worthwhile, and yet, it is by means of
this prepared noble eightfold path that the Buddha clears the path to
the unprepared."

## REFUTATIONS

### The commentary's tuleti
"It is interpreted as a reference to contemplation on insight,
aniccādivasena tīreti, 'adjudges as impermanent etc.'. But if we
examine the word within the context here, as it occurs between
ussahati, 'strives'... and padahati, 'endeavours', the obvious meaning
is 'equalizing' or 'balancing'."

### Middle path as 50/50 compromise
"The concept of a 'middle' might make one think that the noble
eightfold path is made up by borrowing fifty per cent from each of the
two extremes... But it is not such a piecemeal solution."

### The terror of suññatā
"Perhaps, while listening to them, some might have got scared at the
thought 'so then there is not even a mother or a father'. That is why
the word suññatā, voidness, drives terror into those who do not
understand it properly."
