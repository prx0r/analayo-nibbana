# SERMON 24 — EXCAVATION

Source: Nibbana-24.pdf (BCBS/Dharma Seed, free). 8316 words.

---

## SECTION 1: WHAT NIBBANA IS

### The unprepared element (asaṅkhata dhātu)
"It is this state of the cessation of the world that is known as
asaṅkhata dhātu, or the 'unprepared element'. That unprepared state,
described in discourses on Nibbāna in such terms as atthi, bhikkhave,
ajātaṃ abhūtaṃ akataṃ asaṃkataṃ, 'monks, there is an unborn, an
unbecome, an unmade, an unprepared', is this cessation of the six
spheres of sense, which is the end of that prepared world."

### Rohitassa took the world with him
"He took 'the world' along with him in his journey to see its end.
Since this six-based body with its perceptions and mind is itself the
world, he was taking the world with him in his exploration. That is
why he had to die on the way without seeing the end of the world."
"He made another blunder. He was going in search of a place where there
is no death, in order to escape death... Rohitassa took Māra along with
him in his journey to find a place where there is no death."

### The unestablished sphere (Udāna, first nibbāna discourse)
"In that particular state, described as a 'sphere'... I say, there is
neither a coming, nor a going, nor a standing, nor a passing away, nor
a being reborn; that state which is unestablished, non-continuing and
objectless, is itself the end of suffering."
Analayo parallel: Ireland (1990: 108).

### The chain from attachment to the end (Udāna, fourth)
"To the attached there is wavering, to the unattached there is no
wavering; wavering not being, there is calm; calm being, there is no
inclination; inclination not being, there is no coming and going;
coming and going not being, there is no passing away or reappearing;
when there is no passing away or reappearing, there is neither a
'here', nor a 'there', nor anything between the two — this is the end
of suffering."
Analayo parallel: Ireland (1990: 110).

### The deathless here and now
"By giving up the five aggregates one is freed from Māra, is liberated
from death and attains the deathless state. That is why we said that
the arahant has attained the deathless state, here and now, in this
world itself."

## SECTION 2: HOW THE MECHANISM WORKS

### Māra defined (Rādhasaṃyutta)
"Where there is form, Rādha, there would be a Māra, or one who kills,
or one who dies. Therefore, Rādha, in this context you look upon form
as 'Māra', as 'one who kills', as 'one who dies', as a disease, as a
boil, as a dart, as a misery, as a wretchedness. They that look upon
thus are those that see rightly."
"As in the case of form, so also in regard to feeling, perception,
preparations and consciousness... each of the five aggregates is looked
upon as a Māra."
Analayo parallel: SĀ 120 ("contemplated as being completely made by
Māra").

### Grasping invites Māra (Bhadrāvudha, Pārāyana)
"Yaṃ yaṃ hi lokasmiṃ upādiyanti, ten' eva Māro anveti jantuṃ —
'Whatever thing they grasp in this world, by that itself Māra pursues
a man.'"
"Because of grasping, there is becoming or existence and with it birth,
decay and death, etc., follow suit, all due to craving."
Analayo parallel: Bodhi (2017: 341).

### Māra's boast and the Buddha's reply (Kassakasutta)
Māra: "Mine, O recluse, is the eye, mine are the forms and mine the
sphere of eye-contact, where will you, recluse, go to escape me?...
Mine, O recluse is the mind, mine are the mind-objects and mine the
sphere of mind-contact..."
Buddha: "Yours, O Evil One, is the eye... but where there is no eye,
no forms and no sphere of eye-contact, there you cannot go, Evil One."
"From the Buddha's reprisal to Māra's challenge, we can well infer that
there indeed is a place to which Māra has no access. That is none other
than the cessation of the six sense-spheres."
Analayo parallel: SĀ 246 (Māra calls the spheres "my vehicle").

### The dyad (Dvayamasutta, second of Saḷāyatanavagga)
"Dependent on a dyad, monks, consciousness comes to be... Depending on
eye and forms arises eye-consciousness. Eye is impermanent, changing,
becoming otherwise. Forms are impermanent, changing, becoming
otherwise... How can eye-consciousness, arisen in dependence on an
impermanent condition, be permanent, monks?"
"That concurrence, that meeting, that togetherness of these three
things, monks, is called eye-contact. Even the eye-contact is
impermanent... Contacted, monks, one feels, contacted one intends,
contacted one perceives. Thus these things, too, are unstable,
evanescent, impermanent, changing and becoming otherwise."
"The entire discourse vibrates with the tone of impermanence."
"It is not founded on stable existing things, but on what is
impermanent, unstable and changing... Its foundation is always
crumbling, changing and transforming."
Analayo parallel: SĀ 214, SĀ 213.

### The movie simile (Gaddulasutta)
"Monks, have you seen a picture called a movie?... That picture called
a movie is something thought out by the mind. But the thought itself,
monks, is even more picturesque than that picture."
"The modern day movie has a way of concealing impermanence by the
rapidity of projections of the series of pictures on the screen. The
rapidity itself gives an impression of permanence, which is a
perversion, vipallāsa. The movie is enjoyable because of this
perversion."
"Due to the perception of permanence, there is a grasping of signs, and
in the wake of it influxes flow in, giving rise to proliferation...
That is how one enjoys a film show."
"Upamā pi na sukarā yāva lahuparivattaṃ cittaṃ — 'it is not easy even
to give a simile to show how rapidly thought changes'."
"He puns on the word citta, 'thought' or 'picture'... when he asserts
that thought is more picturesque, cittatara, than the picture."
Analayo parallel: SĀ 267 (Chinese reads caraṇa bird; original likely
"picture").

### Parāmasana vs sammasana (two kinds of reflection)
"Where there is a perception of permanence, the tendency is to grasp
the object tenaciously and hang on to it. This pervert tendency is
known as parāmasana. It is impelled by the triple proliferations of
craving, conceits and views."
"On the contrary, the perception of impermanence fosters a detached and
observant attitude in reflection, which is known as sammasana... It
attenuates the prolific tendencies to craving, conceits and views.
This kind of reflection is the harbinger of insight."

### The power of the impermanence perception (Khandhasaṃyutta)
"The perception of impermanence, monks, when developed and intensively
practised, extirpates all sensual lust, extirpates all lust for forms,
extirpates all lust for existence, extirpates all ignorance and
extirpates and eradicates the conceit 'am'."
Analayo parallel: SĀ 270.

## SECTION 3: WHAT KEEPS THE MECHANISM RUNNING

### The perception of permanence in the impermanent
"There is a malignantly pervert idea, ingrained in saṃsāric beings,
known as the perception of permanence in the impermanent, anicce
niccasaññā, which prevents them from seeing the inherent transience of
eye and forms. That is how the six spheres of sense create a world
before us."

### Sign-grasping and the influxes
"The tendency to grasp a sign in regard to the objects of the six
senses is the result of the perception of permanence. Due to the
perception of permanence, there is a grasping of signs, and due to that
grasping of signs, influxes flow in. Proliferations through craving,
conceits and views get heaped up. This is how our world is
constructed."
"Na nimittaggāhi nānuvyañjanaggāhī — 'he doesn't grasp a sign nor does
he dwell on its details'."

### Prolific reckonings (papañcasaññāsaṅkhā)
"One is carried away by prolific perceptions, papañcasaññā, and is
kept under the sway of worldly concepts and designations born of
prolific perceptions, papañcasaññāsaṅkhā."
"Proliferation creates things, giving rise to the antinomian conflict.
Duality masquerades behind it."
"The glue of craving and intoxicating influxes create a facade of a
real world before him. That is the world we touch with our hands and
see with our eyes."

## SECTION 4: WHAT THE MECHANISM BUILDS

### The world as insubstantial construction
"The Dvayamsutta brings to light the fact that all these four
[diṭṭha, suta, muta, viññāta — things seen, heard, sensed and
cognized] are insubstantial and coreless."
"All this exposes the insubstantial nature of this world."
"The entire world is structured on these vibrant, transient and
evanescent basic elements."

### Consciousness not a soul
"Consciousness is not something substantial and absolute, like the
so-called soul. That is precisely the point of divergence for Buddhism,
when compared with those religious systems which rely on soul
theories."
"Everyone of these consciousnesses is based on a dyad... Even when we
come to mind-consciousness, the theme is the same, manañca paṭicca
dhamme ca."

## SECTION 5: HOW TO SEE THROUGH IT

### Impermanence to the signless = cessation of the world
"If someone developed the contemplation of impermanence to the highest
degree and brought his mind to the signless state, having started from
the sign itself, it goes without saying that he has realized the
cessation of the world. That is, the experience of Nibbāna. It is, at
the same time, the cessation of proliferation, papañcanirodha."
"The contemplation of impermanence that leads to the signless
concentration is helpful in freeing the mind from these signs."

### The Kāḷakārāma declaration (knowing without stance)
"There is an extremely important discourse connected with the idea of
the void, suññatāpaṭisaṃyutta, which echoes this epistemological
crisis, in the section of the Fours in the Aṅguttara Nikāya, entitled
Kāḷakārāmasutta." (Same voidness-discourse category as Sermon 22.)
"This Kāḷakārāmasutta was preached by the Buddha to the congregation of
monks at the Kāḷaka monastery in the city of Sāketa."
Historical background: "Cūḷasubhaddhā, daughter of Anāthapiṇḍika... a
stream-winner, was given in marriage to the son of the millionaire
Kāḷaka of Sāketa, a devout follower of Nigaṇṭha Nātaputta."
"Cūḷasubhaddhā managed to convert him by inviting the Buddha to Sāketa
and getting Kāḷaka to listen to the Dhamma." (Named people: Cūḷa-
subhaddhā the converting daughter; Kāḷaka the converted millionaire.)
"All that is known to the Tathāgata, but the Tathāgata has not taken
his stand upon it."
"Thus, monks, a Tathāgata does not imagine a visible thing as apart
from seeing, he does not imagine an unseen, he does not imagine a thing
worth seeing, he does not imagine a seer." (Same for heard, sensed,
cognized.)
"It is precisely because he has understood that he takes no stance.
But the worldlings are of the opinion that knowledge of a thing
entitles one to assert it dogmatically."
Analayo parallel: Bodhi (2012: 411).

### Bahiya connection (the radical seen)
"In the seen there will be just the seen, in the heard there will be
just the heard, in the sensed there will be just the sensed, in the
cognized there will be just the cognized."
"In common parlance, the word 'seen' connotes something seen. But here
we have something more radical, avoiding substantialist insinuations."
"He does not project an agent into the phenomenon by taking seriously
the subject-object relationship."

### Two grades of knowledge (Mūlapariyāya)
Low: "He perceives earth as 'earth'. Having perceived earth as 'earth',
he imagines 'earth' as such, he imagines 'on the earth', he imagines
'from the earth', he imagines 'earth is mine', he delights in earth."
(Untaught ordinary person — sañjānāti.)
High: "He understands through higher knowledge earth as 'earth'...
he does not imagine earth to be 'earth', he does not imagine 'on the
earth'... he does not delight in earth." (Tathāgata — abhijānāti.)
"He imposes the grammatical superstructure on it."
Analayo parallel: Ñāṇamoli (1995: 83, 87).

## SECTION 6: WHAT REMAINS

### Tādī — the steadfast such
"Thus, monks, the Tathāgata, being such in regard to all phenomena,
seen, heard, sensed and cognized, is 'such'. Moreover than he who is
such there is none other higher or more excellent, I declare."
"The term tādī... stands for the quality of steadfastness of the
arahant in remaining unshaken by the eight worldly vicissitudes. His
mainstay, in this respect, is atammayatā, or non-identification. He is
such because he does not grasp any of those things as 'mine'."

### The closing couplet
"Whatever is seen, heard, sensed, or clung to and esteemed as truth by
other folk, midst those who are entrenched in their own views, being
such, I hold none as true or false."
"This barb I beheld well in advance, whereon mankind is hooked
impaled, I know, I see, 'tis verily so, no such clinging for the
Tathāgatas."
Analayo parallel: Bodhi (2012: 411).

### The non-prolific state inaccessible to Māra
"That itself is the non-prolific state. All concepts of 'going',
'coming', 'being born', 'growing old' and 'dying', are to be found in
the prolific. They simply do not exist in the non-prolific. That is
why it is inaccessible to Māra."

## REFUTATIONS

### The commentary's omniscience reading of Kāḷakārāma
"The commentary has it that the earth shook at five points in the
discourse... the three significant terms jānāmi, abbhaññāsiṃ and
viditaṃ, 'I know', 'I have fully understood', all that is 'known' to
the Tathāgata represent a plane of omniscience, sabbaññutabhūmi,
peculiar to a Buddha."
Nāṇananda's reading: "It is precisely because of this full
understanding that he has not taken his stand upon it... As a matter of
fact, it is precisely because he has understood that he takes no
stance." (The stancelessness is the fruit of understanding, not a
metaphysical plane.)

### The caraṇa bird misreading
"The simile is not our own, but only a modernization of a canonical
simile used by the Buddha himself... The original would presumably have
had a reference to caraṇa, which was then in some way misunderstood to
refer to a bird."
Analayo parallel: SĀ 267.

### Variant readings in Kāḷakārāma
"As in the case of many other deep discourses, here too we are faced
with the problem of variant readings. Even the commentator is at a loss
to conclude and editors go their own way. We have to wade through the
variant readings to make some sense out of the discourse."

### The soul-theory divergence
"Consciousness is not something substantial and absolute, like the
so-called soul. That is precisely the point of divergence for
Buddhism."

## PASS 2 FINDS

- **Miss 1:** The suññatāpaṭisaṃyutta categorization of Kāḷakārāma
  (voidness discourses — the Sermon 22 category recurs) plus the
  Cūḷasubhaddhā/Kāḷaka historical background including Anāthapiṇḍika
  as her father — added to Section 5.
- **Miss 2:** abbhaññāsiṃ spelling in the commentary's three-terms
  list (had single b) — corrected in Refutations.
- **Miss 3:** Voice line trimmed "But...monks," out of the movie pun —
  restored to exact wording.
- Māra's boast and the Buddha's "there you cannot go" reply
  (Kassakasutta) is a complete dialogue and the most dramatic passage
  in the sermon — extracted in full, with SĀ 246 where Māra calls the
  spheres "my vehicle."
- The Gaddulasutta "picture called a movie" is a canonical movie
  simile — Nāṇananda explicitly says the modern motion-picture version
  is his modernization of a canonical simile. The citta/cittatara pun
  and the "no fitting simile for rapid thought" line attach to it.
- Parāmasana vs sammasana is a technical pair (tenacious clinging vs
  detached observant reflection) — added to Section 2; it is the
  psychological mechanism of the signless path.
- The Kāḷakārāma historical background (Cūḷasubhaddhā, Kāḷaka the
  Nigaṇṭha follower, the five hundred monks, Asoka's Mahārakkhita to
  the Yonakas, Kālabuddharakkhita's all-night sermon) — kept in
  Evidence/Pass 2 rather than a scaffold section.
- Pass 1 initially under-weighted the sañjānāti/abhijānāti distinction
  from Mūlapariyāya — it is the key to the Kāḷakārāma paradox
  (higher understanding → no stance), promoted to Section 5.
- The four negations (I do not know / I both know and do not know /
  I neither know nor am ignorant) as forced choices the Tathāgata
  refuses — the tetralemma structure again.

## PASS 3: HOLES AND CONNECTIONS

- **Hole:** The exact method for "bringing the mind to the signless
  state, having started from the sign itself" is asserted, not taught —
  the method lives in the signless-concentration passages of Sermon 15
  territory (satipaṭṭhāna + impermanence). Note for the scaffold
  Section 5.
- **Hole:** The relation between the Kāḷakārāma "no stance" and the
  earlier tetralemma refusals is drawn but not fully worked — the
  sermon says "bears some resemblance... but there seems to be a
  difference" and leaves it there.
- **Connection to Sermon 23:** Direct continuation — the six-sphere
  cessation, papnca end, and Mahākoṭṭhita statement recur; Sermon 24
  adds Māra's jurisdiction and the dyad impermanence.
- **Connection to Sermon 20/21:** The tetralemma returns a third time
  (Kāḷakārāma's five statements).
- **Connection to Sermon 22:** The "five aggregates as murderer" is
  here "five aggregates as Māra" — same insight, different sutta
  (SĀ 120 vs SĀ 104).
- **Connection to VBT pack:** The movie simile IS the VBT practice of
  seeing the sign as a sign — rapidity concealing flux, verifiable in
  any flickering perception. Parāmasana/sammasana names the practice
  shift directly.
- **Analayo note:** SĀ 267's caraṇa-bird reading shows the Chinese
  preserving a different textual state — the "picture" reading is the
  one Nāṇananda argues for.
