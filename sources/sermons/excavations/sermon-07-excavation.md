# SERMON 7 — EXCAVATION

Source: Nibbana-07.pdf (BCBS/Dharma Seed, free). 10704 words.

---

## SECTION 1: WHAT NIBBANA IS

### The Kevaddha verse (full, with variants)
"Viññāṇaṃ anidassanaṃ, anantaṃ sabbato pabhaṃ, ettha āpo ca paṭhavī,
tejo vāyo na gādhati, ettha dīghañca rassañca, aṇuṃ thūlaṃ subhāsubhaṃ,
ettha nāmañca rūpañca, asesaṃ uparujjhati, viññāṇassa nirodhena,
etth'etaṃ uparujjhati."
Walshe: "Where consciousness is signless, boundless, all-luminous —
that's where earth, water, fire and air find no footing. There both long
and short, small and great, fair and foul — there 'name-and-form' are
wholly destroyed. With the cessation of consciousness this is all destroyed."

### The textual variant problem (pahaṃ vs pabhaṃ)
Ceylonese and PTS editions: pahaṃ. PED: "flight of steps from which to
step down into the water." Rhys Davids: "accessible from every side."
Neuman: "consciousness that has vanished and completely been dropped
off." Norman 1987: "an earlier version had sabbato-paha... the commentarial
tradition came into being at a time when the spelling –paha had not yet
replaced –pabha."
Sanskrit fragment (Zhou 2008): pṛthuṃ supports pahaṃ. Tibetan: khyab
supports pahaṃ. Chinese DĀ: 有光 supports pabhaṃ. Ratnāvalī: no
luminosity.

### Nanananda's reading: anidassana = non-manifestative
"Nidassana means 'illustration'. Something that 'throws light on' or
'makes clear' is called nidassana. Anidassana could therefore be said to
refer to whatever does not manifest anything."

### The sky simile (Kakacūpamasutta)
"Suppose a man comes with crimson, turmeric, indigo or carmine and says:
'I shall draw pictures and make pictures appear on the sky!'"
"This sky, Lord, is immaterial and non-illustrative. It is not easy to
draw a picture there or make manifest pictures there."
MĀ 193 parallel. "The sky is said to be arūpī anidassano, immaterial
and non-illustrative."

### The mirror simile (paccavekkhana)
"Our consciousness has in it the ability to reflect. That ability is
called paccavekkhana, 'looking back'. The Buddha has given the simile
of the mirror with reference to this ability, as for instance in the
AmbalatthikāRāhulovādasutta."
"The word stands for a mode of becoming deluded as well as a mode of
getting rid of the delusion."
"What creates a delusion is the way that dog is repeatedly looking down
from his own point of view on the plank to see a dog in the water. That
is unwise reflection born of non-radical attention."
"Wise reflection born of radical attention, yoniso manasikāra, is what
is recommended."

### The arahant's consciousness does not reflect name-and-form
"Viññāṇaṃ anidassanaṃ is a reference to the nature of the released
consciousness of an arahant. It does not reflect anything. To be more
precise, it does not reflect a nāma-rūpa, or name-and-form."
"An ordinary individual sees a nāma-rūpa, when he reflects, which he
calls 'I' and 'mine'. It is like the reflection of that dog."
"The arahant's consciousness is an unestablished consciousness... It is
a sort of penetration of name-and-form, without getting entangled in it."

### The plumb-line (gādhati)
"When, for instance, we want to plumb the depth of a deep well, we
lower something material as a plumb into the well. Where it comes to
stay, we take as the bottom. In the consciousness of the arahant, the
material elements cannot find such a footing. They cannot manifest
themselves in that unplumbed depth of the arahant's consciousness."

### Ananta = endless (transcending relative concepts)
"All measurements are relative to some scale or other. They are
meaningful within some framework of a scale. In this sense, too, the
worldling's way of thinking has a tendency to go to extremes... The
arahant transcends it, his consciousness is, therefore, endless, ananta."
Udana verse: "Hard to see is the Endless, not easy 'tis to see the
truth, pierced through is craving, and naught for him who knows and
sees."

### Anantagocara = infinite range, trackless
Dhammapada: "He who has no craving, with nets and agglutinates to lead
him somewhere — by what track could that Awakened One of infinite range
be led — trackless as he is?"
"The very fact that there is 'no object' makes the Buddha's range
endless and untraceable. Had there been an object, craving could have
netted him in."

### The birds in the sky
"Those who have no accumulation and who have comprehended their food...
whose range is the deliverance of the void and the signless... their
track is hard to trace, like that of birds in the sky."
"The word gati... only refers to their mental compass, which is
untraceable, because of their deliverance through the void and the
signless."

### Sabbato pabhaṃ = lustrous on all sides
"In the case of a matinee show, dark curtains and closed doors and
windows ensure the necessary dark background... Suddenly doors and
windows are flung open and the dark curtains are removed. Then immediately
one slips out of the cinema world. The film may go on, but because of
the light coming from all sides, the limited illumination on the screen
fades away, before the total illumination."
"Consciousness is as narrow as that beam of light falling on the cinema
screen... Now what happens when it is fully illuminated on all sides
with wisdom? It becomes sabbato pabhaṃ, lustrous on all sides. In that
lustre, which comes from all sides, the framework of ignorance fades
away."

### The four lustres (AN)
"The lustre of the moon, the lustre of the sun, the lustre of fire, and
the lustre of wisdom. This, monks, is the highest among these four
lustres, namely the lustre of wisdom."

### The luminous mind (pabhassara citta)
"This mind, monks, is luminous, but it is defiled by extraneous
defilements. That, the uninstructed ordinary man does not understand as
it is... This mind, monks, is luminous, but it is released from
extraneous defilements. That, the instructed noble disciple understands
as it is."
Karunaratne: "what is meant by lustrous and pure mind is not a state of
mind which is absolutely pure... pure only in the sense, and to the
extent, that it is not disturbed or influenced by external stimuli."
Shih Ru-nien: "unlike the Mahāyāna theory of tathāgatagarbha... statements
in the Pali texts only emphasize the knowledge of the innate purity of
the mind as a prerequisite step... the tranquil, luminous, and pliable
mind is just the basis for further practice."

### The theme verse mapped to the cinema
"Sabbasankharasamatha, the stilling of all preparations — the
preparations which go to make it a 'movie' film are 'stilled'. The
multicoloured dresses of actors and actresses become colourless before
that illumination."
"Sabbupadhipatinissagga — the bundle of experiences coming out of one's
'vested-interests' in the marvellous cinema world. These assets are
relinquished at least for the moment."
"Tanhakkhayo — destruction of craving is momentarily experienced with
regard to the blurred scenes on the screen."
"Nirodha — the film has now ceased to be a film, though the machines are
still active."
"Nibbana — whatever heated emotions and delirious excitements that arose
out of the film show cooled down, at least momentarily."

## SECTION 2: HOW THE MECHANISM WORKS

### Uparujjhati vs nirujjhati (held in check vs ceased)
"In this restatement of the riddle, the Buddha has purposely avoided the
use of the verb nirujjhati. Instead, he had recourse to such terms as
na gādhati, 'does not find a footing', and uparujjhati, 'is held in
check'."
"The monk's riddle used nirujjhanti. The Buddha's restatement uses
uparujjhati. This is evidence enough to infer that there is a subtle
distinction."
"The Cūḷaniddesa... paraphrases uparujjhati by nirujjhati: uparujjhatī'ti
nirujjhati."
Bhikkhu Anandajoti: "nirujjhati is passive, and uparujjhati is active so
there is a slight change of tone."

### The materialist fallacy
"To say that the four elements cease somewhere in the world, or in the
universe, is a contradiction in terms. Why? Because the very question:
'Where do they cease?', presupposes an answer in terms of those elements,
by way of defining that place."

### The four elements as ghosts
"The four great primaries haunt the minds of the worldlings like ghosts,
so they have to be exorcised from their minds. It is not a question of
expelling them from this world, or from any heavenly realm... That
exorcism should take place in this very consciousness."
"Before the light of wisdom those ghosts, namely the four great primaries,
become ineffective. It is in the darkness of ignorance that these ghosts
haunt the worldlings with the perception of form."

### The Brahma Baka passage (Brahmanimantanika, MN 49)
"Consciousness which makes nothing manifest, infinite and all lustrous,
it does not partake of the earthiness of earth, the wateriness of water,
the fieriness of fire, the airiness of air, the creature-hood of
creatures, the deva-hood of devas, the Pajāpati-hood of Pajāpati, the
Brahma-hood of Brahma, the radiance of the Radiant Ones, the Subhakiṇha-
hood of the Subhakiṇha Brahmas, the Vehapphala-hood of the Vehapphala
Brahmas, the overlord-ship of the overlord, and the all-ness of the all."
"To dispel a wrong view that Baka the Brahma conceived, in regarding
his Brahma status as permanent, ever lasting and eternal, that the
Buddha made this declaration before that Brahma himself in the Brahma
world."

## SECTION 3: WHAT KEEPS THE MECHANISM RUNNING

### The specifically prepared consciousness
"The specifically prepared consciousness, or the consciousness crammed
up in name-and-form, as in the case of the non-arahant. It is as narrow
as the perspective of the audience glued to the screen."
"The consciousness of the ordinary worldling is likewise limited and
committed."

### The mirror as delusion and liberation
"What creates a delusion is the way that dog is repeatedly looking down
from his own point of view on the plank to see a dog in the water. That
is unwise reflection born of non-radical attention, ayoniso manasikāra."

## SECTION 4: WHAT THE MECHANISM BUILDS

### Consciousness reflects name-and-form as self
"An ordinary individual sees a nāma-rūpa, when he reflects, which he
calls 'I' and 'mine'."
"A non-arahant, upon reflection, sees name-and-form, which however he
mistakes to be his self."

### The cinema beam = ordinary consciousness
"Consciousness is as narrow as that beam of light falling on the cinema
screen. That is to say, the specifically prepared consciousness."

## SECTION 5: HOW TO SEE THROUGH IT

### Yoniso manasikara (radical attention)
"Wise reflection born of radical attention, yoniso manasikāra, is what
is recommended in the AmbalatthikāRāhulovādasutta with its thematic
repetitive phrase paccavekkhitvā, paccavekkhitvā, 'reflecting again and
again'."
"Wise reflection inculcates the Dhamma point of view. Reflection based
on right view, sammā diṭṭhi, leads to deliverance."

### Wisdom illuminates consciousness
"Wisdom is to be developed, consciousness is to be comprehended."
Analayo: "Chinese and Tibetan parallels do not have such a statement."
"When it is fully illuminated on all sides with wisdom, it becomes
sabbato pabhaṃ, lustrous on all sides."

---

## SECTION 6: WHAT REMAINS

### The cessation of the specifically prepared consciousness
"The reference here is to that abhisankhata vinnana, or the specifically
prepared consciousness. It is the cessation of that concocted type of
consciousness which was formerly there."
"With the cessation of that specifically prepared consciousness, all
constituents of name-and-form are said to be held in check."

### The final rendering
"Consciousness, which is non-manifestative, endless, lustrous on all
sides — here it is that earth and water, fire and air no footing find.
Here it is that long and short, fine and coarse, pleasant, unpleasant,
and name-and-form, are cut off without exception. When consciousness has
surceased, these are held in check herein."

## REFUTATIONS

### The commentarial interpretation (Buddhaghosa)
"Nāma is said to be the name of the person and rūpa is his form or
shape. All this goes to show that the commentator has gone off at a
tangent."
"Viññāṇan'ti... viññāṇaṃ nibbānassa nāmaṃ — the word consciousness is
in this context a synonym for Nibbāna. This forced etymology is far
from convincing."
"Sabbato pabhā means 'with fords on all sides'... Nibbāna is supposed
to be metaphorically conceived as the ocean, to get down into which
there are fords on all sides, namely the thirty-eight topics of
meditation. This interpretation seems rather farfetched."
"In his commentary to the Majjhima Nikāya he seems to have had second
thoughts... here he takes it in the sense of not being visible to the
eye."
"In this interpretation Nibbāna is even regarded as something white in
colour!"

### The luminous mind is NOT tathāgatagarbha
"Unlike the Mahāyāna theory of tathāgatagarbha... statements in the
Pali texts only emphasize the knowledge of the innate purity of the
mind as a prerequisite step... the restoration of the purity of the
mind is not the end of religious practices."

### Brahmali's caution
"Brahmali argues that 'due to the qualifiers ananta and pabhā, anidassana
vinnana is described in a way that resembles the description of certain
states of samādhi. It seems that ananta is never used with mind or
consciousness apart from describing states of samādhi.'"

## PASS 2 FINDS

- The pahaṃ/pabham textual variant is the most detailed comparative
  analysis in the series so far — Sanskrit, Tibetan, Chinese, multiple
  editions, Norman's 1987 article, Bhikkhu Anandajoti's comment.
- The Brahma Baka passage (MN 49) — thirteen concepts the arahant's
  consciousness does not partake of. This is the fullest description of
  the signless consciousness in the Pali canon.
- The luminous mind (pabhassara citta) — carefully distinguished from
  Mahāyāna tathāgatagarbha. Shih Ru-nien and Karunaratne quotes are
  scholarly anchors.
- The uparujjhati vs nirujjhati distinction — "held in check" vs
  "ceased." The Buddha deliberately changed the verb in reformulating
  the monk's question.
- The commentary critique is the most extensive so far — Buddhaghosa's
  forced etymology, the ford interpretation, the white colour, the
  second thoughts in the MN commentary.
- The theme verse mapped to the cinema — every term of the theme verse
  gets a cinema interpretation. This is the complete bridge between the
  opening formula and the movie simile.

## PASS 3: HOLES AND CONNECTIONS

- **Hole:** The relationship between the luminous mind (pabhassara) and
  the signless consciousness (anidassana) is established but not fully
  worked through. Are they the same? Different aspects? Nanananda seems
  to treat them as related but distinct.
- **Hole:** Brahmali's caution (ananta + pabha resemble samādhi states)
  is noted but not resolved. This is a genuine scholarly disagreement.
- **Connection to Sermon 6:** The cinema simile is continued and completed.
  The theme verse is fully mapped to the cinema for the first time.
- **Connection to Sermon 5:** The three deliverances (signless, desireless,
  void) are the result of the light that ends the movie.
- **Connection to Sermons 8-9:** The Brahmanimantanika (MN 49) passage is
  the setup for Sermon 8, which continues the analysis of anidassana
  vinnana.
- **Connection to VBT pack:** The signless consciousness is the target
  of the sound-dissolution practice. The luminous mind is what remains
  when the signs stop.
