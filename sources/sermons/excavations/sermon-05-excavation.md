# SERMON 5 — EXCAVATION

Source: Nibbana-05.pdf (BCBS/Dharma Seed, free). 8032 words.

---

## SECTION 1: WHAT NIBBANA IS

### Nibbana = cessation of existence = extinction of influxes
"Bhavanirodho Nibbanaṃ — cessation of existence is Nibbana. It comes
about by this extinction of influxes. The very existence of 'existence'
is especially due to the flowing in of influxes of existence."
"What is called 'existence' is not the apparent process of existing
visible to others. It is something that pertains to one's own mental
continuum."

### The arahant is free from the conjoined pairs
Attakavagga: "He neither grasps nor gives up." "There is nothing taken
up or rejected by him." "He is neither attached to attachment, nor
attached to detachment." "He is neither attached nor detached."
Khajjaniyasutta: "Such a monk is called one who neither amasses nor
diminishes; already diminished as he is, he neither gives up nor grasps;
already given up as he is, he neither disbands nor binds together;
already disbanded as he is, he neither exorcizes nor proficiates."

### The snake slough (Uragasutta)
"That monk forsakes the hither and the tither, like a snake its slough
that doth wither." Refrain throughout all 17 verses.
"That monk who sees no essence in existence, like one seeking flowers
in Udumbara trees, will give up the hither as well as the thither."
"The arahant looks at the worldly usage in the same way as a snake would
turn back and look at the worn-out skin he has sloughed off. Sometimes
we see a snake moving about with a remnant of its slough hanging on.
We might even think that the snake is carrying its slough around. It is
the same in the case of the arahants."

### Sa-upadisesa explained (the sloughed skin still hangs on)
"Now there is this term sa-upadisesa Nibbana dhatu. Taking the term at
its face value, some might think that the clinging is not yet over for
the arahants... But we have to understand this in the light of the
simile of the worn-out skin. In the case of the arahant, too, the
sloughed off skin is still hanging on."
Sariputta: "I am harassed and repelled by this body, I am ashamed of it."
"This is because the body is for him something already abandoned."

### The Brahmin shore simile
"For whom there is neither a farther shore, nor a hither shore, nor
both, who is undistressed and unfettered, him I call a Brahmin."
"Our usual concept of an arahant is of one who has crossed over the
ocean of samsara and is standing on the other shore. But here is
something enigmatic."

## SECTION 2: HOW THE MECHANISM WORKS

### The four nutriments
"The concept of nutriment in this Dhamma is much broader than the
worldly concept of food... it includes the other three kinds of nutriment
as well, namely phassa, or contact, manosacetana, or volition, and
vinnana, or consciousness."

### The Samudayasutta: four foundations arising and ceasing
"What is the arising of the body? With the arising of nutriment is the
arising of the body and with the cessation of the nutriment is the going
down of the body." / "With the arising of contact is the arising of
feeling..." / "With the arising of name-and-form is the arising of the
mind..." / "With the arising of attention is the arising of mind-objects..."

### The asava (influxes) analysis
"In ordinary life, this word is used to denote fermentation or liquor
that has got fermented for a long time. If there is even a dreg of
ferment in a vessel, it is enough to cause fermentation for any suitable
raw material put into it. So also are the asavas. They are like the
residual dregs of the ebullient mass of defilements in beings, which
have undergone fermentation for a long, long time in samsara."
Three kinds: kamasava (sense-desire), bhavasava (existence), avijjasava
(ignorance). Sometimes a fourth: ditthasava (views), subsumable under
avijjasava.
"They are like certain intoxicating influences, which create a world of
sense-desires, a stupor that gives a notion of existence and leads to
ignorance."

### The MN 9 contradiction on asavas
"At one place in the sutta it is said that the arising of ignorance is
due to the arising of influxes and that the cessation of ignorance is
due to the cessation of influxes. But then, a little way off, in the
selfsame sutta, we read: with the arising of ignorance is the arising
of influxes. Apparently this contradicts the previous statement. The
preacher of this discourse, Venerable Sariputta, is not one who
contradicts himself. So most probably there is some deep reason behind
this."
Analayo parallel: "The Chinese and Sanskrit fragment parallels to MN 9
only relate the influxes to ignorance, they do not specify any cause for
ignorance (Anālayo 2011: 72)."

### The Gaddulasutta: the picture called a movie
"Monks, have you seen a picture called a movie (carana)? Yes, Lord.
Even that picture called a movie is something thought out by the mind.
But this mind, monks, is more picturesque than that picture called a
movie. Therefore, monks, you should reflect moment to moment on your
own mind with the thought: For a long time has this mind been defiled
by lust, hate, and delusion."
"Monks, I do not see any other class of beings as picturesque as beings
in the animal realm. But those beings in the animal realm, monks, are
also thought out by the mind. And the mind, monks, is far more
picturesque than those beings in the animal realm."

### The movie extended: the cinema simile
"Usually a film or a drama is shown at night. The reason for it is the
presence of darkness. This darkness helps to bring out the darkness of
ignorance that dwells in the minds of beings."
"One goes to the cinema saying: 'I am going to see a film show.' And
one returns saying: 'I have seen a film show.' But while the film show
is going on, one forgets that one is seeing a show. Such a strange spell
of delusion takes over. This is due to the intoxicating influence of
influxes."
"In the ancient Indian society, one of the primary senses of the word
sankhara was the make-up done by actors and actresses."
"So far not a single cinema has held a film show and not a single theatre
has staged a drama. And yet, those who had gone to the cinema and the
theatre had seen film shows and dramas. Now, how can that be?"
"A film show is produced, in the last analysis, by the spectator himself.
When he goes to the cinema, he takes with him the spices needed to
concoct a film or a drama, and that is: the influxes, or asavas.
Whatever technical defects and shortcomings there are in them, he makes
good with his influxes."
"Whatever preparations others may make, if the audience does not respond
with their own preparations along the same lines, the drama will not be
a success."

### The door flung open (the light that ends the show)
"Supposing the doors are suddenly flung open, while a vivid technicolour
scene is flashing on the screen, what happens then? The spectators will
find themselves suddenly thrown out of the cinema world they had created
for themselves. Why? Because the scene in technicolour has now lost its
colour. It has faded away. The result is dejection, disenchantment."
"The word raga has a nuance suggestive of colour, so viraga, dispassion,
can also literally mean a fading away or a decolouration. Here we have
a possible instance of nibbida viraga, disenchantment, dispassion, at
least in a limited sense."
"If the light of wisdom dawns on one's mind while watching a film show,
one would even wonder whether it is actually a film or a drama, while
others are enjoying it."

### Sankhara as make-up
"Sankhara generally means preparations. It is the make-up and the
make-believe which accounted for the delusion. The darkness of ignorance
provided the setting for it. If somehow or other, the light of wisdom
enters the scene, those preparations become no-preparations, visankhara,
and the prepared, sankhata, becomes a non-prepared, asankhata. There is
a subtle principle of un-doing involved in this."

### Dvayatanupassana on light and darkness
"Murk it is to those enveloped, as darkness unto the undiscerning, but
to the good wide open it is, as light is unto those discerning. So near,
and yet they know not, fools, unskilled in the Norm."

### Udana: the world appears real
"The world, enfettered to delusion, feigns a promising mien. The fool,
to his assets bound, sees only darkness around. It looks as though it
would last, but to him who sees there is naught."

## SECTION 3: WHAT KEEPS THE MECHANISM RUNNING

### The influxes create the world
"These influxes are often said to have the nature of infiltrating into
the mind... capable of creating intoxication again and again."
"It is because of the defiling influence of influxes, such as ignorance,
that the world around us appears so picturesque."
"Here one's personal attitude accounts much for the concepts of beauty,
ugliness, fierceness, and innocence of animals."

### Anusaya and nissaya (from Sermon 4, continued)
"Caught up in the dichotomy of samsaric existence, which alternates
between this-ness and otherwise-ness, one is unable to transcend it, so
long as there is attachment and clinging. Nissayas are the supports
that encourage clinging in the form of dogmatic adherence to views."

## SECTION 4: WHAT THE MECHANISM BUILDS

### The world is made by the spectator
"A film show is produced, in the last analysis, by the spectator himself."
"The life outside is not much different from what goes on within the
four walls of the cinema and the theatre. Just as, in the latter case,
an enjoyable story is created out of a multitude of scenes... even so,
according to the point of view of Dhamma, the lifestyle outside is
something made up and concocted."

### The darkness within is thicker than the darkness outside
"The darkness within is much thicker than the darkness outside. The
darkness outside may be dispelled even by a door flung open... But not
so easily the darkness within. That is why, in the psalms of the Theras
and Theris, it is said that they split or burst asunder the mass of
delusion, tamokkhandham padaliya."

## SECTION 5: HOW TO SEE THROUGH IT

### The razor simile (parāmasana vs sammasana)
"With the idea of sharpening the razor, if one grabs it tightly and
rubs it on the sharpening stone, it will only become blunt. Paramasana,
grasping, grabbing, is something like that."
"One has to hold the razor in a relaxed way, as if one is going to throw
it away. One holds it lightly, ready to let go of it at any time. But,
of course, with mindfulness. The wrist, also, is not rigid, but relaxed.
Hand is supple at the joints and easy to swing."
"First: up, up, up, then: down, down, down, and then: up down, up down,
up down. The third combined movement ensures that those parts of the
blade still untouched by the stone will also get duly sharpened."
"Inward, inward, inward — outward, outward, outward — inward outward,
inward outward. Or else: arising, arising, arising — ceasing, ceasing,
ceasing — arising ceasing, arising ceasing."
"It is in the same manner that the razor of insight wisdom has to be
whetted on the sharpening stone of the Satipatthanasutta."

### The four foundations: attending makes objects
"The fact that the mind-objects arise due to attention is noteworthy.
All the mind-objects mentioned in the fourth section of contemplation
arise when there is attention. And they go down when attention is not
there. In other words, attending makes objects out of them."

### The Upatissa/Assaji story (stream-entry on two lines)
"Formerly, as brahmin youths, they were known as Upatissa and Kolita.
These two young men once went to see a hill-top festival... Since by
then, their discerning wisdom was already matured, they suddenly
developed a dejection about the entertainment going on. The hill-top
festival, as it were, lost its festivity for them."
"When Upatissa met the Venerable Assaji Thera on his alms-round, he
begged the latter to preach the Dhamma. Assaji said: 'I know only a
little'. Upatissa also assured him: 'I need only a little'."
"Of things that proceed from a cause, their cause the Tathagata has
told, and also their cessation, thus teaches the great ascetic."
"Some interpret the word hetu, cause, in this verse, as avijjā. But
that is not the case. It refers to the basic principle known as
idappaccayata, the relatedness of this to that."
"It is said that the wandering ascetic Upatissa was able to arouse the
path of Stream-winning on hearing just the first two lines."

## SECTION 6: WHAT REMAINS

### The four realizations
"Monks, there are these four realizable things: things realizable through
the body, through memory, through the eye, and through wisdom."
"The things realizable through the body are the eight deliverances,
through memory are one's former habitations, through the eye are the
death and rebirth of beings, and what is realizable through wisdom, is
the extinction of influxes."
"That is why special mention is made of Nibbana as something realizable.
Because Nibbana is said to be something realizable, some are of the
opinion that nothing should be predicated about it. What is the reason
for this special emphasis on its realizability? It is to bring into
sharp relief the point of divergence, since the Buddha taught a way of
realizing here and now something that in other religions was considered
impossible."

### The certitude of no more birth
"By this extinction of influxes that one wins to the certitude that
there is no more birth after this. Khina jati, extinct is birth!"
"The term sacchikiriya implies a seeing with one's own eyes, as the
word for eye, aksi, is implicit in it."

## REFUTATIONS

### The asava/ignorance contradiction in MN 9
The sutta says both "arising of asavas → arising of ignorance" and
"arising of ignorance → arising of asavas." Nanananda: "Sariputta is
not one who contradicts himself. So most probably there is some deep
reason behind this." The movie simile resolves it: the spectator
creates the show with the asavas, and the show feeds the asavas.

### The carana interpretation
"Not a motion picture of the sort we have today. According to the
commentary, it is some kind of variegated painting done on a mobile
canvas-chamber." Nanananda extends it to the modern cinema by analogy.

### The sa-upadisesa face-value reading
"Taking the term at its face value, some might think that the clinging
is not yet over for the arahants." The slough simile resolves it: the
skin still hangs on but the snake is free of it.

## PASS 2 FINDS

- The razorsimile is the most practical instruction in the series so
  far — a concrete method for sammasana meditation.
- The cinema simile is the most extended metaphor in the series — it
  runs for about two pages and maps every element of dependent origination
  to the cinema experience.
- The asava/ignorance contradiction in MN 9 is resolved by the movie
  simile — the spectator creates the show with asavas, and the show feeds
  the asavas. Circular, like the whirlpool.
- The Upatissa/Assaji story — "I know only a little" / "I need only a
  little" — is one of the best exchanges in the Pali canon.
- The word sankhara as "make-up" — this is a specific etymological point
  that connects the technical term to everyday experience.
- The raga/viraga wordplay (colour/fading) — the door flung open, the
  technicolour scene losing its colour. This is the visual grammar for
  the dhamma-visual language.

## PASS 3: HOLES AND CONNECTIONS

- **Hole:** The asava/ignorance circularity is resolved by the movie
  simile but not fully worked through philosophically. The circular
  nature of asavas ↔ ignorance mirrors the whirlpool of consciousness ↔
  nama-rupa. This connection is implicit, not stated.
- **Hole:** The four realizations (body, memory, eye, wisdom) are
  listed but only the wisdom-realization (extinction of asavas) is
  developed. The other three are mentioned in passing.
- **Connection to Sermon 4:** The nissaya/parāmasana/sammasana framework
  continues the Satipatthana thematic paragraph. The razor simile IS
  the practice method for the four modes (internal/external/both,
  arising/dissolving/both).
- **Connection to Sermon 3:** The Gaddulasutta "picture called a movie"
  is the same insight as the Cetasutta's "intention, construction,
  latency" — the mind creates its world from these three.
- **Connection to Sermons 7-8:** The anidassana vinnana is the "light"
  that ends the movie. The door flung open is the first glimpse of the
  signless.
- **Connection to VBT pack:** The razor simile maps to the practice of
  holding attention lightly — the VBT "mind between objects" practice.
  The cinema simile maps to the "body as consciousness" practice — the
  spectator is the body of consciousness watching its own show.
- **Analayo note:** The MN 9 asava/ignorance contradiction — the Chinese
  parallels do NOT have the "asavas arise from ignorance" direction.
  Only the Pali has both. This is a significant comparative finding.
