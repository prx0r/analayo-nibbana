# SERMON 13 — EXCAVATION

Source: Nibbana-13.pdf (BCBS/Dharma Seed, free). 8368 words.

---

## SECTION 1: WHAT NIBBANA IS

### Nibbana in the mula-pariyaya list (last of 24)
"Nibbana is counted as the last of the twenty-four concepts. The
worldling is generally tempted to entertain some kind of manana,
or me-thinking, even regarding Nibbana. They indulge in prolific
conceptualisation on the basis of such conventional usages as 'in
Nibbana', 'from Nibbana', 'on reaching Nibbana' and 'my Nibbana'."

### Nibbana is a designation, not an absolute
"The word Nibbana is essentially a verbal noun... Nibbana is therefore
some sort of reckoning, an appellation or designation."
"So then, Nibbana is not an absolute, paramattha? It is not a paramattha
in the sense of an absolute. It is a paramattha only in the sense that
it is the highest good, parama attha."

### The Tathagata does not delight in Nibbana
"He does not delight in Nibbana. Why so? Because he knows that delighting
is the root of suffering, and from becoming comes birth and to the one
become there is decay-and-death."
"Nandi is a form of grasping, upadana, impelled by craving."

### Dvayatanupassana: nibbana is unfalsifying
"In whatever way they imagine, thereby it turns otherwise, that itself
is the falsity of this puerile deceptive thing. Nibbana is unfalsifying
in its nature."
"Nibbana is called animitta, or the signless, precisely because there
is no-thing in it. Because it is signless, it is unestablished. Only
where there is an establishment can there be a dislodgement."

## SECTION 2: HOW THE MECHANISM WORKS

### The three attitudes (mula-pariyaya, full)
1. Untaught person: perceives earth as 'earth', imagines, delights.
2. Monk in higher training: understands through higher knowledge, does
   not imagine, does not delight. "Because it should be comprehended by
   him."
3. Arahant/Tathagata: understands through higher knowledge, does not
   imagine, does not delight. "Because it has been well comprehended
   by him."

### Maññanā (methinking) as grammar
"The grammatical structure invests words with life, as it were. This
mode of hypostasizing is revealed in the nouns and substantives implying
such notions as 'in it', 'by it' and 'from it'."
"The vocative case, he pathavi, 'hey earth', effectively illustrates
this hypostasizing character of grammar. It is even capable of infusing
life into the concept of 'earth'."

### The tiger resurrection (grammar's feat)
"The structure of grammar seems to be capable of a similar feat [to the
three magicians] — reconstituting and resurrecting a tiger from its
skeletal remains."

### Perception ripens into language
"Monks, I say that perception has linguistic usage as its result. In
whatever way one perceives, so one speaks out about it."
Analayo parallel: MĀ 111. "Following on perception there is
verbalization."

### Maññanā as Mara's bondage (Yavakalapi)
"Imagining, monks, one is bound by Mara, not imagining one is freed
from the Evil One."
"'Am', 'this am I', 'I shall be', 'I shall not be', 'I shall be one
with form', 'I shall be formless' — all are imaginings."
"Imagining is a disease, imagining is an abscess, imagining is a barb."
Analayo parallel: SĀ 1168.

### The four qualifiers of imaginings
"Iñjitaṃ (agitation, from ejā = craving), phanditam (palpitation, the
fickle mind), papañcitam (proliferation), managatam (conceit — asmi
is the peg from which all measurements take their direction)."

### Vepacitti's fivefold bondage
"When Vepacitti thinks 'gods are righteous', he finds himself free.
But as soon as he slips into 'gods are unrighteous', he finds himself
bound again. So subtle is the bondage of Vepacitti. But more subtle
still is the bondage of Mara."

## SECTION 3: WHAT KEEPS THE MECHANISM RUNNING

### The house and tiles relativity
"One might think that a house is made up of tiles and bricks, but tiles
and bricks themselves come to be because of a house. There is a mutual
relationship between them."
"If one raises the question: 'What is a tile?', the answer will be: 'It
is an item used for building the roof of a house'."
"There is no need to get down to an atomistic analysis like nuclear
physicists. Wisdom is something that enables one to see this relativity
penetratively."

### The child's hut
"Little children sometimes build a little hut, out of fun, with a few
sticks and shady leaves. When the mother creeps into the improvised hut,
she does not seriously entertain the concept of 'a house' in it."
"To the Emancipated Ones, the sandcastles of adults appear no better
than the playthings of little children."

### The arahant uses convention
"That monk might still say 'I speak', he might also say 'they speak to
me', being skilful, knowing the world's parlance, he uses such terms
merely as a convention."

## SECTION 4: WHAT THE MECHANISM BUILDS

### The concept of a house
"Entering the house physically does not necessarily mean that one is
'in it'. Only if one has entered into the concept of a house is he 'in
it'."

### The skeleton of grammar
"If one imagines that the grammar of language must necessarily conform
to the grammar of nature, one falls into a grievous error."

---

## SECTION 5: HOW TO SEE THROUGH IT

### The scaffolding metaphor
"We should make use of the conceptual scaffolding only for the purpose
of putting up the building. As the building comes up, the scaffolding
has to leave. If one simply clings onto the scaffolding, the building
would never come up."

### The Sakka episode (Cula-Tanhasankhaya)
"Sakka came to see the Buddha... asked: 'How does a monk attain
deliverance by the complete destruction of craving?' The Buddha's reply:
'Nothing is worth entering into dogmatically.'"
"Moggallana shook the Vejayanti palace with the point of his toe. Since
Sakka had 'entered into' the palace with his craving, conceit and views,
he also was thoroughly shaken."
"The heavenly maidens entered their rooms. Sakka also had entered into
this Vejayanti palace of his own creation."

### The chemist's symbols
"Chemists make use of a certain system of symbolic formulas in their
laboratories, but back at home they revert to another set of symbols.
However, both are symbols. There is no need to discriminate between them
as higher or lower, so long as they serve the purpose at hand."

## SECTION 6: WHAT REMAINS

### The monks' displeasure
"The Exalted One said this, but those monks did not rejoice in the
words of the Exalted One."
"The declaration that none of the concepts, including that of Nibbana,
should be egoistically imagined, could have caused displeasure in monks,
then as now."

## REFUTATIONS

### The commentator on Nibbana in the mula-pariyaya
"The commentator takes much pains to prove that the Nibbana mentioned
at the end of the list refers not to our orthodox Nibbana, but to a
concept of Nibbana upheld by heretics. His attempt is at odds with the
trend of this discourse."

### The commentator on manati and abhinandi
"He treats it as a repetition and seeks for a justification... The
reason is that each of those concepts crystallized into a real thing
as a result of imagining, based on the framework of grammar."

### The commentators' relegation of the mula-pariyaya
"This particular discourse is distinguished from all other discourses
in respect of one significant feature: the concluding statement to the
effect that the monks who listened to the sermon were not pleased by it."

## PASS 2 FINDS

- The Yavakalapi (Vepacitti) parable is the most precise illustration
  of how imagination binds — the bondage depends on the thought. Change
  the thought, the bondage changes.
- The Sakka episode (Cula-Tanhasankhaya) is the most dramatic — Moggallana
  shakes the palace with his toe, and Sakka's craving-shaken palace falls
  apart. The palace is Sakka's own creation.
- The house/tiles relativity is a stunning philosophical point — tiles
  depend on houses as much as houses depend on tiles.
- The chemist's symbols — no discrimination between lab symbols and home
  symbols — is the Tathagata's use of worldly language.
- The monks' displeasure at the mula-pariyaya is the historical proof
  that this teaching is hard to accept.

## PASS 3: HOLES AND CONNECTIONS

- **Hole:** The relationship between the mula-pariyaya's 24 concepts and
  the practice method is established but not fully worked through. The
  satipatthana four modes (Sermon 4) is the practice.
- **Hole:** The arahant's use of convention ("I speak") is established
  but the practical implications for the meditator are only implied.
- **Connection to Sermon 12:** The milk simile is continued. The mula-
  pariyaya introduction (Sermon 12) is completed here.
- **Connection to Sermon 11:** The papanca analysis is extended through
  the Yavakalapi (imagination as Mara's bondage).
- **Connection to Sermon 10:** The mirror simile (self created by looking)
  is the same as the house concept (house created by entering into the
  concept).
- **Connection to VBT pack:** The scaffolding metaphor is the VBT practice
  — use the concepts, don't cling to them. The Sakka palace is the
  signless practice — the palace falls when the craving-shake comes.
