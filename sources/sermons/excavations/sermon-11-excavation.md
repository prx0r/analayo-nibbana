# SERMON 11 — EXCAVATION

Source: Nibbana-11.pdf (BCBS/Dharma Seed, free). 8425 words.

---

## SECTION 1: WHAT NIBBANA IS

### The four negations (Kalahavivada verse 13)
"Not percipient through perception, not percipient through disturbed
perception, not altogether without perception, not percipient of what
has vanished: form vanishes for one who has so attained, for concepts
due to proliferation are based on perception."
Analayo: "Not percipient of a perception, nor with perception of form,
neither impercipient, nor with active perception, one gives up all
perceptions, without attachment, because perception is the root of the
drama that follows dukkha."
"These four negations insinuate a strange supramundane level of
perception. It is an attempt to analyse the crux of the Dhamma in terms
of perception."

### The vacant gaze (the magic show)
"How would a man with radical attention and penetrative wisdom, who is
fully aware of the tricks of the magician, watch a magic show? He is
simply looking on with a vacant gaze."
"That gaze is 'endless', ananta, in the sense that it does not have the
magic show as its object. It goes beyond. It is also 'nonmanifestative',
anidassana, since the magic show does not manifest itself."
"The person referred to is not one with the ordinary worldling's
perception, nor has he fainted, nor is he in a trance, nor has he put an
end to perception. What these four negations highlight is that vacant
gaze of the one who is emancipated through wisdom."

### The Upasiva flame (revisited)
"As flame flung on by force of wind, reaches its end, comes not within
reckoning, so the sage, released from name-and-form, reaches his end,
comes not within reckoning."
"There is no measure of one who has gone out. There is no means by which
they might speak of him. When all phenomena have been uprooted, all
pathways of speech are also uprooted."

## SECTION 2: HOW THE MECHANISM WORKS

### The Kalahavivada analysis (deepening contact)
"Contact is the cause of the two views of existence and non-existence."
"When form has vanished, contacts do not touch one."
"The verse alludes to a supramundane state far transcending the formless
or any supposed intermediate stage."

### The three magicians (the papañca mechanism)
"Three men, skilled in magic, came upon a scattered heap of bones of a
tiger. To display their skill, one of them converted the bones into a
complete skeleton, the second gave it flesh and blood, and the third
gave it life. The resurrected tiger devoured all three of them."
"The three phases of Mahakaccana's formula: impersonal (contact →
feeling), personal (one perceives → one reasons about), gross (one
turns into papañca)."

### Mahakaccana's exposition (Madhupindika / honey-ball)
"Dependent on the eye and forms, eye-consciousness arises. The meeting
of the three is contact. Because of contact, feeling. What one feels,
one perceives. What one perceives, one reasons about. What one reasons
about, one turns into papañca. What one turns into papañca, owing to
that papañcasaññāsankhā beset him."
"The formula begins on an impersonal note... From here onwards the
formula takes a different turn... What one feels, one perceives — the
active voice. The person behind it takes over."
"The comparison of this discourse to a honey-ball is understandable,
since it holds the secret of the latent tendencies towards dogmatic
views."

### Papañca etymology
"Pra + panc, and it conveys such meanings as 'spreading out', 'expansion',
'diffuseness' and 'manifoldness'."
"Papanca in linguistic usage has the insinuation of a certain degree of
delusion brought about by verbosity and circumlocution. But here the
term has a deeper philosophical dimension. Here it is not a case of
linguistic usage, but the behaviour of the mind as such, since it
concerns sense-perception."
"The fact that it follows in the wake of vitakka is suggestive of its
affinity to vicara, or discursive thought... When vitakka breaks loose
and runs riot, it creates a certain deluded state of mind, which is
papanca."

### Anuruddha's eight thoughts
"This Dhamma is for one who likes and delights in nippapanca and not
for one who likes and delights in papanca."
"Whatever thoughts I had on my own, going far beyond them the Lord
preached to me, the Buddha, who delights in nippapanca, preached
nippapanca to me."
Analayo parallel: MĀ 74.

## SECTION 3: WHAT KEEPS THE MECHANISM RUNNING

### The Madhupindika three-act play
Act 1: Dandapani's question and the Buddha's enigmatic reply.
Act 2: The Buddha's explanation to the monks — even more enigmatic.
Act 3: Mahakaccana's exposition.

### The Buddha's reply to Dandapani
"According to whatever doctrine, friend, one does not quarrel with
anyone in the world... such is my doctrine, friend, thus do I proclaim
it."
"Dandapani shook his head, wagged his tongue, raised his eyebrows into
a three-lined frown on his forehead and departed, leaning on his stick."

### The seven underlying tendencies (the Buddha's summary)
"From whatever source papañcasaññāsankhā beset a man, if, in regard to
that, there is nothing to be delighted in, asserted, or clung to, then
this itself is the end of the underlying tendencies to attachment, to
aversion, to views, to doubts, to conceit, to attachment towards
existence, and to ignorance."

## SECTION 4: WHAT THE MECHANISM BUILDS

### Papañca creates the world
"What one turns into papañca, owing to that papañcasaññāsankhā beset
him who directed his powers of sense-perception. They overwhelm him and
subjugate him in respect of forms cognizable by the eye belonging to
the past, the future and the present."

### The perception-of-form trap
"Before the advent of the Buddha the general belief was that, in order
to be free from form, one has to attain to the formless. But this kind
of approach is like the attempt of one who, having imagined a ghost in
the darkness of the night, runs away to escape it. He is simply taking
the fantasy of the ghost with him."
"Perception of form is already implicit in the formless. What has been
done is only a pushing away of the perception of form with the help of
sankharas. It is merely a suppression of form through the power of
absorption."

## SECTION 5: HOW TO SEE THROUGH IT

### Nippapanca as the practice
"This Dhamma is for one who delights in nippapanca."
"Anuruddha attained Arahant-hood after the Buddha gave him the eighth
thought: delight in non-proliferation."

### The magic show viewer
"The man with discernment is watching with a vacant gaze."
"The audience in general reacts to it with gaping mouths and exclamations.
But the man with radical attention and penetrative wisdom... is simply
looking on."

## SECTION 6: WHAT REMAINS

### The Upasiva flame (final)
"When all phenomena have been uprooted, all pathways of speech are also
uprooted."

### The truly wise sage
"Knowing that they are dependent on speculative views, the sage with
discernment, emancipated as he is through understanding, does not enter
into disputes. A truly wise man does not fall back either on existence
or on non-existence."

## REFUTATIONS

### The commentary on the Kalahavivada verse
"The Mahaniddesa interprets the verse as referring to one who is on the
path to formless realms. However, considerations of context and
presentation would lead to a different conclusion. The extraordinary
state alluded to by this verse seems to be a supramundane one."

### The commentary on Mahakaccana's formula
"The commentary ignores the significance of the personal ending and
interprets the sensory process periphrastically... It has left out of
account the significance of the use of the active voice in this section
of the formula."

## PASS 2 FINDS

- The three magicians parable is the complete mechanism of papañca —
  bones → skeleton → flesh → tiger. Three phases of perception. The
  tiger devours all three. This is the visual grammar for the vortex.
- The Madhupindika three-act structure is the dramatic form of the
  teaching — Dandapani asks, Buddha cryptically replies, Mahakaccana
  unpacks. The honey-ball name is the reward for seeing through it.
- The four negations verse is the most precise description of the
  signless consciousness in the series — not normal perception, not
  disturbed, not non-percipient, not ended.
- The vacant gaze — watching the magic show without being fooled — is
  the practice image for the signless.
- The papañca etymology (pra + panc = spreading out) connects the
  linguistic and philosophical senses.
- Anuruddha's eight thoughts — the eighth being nippapanca — is the
  specific trigger for his arahantship.

## PASS 3: HOLES AND CONNECTIONS

- **Hole:** The practice for nippapanca is established (delight in it)
  but the method is only implied. The satipatthana thematic paragraph
  (Sermon 4) is the method.
- **Hole:** The relationship between papañca and the VBT practices is
  not made explicit. The VBT gap practice IS nippapanca — resting in
  the space between proliferations.
- **Connection to Sermon 6:** The dog on the plank (looking creates the
  reflection) is the same as the three magicians (creating the tiger).
  Both show that the perceiver creates the perceived.
- **Connection to Sermon 8:** The rope/cord (grasping in the middle) is
  the mechanism; the three magicians (papañca as tiger-creation) is the
  result.
- **Connection to Sermon 10:** The mirror simile (self created by
  looking) is the same insight as the magic show (world created by
  watching).
- **Connection to VBT pack:** The vacant gaze IS the signless practice.
  The magic show viewer IS the observer-inquiry practice.
