## SECTION 1: WHAT NIBBANA IS

### Nibbana = nippapanca (one of the 33 epithets)
"Nippapanca is regarded as one of the epithets of Nibbana in a long
list of thirty-three. Like dukkhu pasama, quelling of suffering,
papanca vupasama, 'quelling of prolificity', is also recognized as an
epithet of Nibbana. It is also referred to as papancanirodha, 'cessation
of prolificity'."

### The Tathagatas are nippapanca
"No track is there in the air, and no recluse elsewhere. This populace
delights in prolificity, but 'Thus-gone-ones' are non-prolific."
"The Tathagatas are free from papanca, that is to say they are
nippapanca, whereas the world delights in papanca."

### The Udana verse
"And at that time the Exalted One was seated contemplating his own
abandonment of reckonings born of prolific perception."
"To whom there are no proliferations and standstills, who has gone
beyond the bond and the deadlock, in that craving-free sage, as he
fares along, the world with its gods sees nothing to decry."

## SECTION 2: HOW THE MECHANISM WORKS

### Papañca vs pamada (the errand to Colombo)
"Suppose we ask someone to go on an urgent errand to Colombo. If
instead of going to Colombo, he goes to the nearest tavern and gets
drunk and sleeps there — that is a case of pamada. If, on the other
hand, he takes to a long labyrinthine road, avoiding the shortest cut
to Colombo, and finally reaches Kandy instead of Colombo — that is
papanca."
"Pamada has a nuance of inactivity or inefficiency, due to intoxication.
Papanca has an etymological background suggestive of expansion, elaboration.
It implies an inability to reach the goal due to a deviation from the
correct path."

### The AN couplet
"The fool who indulges in papanca, being excessively fond of it, has
missed the way to Nibbana, the incomparable freedom from bondage. He
who, having given up papanca, delights in the path to nippapanca, is
well on the way to Nibbana."

### Sankha = reckoning (not part)
"Sankha, samanna and pannatti are more or less synonymous terms...
Sankha means 'reckoning' and samanna is 'appellation'."
"The term could thus be rendered as 'reckonings born of prolific
perceptions'."

### Perception grasps a sign
"To perceive is to grasp a sign of permanence in something. Perception
has the characteristic of grasping a sign. Just as a party going through
a forest would blaze a trail with an axe in order to find their way back
with the help of notches on the trees, so does perception catch a sign
in order to be able to recognize."
"The word sanna, used to denote perception as such, primarily means the
'sign', 'symbol', or 'mark', with which one recognizes."

### The milk simile (Potthapada)
"Just as from a cow comes milk, and from milk curds, and from curds
butter, and from butter ghee, and from ghee junket. But when it is milk,
it is not reckoned as curd or butter or ghee or junket, it is then
simply reckoned as milk."
Analayo parallel: DĀ 28.
"These three modes of personality correspond to the three planes of
existence, the sensuous, the form, and the formless."
"If someone gets a jhana and attains to a mental mode of personality.
He should not imagine that the formless mode of personality is already
latent in him."

### The Tathagata uses worldly language without grasping
"For all these are worldly apparitions, worldly expressions, worldly
usages, worldly designations, which the Tathagata makes use of without
tenacious grasping."
"The Buddha uses the language much in the same way as parents make use
of a child's homely prattle, for purpose of mediation."
"When an adult uses a child's language, he is not bound by it."

### The Niruttipathasutta
"The three periods of time should never be mixed up or confounded. What
is past is 'it was'. What is present is 'it is'. What is future is 'it
will be'."

### The Sangarakkhita nephew (the daydream)
"The pupil, while fanning his teacher, indulged in a papanca. In his
imagination, he disrobed, got married, had a child, and was coming in a
chariot with his wife and child to see his former teacher. The wife,
through carelessness, dropped the child and the chariot run away. So he
whipped his wife in a fit of anger, only to realize that he had dealt
a blow on his teacher's head with the fan still in his hand."

### The poet and the ruby
"An Indian poet once spotted a ruby, shining in the moon light, and
eagerly approached it, enchanted by it, only to find a blood red spittle
of beetle."

### The golden goose
"A certain goose used to lay a golden egg every day. Its owner, out of
excessive greed, thought of getting all the as yet ones. He killed the
goose and opened it up, only to come to grief. He had wrongly imagined
the future to be already existing in the present."

## SECTION 3: WHAT KEEPS THE MECHANISM RUNNING

### The mula-pariyaya (24 concepts, 4 persons)
"Earth, water, fire, air, beings, gods, Pajapati, Brahma... the seen,
the heard, the sensed, the cognised, unity, diversity, all, Nibbana."
The four persons: untaught ordinary person, monk in higher training,
arahant, Tathagata.

### The worldling's view (mula-pariyaya)
"He perceives earth as 'earth'. Having perceived earth as 'earth', he
imagines 'earth' as such, he imagines 'on the earth', he imagines 'from
the earth', he imagines 'earth is mine', he delights in earth. Why? I
say that it is because he has not fully comprehended it."

### The sekha's view
"He understands through higher knowledge earth as 'earth'. Let him not
imagine 'earth' as such... Why? It is because it should be well
comprehended by him."

### Maññati (methinks)
"Here it refers to an indulgence in a deluded mode of thinking under
the influence of craving, conceit and views."
"The word maññati, methinks. One usually methinks when a simile or a
metaphor occurs, as a figure of speech. But here it is something more
than that."

## SECTION 4: WHAT THE MECHANISM BUILDS

### Reckonings born of prolific perception
"One is overwhelmed by one's own reckonings and figurings out, under
the influence of prolific perceptions."

### The conceptual trap
"Milk, curd, butter and ghee are merely so many stages in a certain
process. The worldlings, however, have put them into watertight
compartments, by designating and circumscribing them. They are caught
up in the conceptual trap of their own making."

### The philosopher's dilemma
"When the philosophers started working out the logical relationship
between cause and effect, they tended to regard these two as totally
unrelated. Since milk becomes curd, either the two are totally different
from each other, or curd must already be latent in milk."

## SECTION 5: HOW TO SEE THROUGH IT

### The Sabhiya verse
"Having understood name-and-form, which is a product of prolificity,
and which is the root of all malady within and without, he is released
from bondage to the root of all maladies. That Such-like-one is truly
known as 'the one who has understood'."

### The Magandiya verse
"To one unattached to percepts no bonds exist, in one released through
wisdom no delusions persist. But they that cling to percepts and views
go about rambling in this world."

### The Puppha verse
"Monks, I do not dispute with the world, it is the world that is
disputing with me."

### Saccaka debate
"As for you, Aggivessana, drops of sweat have come down from your
forehead... But, Aggivessana, there is no sweat on my body now."
"Even in the midst of a hot debate the Buddha had no agitation because
he did not adhere to any views."

## REFUTATIONS

### The commentary on mā maññi
"The commentary simply glosses over with the words maññatī'ti maññi...
Its only explanation is that the sekha has already done away with
imagining in terms of views, though he still has imaginings through
craving and conceit. But this interpretation is not at all convincing.
Obviously enough the particle mā has a prohibitive sense here."

### The commentary on papañca as craving/conceit/views
"The commentarial exegesis on papanca in terms of craving, conceit and
views is to a great extent justifiable. However, what is particularly
significant about the term papanca is that it conveys the sense of
proliferation and complexity of thought."

## PASS 2 FINDS

- The milk simile (Potthapada) is the most precise explanation of how
  the three personality modes relate — milk, curd, butter, ghee are
  stages, not latent realities.
- The Sangarakkhita nephew daydream is the most vivid illustration of
  papañca in the series — marriage, child, chariot, anger, blow, all
  from fanning his teacher.
- The mā maññi commentary critique is a specific exegetical move —
  Nanananda shows the commentary missed the prohibitive sense.
- The mula-pariyaya introduction (24 concepts, 4 persons) sets up
  Sermon 13's full analysis.
- The errand to Colombo (pamada vs papanca) is the cleanest distinction
  between the two terms.
