# SERMON 3 — EXCAVATION

Source: Nibbana-03.pdf (BCBS/Dharma Seed, free). 9528 words.

---

## SECTION 1: WHAT NIBBANA IS

### Nibbana = going out (nissarana) vs samsara = going round (samsarana)
"The other day, with the help of the simile of a whirlpool, we attempted
an explanation of the terms samsara on the one hand, and Nibbana on the
other, that is to say 'going round', or samsarana, and 'going out', or
nissarana."

### Where has the whirlpool gone?
"If someone asks where the vortex or the whirlpool has gone, what sort
of answer can we give? It is the same difficulty that comes up in
answering the question: 'Where has the fire gone after it has gone out?'"
"We cannot say that they united, nor can we say that it went and hid
somewhere. Here we find ourselves in a queer situation. All we can say
in fairness to truth is that there had been a certain form of activity,
a certain state of unrest, due to certain causes and conditions. Because
of that activity that was going on there, it was possible to designate
it, to give it a name."

### Nibbana is beyond concepts
"It is in concepts that ignorance finds a camouflage. Being unaware of
it the world goes on amassing concepts and even expects to see them in
Nibbana. There are some who fondly hope to get a vision of their lists
of concepts when they realize Nibbana. But that wisdom penetrates through
even the concepts and that is why it is called udayatthagamini panna
ariya nibbedhika, 'the ariyan penetrative wisdom that sees the rise and
fall'."

### Sariputta on stream-entry: seeing dependent arising = seeing the Dhamma
"He who sees the law of dependent arising, sees the Dhamma; he who sees
the Dhamma, sees the law of dependent arising." (Chinese parallel:
CBETA T01 no. 26.)
"The quintessence of the Dhamma is in fact the law of dependent arising
itself."

### The six qualities tested against the accepted interpretation
"This Dhamma is well-preached, it can be seen here and now, it is
timeless, it invites one to come and see, it leads one on, it can be
realized by the wise each one by himself. Though we all have faith in
these qualities of the Dhamma, let us see whether the traditionally
accepted interpretation of paṭicca samuppāda is faithful to these
qualities, particularly to the two qualities sanditthiko and akaliko."

## SECTION 2: HOW THE MECHANISM WORKS

### The whirlpool mapped to the twelve links
"The ignorance in presuming that it is possible to go against the main
stream of the three signata — impermanence, suffering and not-self — is
the place of its origin. That heap of preparations impelled by ignorance,
which takes the current forward, may be regarded as sankharas. And where
the current in its progress clashes with the main stream to become a
whirlpool, that pushing forward against the main stream is vinnana or
consciousness. The outcome of the clash is nama-rupa, or name-and-form,
with its formal name and nominal form."

### The abyss = painful bodily feelings
"Monks, abyss is a synonym for painful bodily feelings." (Bodhi: "bottom-
less abyss.") The uninstructed worldling "sorrows, grieves, and laments;
he weeps and beats his breast and becomes distraught. This is called an
uninstructed worldling who has not risen up in the bottomless abyss, one
who has not gained a foothold." Chinese parallel: CBETA T02 no. 99.

### Salatana = the abyss; craving = the thirst to quell it
"The thirst of craving arises in beings in various forms of existence
because of painful feeling. The uninstructed worldling, on being touched
by painful feeling, delights in sense pleasures, because he knows no way
out of painful feeling other than the sense pleasures. The abyss is the
endless barrage of painful feelings. The force of attraction that arises
from the abyss is like the thirst to quell those painful feelings."

### The Dhamma-eye = seeing the solution in the problem
"What is called the 'Dhamma-eye', is the ability to see the Nibbanic
solution in the very vortex of the samsaric problem. That way of analysis
which puts samsara and Nibbana far apart, into two watertight
compartments, as it were, gives rise to interminable problems."

### Vipassī's insight (Mahapadana, DN 14): working BACKWARDS from the end
"His mode of approach was one of radical reflection, or yoniso manasikara,
literally: 'attention by way of the matrix'. One might as well say that
it is an attention by way of the vortex. It is as if a man with keen
vision, sitting under a tree by a river, were to watch how a fallen leaf
gets carried away by the water current, only to get whirled up and
disappear in a vortex."

"He started his reasoning from the very end of the paṭicca samuppāda
formula: 'Given what, does decay-and-death come to be, from which
condition comes decay-and-death?' ... Taking pair by pair, he went on
reasoning progressively. But when he came to consciousness, he had to
turn back. When he searched for the condition of consciousness, he found
that name-and-form itself is the condition."

"This consciousness turns back from name-and-form, it does not go beyond.
In so far can one be born, or grow old, or die, or pass away, or
reappear."

"His insight into paṭicca samuppāda was definitely not due to recollection
of past lives." (All versions agree.)

### The Dhamma-eye arising
"'Arising, arising', thus, O! monks, in regard to things unheard of
before, there arose in the bodhisatta Vipassi the eye, the knowledge,
the wisdom, the science, the light." (Analayo note: aloka = "clarity";
bodhi = "awakening".)

### Sariputta's two reeds simile (simpler than the whirlpool)
"Two bundles of reeds standing, one supporting the other. If one of those
is drawn out, the other would fall down. And if the latter is drawn out,
the former will fall down."
"Let us suppose that the consciousness bundle of reeds is standing on the
left side, and the name-and-form bundle is on the right. Then we have a
number of other bundles, such as the six sense-bases, contact and feeling,
all leaning on to the name-and-form bundle of reeds. Now, as soon as the
consciousness bundle is drawn out, all the others on the right side fall
down immediately. There is no interval."
"True to the qualities of the Dhamma — to be seen here and now, not
involving time, and inviting to come and see — the entire mass of samsaric
suffering ceases immediately."

### Why Vipassī doesn't mention avijja and sankhara
"The commentary raises the question, why the bodhisatta Vipassi makes no
mention of the first two links... and gives the explanation that he could
not see them, as they belong to the past. But this is not the reason. The
very ignorance regarding the relationship between name-and-form and
consciousness — is avijja. And what accounts for the continuity of this
relationship — is sankharas."

### The critique of the three-period interpretation
"According to the accepted interpretation, presented by the venerable
author of the Visuddhimagga, the first two links of the formula belong to
the past, and the last two links belong to the future. The remaining
eight links in the middle are taken to represent the present. That means,
we have here the three periods of time. So it is not — timeless."
"The accepted interpretation puts the first two links into the past...
That is to say, ignorance and preparations are referred to the past.
Birth, decay-and-death are referred to the future. The eight links in
between are explained with reference to the present."
"In the attempt to interpret the formula as referring to three stages in
the samsaric journey of an individual, additional links had to be interposed
to prop up the interpretation."
"This kind of interpretation is also advanced. But this interpretation in
terms of pentads violates the interrelatedness between the twelve links
in the formula."
"According to this accepted commentarial exegesis, even the term bhava,
or becoming, is given a twofold interpretation. As kamma-process-becoming
and rebirth-process-becoming... So the same term is explained in two ways.
Similarly, the term jati, which generally means birth, is said to imply
rebirth in the context of the formula of dependent arising."
"The purpose of the formula of dependent arising is to show the way of
arising and cessation of the entire mass of suffering, and not to
illustrate three stages in the samsaric journey of an individual."

### The banana-trunk simile (cutting through, not peeling)
"In order to ascertain whether a banana trunk is pith-less, it is not
necessary to go on removing its bark, layer after layer, from top to
bottom. We only have to take a sharp sword and cut the trunk in the
middle, so that the cross-section will reveal to us its pith-less nature.
Similarly, if we cut in the middle the banana trunk of preparations with
the sharp sword of wisdom, its internal structure as revealed by the
cross-section will convince us of the essence-less nature of the group
of preparations."

### The blood-test simile (here and now)
"A specialist doctor today needs only a drop of blood or blood tissue
for a full diagnosis of a patient's disease. When seen under the
microscope, that blood tissue reveals the pathological condition of the
patient. Even the patient himself could be invited to see for himself
the result of the blood test."

### Hemaka's praise (Sn): the Buddha teaches the present
"Those who explained to me before, outside the dispensation of Gotama,
all of them said: 'so it was, and so it will be', but all that is 'so
and so' talk, all that is productive of logic, I did not delight therein.
But now to me, O! sage, proclaim your Dhamma, that is destructive of
craving, by knowing which and mindfully faring along, one might get
beyond the world's viscosity."
"Itihitiha had already become a technical term for 'hearsay' among the
ascetics. Such teachings based on hearsay were productive of logic, as
for instance testified by the Sabbasavasutta."

## SECTION 3: WHAT KEEPS THE MECHANISM RUNNING

### The Mahanidana womb catechism (four questions)
1. "If consciousness were not to descend into a mother's womb, would
   name-and-form remain there?" — "It would not, Lord."
2. "If consciousness, having descended, were to slip out of it, would
   name-and-form be born into this state of existence?" — "No."
3. "If the consciousness of a boy or girl were cut off when still young,
   will name-and-form come to growth and maturity?" — "No."
4. "If consciousness were not to find a footing in name-and-form, would
   there be an arising of birth, decay, death and suffering in the future?"
   — "No indeed, Lord."

"Therefore, Ananda, this itself is the cause, this is the reason, origin
and condition for name-and-form, namely consciousness."

"The fourth point is extremely important. Though the aforesaid is the
normal state of affairs in samsara, if for some reason or other
consciousness does not get established on name-and-form, if at all such
a contrivance were possible, there will not be any samsaric suffering
again."

### Established vs unestablished consciousness
"Sampasādanīyasutta: a person with such an attainment sees a man's
stream of consciousness that is not cut off on either side, established
in this world and in the next: 'ubhayato abbocchinnam idha loke
patitthitam ca para loke patitthitam ca.'"
"The consciousness of an ordinary samsaric individual is always
established. The above mentioned relationship is always there. Because
of this we can say that there is always a knot in the consciousness of
the samsaric individual. For him, this world and the next world are tied
together in a knot. In this case, what is needed, is only the untying
of the knot. There is no need of a fresh tying up, as the knot is
already there."

### Godhika's unestablished consciousness
"Though he cut his own neck intending to commit suicide, he was able to
attain parinibbana as an arahant by radically attending to the deadly
pain. But Mara took him to be an ordinary person and hovered around in
search of his consciousness — in vain."
"The Buddha declared: 'O! monks, the clansman Godhika passed away with
an unestablished consciousness.'"

### The patisandhi critique
"The term patisandhi vinnana, or rebirth-linking-consciousness, is now
so widely used that we cannot help making use of it... However, some
scholars even raise the question, why a term considered so important is
not to be found in the discourses. On many an occasion the Buddha speaks
about the descent into a womb. But apart from using such terms as okkanti,
descent, gabbhassa avakkanti, descent into a womb, and uppatti, arising,
he does not seem to have used the term patisandhi."
"Going by the same simile [of the reeds], it would be tantamount to
saying that rebirth-linking-consciousness straightens up when death-
consciousness falls, as if, when one bundle of reeds is drawn, the other
straightens up. This contradicts the nature of mutuality condition.
There is no timelessness here."

### The mind's travel is not the body's travel
"The way the mind travels is quite different from the way the body travels.
The journey of the body is a case of leaving one place to go to another.
But the mind's journey is not like that. It is a sort of whirling or
turning round, as in the case of a whirlpool or a vortex."
"Just as in the case of a rubber-band which could be stretched lengthwise
or crosswise, there is a certain whirling round going on between
consciousness and name-and-form. It is because of that whirling motion
that consciousness and name-and-form could either get drawn apart, or
drawn in, as they go round and round in a kind of vortical interplay."

### The person immersed in thought (the mind travels)
"If someone, deeply attached to a person who is not near him, but living
somewhere far far away, is heavily immersed in some deep thought, then,
even if there is some painful contact, such as the prick of a fly, or the
bite of a mosquito, or even if another comes and shakes him by the
shoulder, he might not feel it, because he is so immersed in the thought."
"It is due to a lack of clear understanding about the journey of the
mind, that the concept of a relinking-consciousness was found to be
necessary."

### The photograph simile (latency is enough)
"It is like the shadow that falls on consciousness. Name-and-form is like
an image. Now in taking a photograph, there is a similar turn of events.
Even if one does not pose for the photograph with so much make-up, even
if one turns one's back to the camera, at least a shade of his shape will
be photographed as an image, if not his form."
"In the case of the samsaric individual, even if he does not entertain
an intention or thought construct, if he has at least the latency, anusaya,
that is enough for him to be reborn in some form of existence or other."

### The Cetasutta three instances (SN, Nidana-samyutta)
**Instance 1:** "Whatever one intends, whatever one mentally constructs,
whatever lies latent, that becomes an object for the stationing of
consciousness. There being an object, there comes to be an establishment
of consciousness. When that consciousness is established and grown, there
is the descent of name-and-form."

**Instance 2:** "Even if one does not intend or construct mentally, but
has a latency, that becomes an object for the stationing of consciousness."
"It is as if he has turned his back to the camera, but got photographed
all the same, due to his very presence there."

**Instance 3 (the most significant):** "When one neither intends, nor
constructs mentally, and has no latency either, then there is not that
object for the stationing of consciousness. There being no object, there
is no establishment of consciousness. When consciousness is unestablished
and not grown up, there is no descent of name-and-form, and with the
cessation of name-and-form, the six sense-bases, contact, feeling,
craving, grasping, becoming, birth, decay-and-death, sorrow, lamentation,
pain, grief and despair come to cease."

### The I-making and mine-making
"In regard to this body with its consciousness and all external signs
there is no I-making, mine-making, or underlying tendency to conceit."
"One can have latencies to conceit by way of I-making and mine-making
regarding this conscious body as well as all outside signs."

### Near-death experiences (brief mention)
"Cases have been reported of persons, on the verge of death, going
through such unusual experiences as visualizing their own body from some
outside standpoint. That external standpoint might not be a place which
has the ability to sustain that consciousness... All the same, it
temporarily escapes and goes there and is now wavering to decide, whether
or not to come back to the body, as it were."

## SECTION 4: WHAT THE MECHANISM BUILDS

### The concepts of birth and death are meaningful only within the vortex
"In so far only can one be born, or grow old, or die, or pass away, or
reappear... So the concepts of birth, decay-and-death, passing away and
reappearing, are meaningful only in the context of the samsaric vortex
between consciousness and name-and-form. If somehow or other this
interrelation could be broken, this samsaric vortex, the whirlpool, could
be stopped, then, after that, nothing remains to be said, nothing remains
to be predicated."

### Upasiva: nothing to say
"That by which they would speak of him, that for him exists not."

### Every concept leaning on nama-rupa falls when the other bundle is drawn
"All concepts, in so far as they are leaning on to the name-and-form
bundle, have to fall down when the opposite bundle of reeds is drawn out.
That is to say that the entire mass of samsaric suffering ceases
immediately."

## SECTION 5: HOW TO SEE THROUGH IT

### Yoniso manasikara = attention by way of the matrix / vortex
"Radical reflection, literally: 'attention by way of the matrix'. One
might as well say that it is an attention by way of the vortex."

### The third instance of the Cetasutta = the practice
"When one neither intends, nor constructs mentally, and has no latency
either" — that is the practice. No intention, no construction, no latency.
Then consciousness is unestablished. Then name-and-form ceases.

### Sariputta's quote: seeing dependent arising IS seeing the Dhamma
"He who sees the law of dependent arising, sees the Dhamma."

## REFUTATIONS

### The three-period interpretation (Visuddhimagga)
The first two links = past, last two = future, middle eight = present.
"But this is not — timeless." The pentad interpretation "violates the
interrelatedness between the twelve links." The commentary on Vipassī's
omission of avijja/sankhara is wrong — the ignorance IS the ignorance
of the consciousness/nama-rupa relationship.

### The patisandhi term
Not found in the discourses. The reeds simile contradicts it — "rebirth-
linking-consciousness straightens up when death-consciousness falls...
This contradicts the nature of mutuality condition."

### The commentary on itthatta and vatta (from Sermon 2, continued)
"Itihitiha = hearsay." The old teachers always referred to past and
future. The Buddha teaches the present.

## PASS 2 FINDS

- The Mahapadana parallel (DĀ 1) — the Chinese version does NOT have the
  reciprocal conditioning, but the Sanskrit fragments DO (Waldschmidt).
  This is a significant comparative detail.
- The commentary on Vipassī's omission — Nanananda's refutation is that
  ignorance IS the ignorance of the consciousness/nama-rupa relationship,
  not a past event. This is a major exegetical move.
- The patisandhi critique — Nanananda questions a term used universally
  in Buddhist studies. This is one of his most radical moves.
- The photograph simile — turning your back to the camera but getting
  photographed anyway — is a perfect illustration of anusaya (latency).
- The rubber-band simile for the consciousness/nama-rupa whirlpool —
  stretchable lengthwise or crosswise, vortical interplay.
- The near-death experience mention — brief but notable. Nanananda
  connects it to the mind's travel being different from the body's.

## PASS 3: HOLES AND CONNECTIONS

- **Hole:** The sermon establishes that the third Cetasutta instance is
  the practice, but doesn't fully explain HOW to get to "neither intention
  nor construction nor latency." That's left for the practice sermons.
- **Hole:** The relationship between anusaya (latency) and the practice
  of letting go of latency is established but not worked through.
- **Connection to Sermon 2:** The whirlpool simile is the same, now mapped
  to the twelve links. The itthabhava/annathabhava pair is continued.
- **Connection to Sermon 1:** The fire simile reappears ("where has the
  fire gone"). The idappaccayata formula is the same.
- **Connection to Sermons 5-6:** The satipatthana frame (Sermon 5) is the
  practice that leads to the Cetasutta's third instance. The Samudaya
  contemplations (Sermon 6) map each establishment to its condition.
- **Connection to Sermons 7-8:** The anidassana vinnana (Kevaddha +
  Brahmanimantanika) is the "unestablished consciousness" of this sermon.
  The arahant's consciousness is unestablished.
- **Connection to VBT pack:** The "neither intention nor construction nor
  latency" practice maps to the signless concentration practice. The
  Cetasutta's three instances are a graded practice: from intention → to
  latency → to neither.
- **Analayo note:** The aloka = "clarity" and bodhi = "awakening" note
  is a translation correction worth tracking.
