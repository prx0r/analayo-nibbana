# SERMON 15 — EXCAVATION

Source: Nibbana-15.pdf (BCBS/Dharma Seed, free). 7851 words.

---

## SECTION 1: WHAT NIBBANA IS

### The Bahiya instruction (the full analysis)
"In the seen there will be just the seen... Then you will not be by it.
And when you are not by it, you are not in it. And when you are not in
it, you are neither here nor there nor in between. This itself is the
end of suffering."

### Na tena = not imagining thereby
"Dhono na hi tena manñati yadidam ditthasutam mutesu va — the arahant
does not imagine 'thereby', namely by whatever is seen, heard and
sensed."
"What is implied by the elliptical na tena is the idea of imagining...
Na tena manñati, 'does not imagine thereby'."

### The six qualities exemplified
"The Bahiya sutta illustrates the six qualities of the Dhamma: well
proclaimed, visible here and now, timeless, inviting to come and see,
leading onward, to be realized by the wise each one by himself."

### The dewdrop simile (right view)
"Simply by standing or sitting with one's face towards the rising sun,
one will not be able to catch a glimpse of the brilliant spectrum of
rainbow colours through the dewdrop. It requires a particular viewpoint."
"Right view is not something one inherits by merely going to refuge to
the Buddha. It has to be developed with effort and proper attention."
"One may be short of just one-hundredth of an inch as the proper
adjustment for right view. Yet it is a must."

## SECTION 2: HOW THE MECHANISM WORKS

### The mula-pariyaya substitution
"Suppose we substitute dittha, or the seen, in place of pathavi. This
is what we should get: 'He understands through higher knowledge the
seen as seen. Let him not imagine the seen as a thing. Let him not
imagine "in the seen" or "from the seen". Let him not imagine "the seen
is mine". Let him not delight in the seen.'"

### Atammayata vs tammayata
"When one starts imagining in such terms about something, one tends to
become one with it, tammayo, even as things made out of gold and silver
are called golden."
"To possess a gem is to be possessed by it."
"That attitude leads to non-identification, atammayata."

### The triple training in four usages
1. "In the seen just the seen" = training in higher morality (sense
   restraint)
2. Stopping at just the seen = training in higher concentration (no
   discursive thought, no hindrances)
3. Freedom from imaginings and proliferation = training in higher wisdom
4. The result: na tena, na tattha, nevida na huram = cessation

### The arahant's evidence (Chabbisodhana)
"With regard to the seen, I dwell unattracted, unrepelled, independent,
uninvolved, released, unshackled, with a mind free from barriers."
"The barriers are the bifurcations such as the internal and the external,
which are so basic to what is called existence."

### The six sense-bases as a deserted village
"The Buddha has compared the six sense-bases to a deserted village.
'This is void of a self or anything belonging to a self.' All these
sense-bases are devoid of a self."

### The stars/moon/sun verse (Bahiya conclusion)
"Where water, earth, fire and air do not find a footing, there the
stars do not shine, the sun spreads not its lustre, the moon does not
appear resplendent there, and no darkness is to be found there."
"The sun, the moon and the stars fade away before the superior radiance
of the nonmanifestative consciousness, which is infinite and lustrous
on all sides."

### The four lustres (revisited)
"The lustre of the moon, the lustre of the sun, the lustre of fire, and
the lustre of wisdom. This is the highest among these four."
"Natthi paññasama abha — there is no radiance comparable to wisdom."

### The world cinema and the three lights
"In the twilight glow of the evening the twinkling stars enable us to
faintly figure out the objects around us. Then the moon comes up. The
twinkling little stars fade away. Then the night passes off. The day
light gleam of the sun comes up. The soft radiance of the moon wanes
before the majestic lustre of the sun."
"Now let us suppose that the extraneous defilements in the mind of a
noble disciple get dispelled, allowing its intrinsic lustre of wisdom
to shine forth. The stars, the moon and the sun get superseded by that
light of wisdom."

### The six narrow beams
"The worldling discerns the world around him with the help of six
narrow beams of light, namely the six sense-bases. When the superior
lustre of wisdom arises, those six sense-bases go down."
"The cessation of the six sense-bases does not mean that one does not
see anything. What one sees then is voidness. It is an in-'sight'."

### Dhammadinna's tangle (path order)
"Is the noble eightfold path included by the three groups, or are the
three groups included by the noble eightfold path?"
"It is not that the threefold training is included by the noble eightfold
path, but the noble eightfold path is included by the threefold training."
Analayo parallel: MĀ 210.
"Suppose someone is trying to climb up a long rope, made up of three
strands. As he climbs up, his fingertips might come now in contact with
the first strand, now with the second and now with the third."

## SECTION 3: WHAT KEEPS THE MECHANISM RUNNING

### Proliferation as transaction
"Papanca, or prolific conceptualisation, is a process of transaction
with whatever is seen, heard, sensed. So here there is no process of
such transaction."

### The peg of the conceit 'am'
"This kind of training uproots the peg of the conceit 'am', thereby
bringing about the cessation of prolific conceptualisation, the cessation
of becoming and the cessation of suffering."

---

## SECTION 4: WHAT THE MECHANISM BUILDS

### Form and formless fade
"Umbra of form and the penumbra of the formless get fully erased."
"When one sees forms, one sees them together with their shadows. The
fact that one sees shadows there is itself proof that darkness has not
been fully dispelled."

### The Mahatissa episode
"When he met a certain woman on his way, who displayed her teeth in a
wily giggle, he simply grasped the sign of her teeth. He did not totally
refrain from grasping a sign, but took it as an illustration of his
meditation subject."
"When that woman's husband asked whether he had seen a woman, he replied
that all he saw was a skeleton."

## SECTION 5: HOW TO SEE THROUGH IT

### The training instruction
"In the seen there will be just the seen... Thus should you train
yourself."

### The sense-restraint training
"If one stops short at just the seen in regard to the seen, one does
not grasp a sign in it, or dwell on its details. There is no sorting
out as 'this is good', 'this is bad'. That itself conduces to
sense-restraint."

### The straightening of view
"For diṭṭhujukamma, the act of straightening up one's view is reckoned
as one of the ten skilful deeds."

## SECTION 6: WHAT REMAINS

### Bahiya's parinibbana
"Bahiyaa Dārucīriya was wise, he lived up to the norm of the Dhamma,
he did not harass me with questions on Dhamma. Bahiyaa Dārucīriya has
attained Parinibbana."
"Just a cow with a young calf gored the arahant Bahiyaa to death."

### The six beams go down
"The worldling discerns the world with the help of six narrow beams.
When the superior lustre of wisdom arises, those six sense-bases go down."

## REFUTATIONS

### The commentary on the Bahiya conclusion verse
"The commentator interprets the verse as a description of the destination
of the arahant after Parinibbana — the place he went to. That place is
not easily understood by worldlings."
"The verse is not the answer to the question 'where did he go?' It is
only a joyous utterance, a verse of uplift."
"The place where earth, water, fire and air do not find a footing is
not where Bahiyaa had 'gone'. It is the nonmanifestative consciousness
he had realized here and now."

## PASS 2 FINDS

- The six narrow beams insight — the worldling sees through six narrow
  beams (the six sense-bases), and when wisdom arises those beams go
  down — is the most precise visual description of the signless in the
  series.
- The superseding light (stars → moon → sun → wisdom) is the complete
  visual grammar for the dhamma-visual language's light rule.
- The Bahiya conclusion verse (stars don't shine, sun doesn't spread
  lustre, moon doesn't appear, no darkness) is NOT about a destination
  — it's about the consciousness realized here and now.
- The Dhammadinna tangle (path order) is resolved by the rope simile —
  three strands, well knit, no confusion about numerical order.
- The Mahatissa episode (seeing a skeleton instead of a woman) is the
  most vivid example of yoniso manasikara in the series.

## PASS 3: HOLES AND CONNECTIONS

- **Hole:** The relationship between the Bahiya practice and the VBT
  practices is established but the mapping is not made explicit. The
  Bahiya instruction IS the VBT bare-sensing practice.
- **Hole:** The dewdrop simile (right view as correct viewpoint) is
  established but the practice method for straightening view is only
  implied.
- **Connection to Sermon 14:** The Bahiya sutta is introduced and fully
  analysed here. The elements meditation (Sermon 14) is the practice
  that leads to the Bahiya insight.
- **Connection to Sermon 11:** The vacant gaze (magic show viewer) is
  the same as the Bahiya practitioner — watching without imagining.
- **Connection to VBT pack:** The Bahiya instruction IS the VBT bare-
  sensing practice. The dewdrop simile maps to the VBT practice of
  finding the correct viewpoint.
