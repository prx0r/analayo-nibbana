# SERMON 26 — EXCAVATION

Source: Nibbana-26.pdf (BCBS/Dharma Seed, free). 9134 words.

---

## SECTION 1: WHAT NIBBANA IS

### The magic show seen so well as to miss it
"In short, he has seen the magic show so well as to miss the show."
"I saw it. In fact I saw it so well that I missed the show."
"The Tathāgata saw the magic show of consciousness so well as to miss
the show, from the point of view of the worldlings."

### In the world, not of the world
"One seems to be in the world, but one is not of the world. That man
with discernment was in the hall all that time, but it was as if he
was not there."

### What the arahant is percipient of
"Of what is he percipient or conscious? That is none other than what
comes up as the title of these series of sermons, namely: Etaṃ santaṃ,
etaṃ paṇītaṃ, yadidaṃ sabbasaṅkhārasamatho sabbūpadhipaṭinissaggo
taṇhakkhayo virāgo nirodho nibbānaṃ — 'This is peaceful, this is
excellent, namely the stilling of all preparations, the relinquishment
of all assets, the destruction of craving, detachment, cessation,
extinction.'"

## SECTION 2: HOW THE MECHANISM WORKS

### The magic show parable (full)
"A certain magician is going to hold a magic show in some hall or
theatre. Among those who have come to see the magic show, there is a
witty person with the wisdom eye, who tells himself: 'Today I must see
the magic show inside out!' With this determination he hides himself in
a corner of the stage, unseen by others."
"This person begins to discover, before long, the secrets of the
magician, his deceitful stock-in-trade — counterfeits, hidden strings
and buttons, secret pockets and false bottoms... With this vision, he
comes to the conclusion that there is no magic in any of those gadgets.
Some sort of disenchantment sets in... Instead he now settles into a
mood of equanimity."
"Then he sees the contrast. The entire hall is a sea of craned necks,
gaping mouths and goggle-eyes with 'Ahs' and 'Ohs' and whistles of
speechless amazement. At this sorry sight, he even feels remorseful
that he himself was in this same plight before."
"So in this way he sees through the magic show — an 'insight' instead
of a 'sight'."

### The dialogue after the show
"'Why, you were in the same hall all this time, weren't you?' 'Yes, I
was.' 'Then were you sleeping?' 'Oh, no.' 'You weren't watching
closely, I suppose.' 'No, no, I was watching it all right, maybe I was
watching too closely.' 'You say you were watching, but you don't seem
to have seen the show.' 'No, I saw it. In fact I saw it so well that I
missed the show.'"
"The above dialogue... should give a clue to the riddle-like
proclamations of the Buddha in the Kāḷakārāmasutta."

### Paṭisallāna as the hide-away
"The term paṭisallāna, 'solitude', has a nuance suggestive of a
hide-away. It is in such a hide-away that the Buddha witnessed the
interior of the six-fold sense-base."

### The basic principle of dependent arising (the norm)
"Imasmiṃ sati idaṃ hoti, imassa uppādā idaṃ upajjati, imasmiṃ asati
idaṃ na hoti, imassa nirodhā idaṃ nirujjhati — 'This being, this comes
to be; with the arising of this, this arises; this not being, this does
not come to be; with the cessation of this, this ceases.'"
"This statement of the basic principle of dependent arising is very
often overlooked."
"This is the norm, the suchness, tathatā, that the Buddha discovered."

### The twelve links are illustrations, not the cause
"In such discourses as the Bahudhātukasutta the twelve-linked formula
is introduced with a set of these thematic phrases, which is then
related to the formula proper with the conjunctive 'that is to say',
yadidaṃ. This conjunctive clearly indicates that the twelve-linked
formula is an illustration. The twelve links are therefore things
dependently arisen, paṭicca samuppannā dhammā."
"All the twelve links are dependently arisen, and avijjā is no
exception. Even ignorance arises with the arising of influxes,
āsavasamudayā avijjāsamudayo. So even ignorance is not the cause. The
cause is the underlying principle itself."

### The forest and the trees
"It is as if the Buddha stretches his arm and says: 'That is a forest',
and one goes and catches hold of a tree, exclaiming: 'Ah, this is the
forest'. To rattle off the twelve links in the hope of grasping the
law of paṭicca samuppāda is like counting the number of trees in order
to see the forest."
"One has to get a synoptic view of it from here itself. Such a view
takes into account not only the trees, but also the intervening spaces
between them, all at one synoptic glance."
"One has to bring one's mind to rest on the norm that obtains between
every two links."

### The Assaji verse (Upatissa and Kolita)
"Ye dhammā hetuppabhavā, tesaṃ hetuṃ tathāgato āha, tesañca yo
nirodho, evaṃ vādī mahāsamaṇo — 'Of things that proceed from a cause,
their cause the Tathāgata has told, and also their cessation, thus
teaches the great ascetic.'"
"The wandering ascetic Upatissa, who was to become venerable Sāriputta
later, grasped the clue to the entire saṃsāric riddle then and there,
and discovered the secret of the magic show of consciousness, even by
the first two lines... The wandering ascetic Kolita, however, became a
stream-winner only on hearing all four lines."
"The word hetu in this verse has to be understood as a reference to the
law of dependent arising."

### The links all wear out
"Everyone of those twelve links... is impermanent, prepared,
dependently arisen, of a nature to wither away, wear away, fade away
and cease, aniccaṃ, saṅkhataṃ, paṭicca samuppannaṃ, khayadhammaṃ,
vayadhammaṃ, virāgadhammaṃ, nirodhadhammaṃ."
Analayo parallel: Bodhi (2000: 551).
"The term saṅkhataṃ has nuances of artificiality and spuriousness."

### Preparations only when ignorance is there
"Avijjāya sati saṅkhārā honti — preparations are there only when
ignorance is there. With the arising of ignorance, preparations arise.
When ignorance is not there, preparations lose their provenance."
"To understand preparations for what they are is knowledge.
Simultaneous with the arising of that knowledge, preparations become
mere preparations, or pure preparations, suddha saṅkhārā."
Adhimutta Theragāthā: "To one who sees the arising of pure dhammas and
the sequence of pure preparations, as they are, there is no fear, oh
headman."
Analayo parallel: Norman (1969: 14).

### Consciousness always specific, never abstract
"Now if someone is always aware that consciousness arises dependent on
preparations... always specific and never abstract — consciousness
ceases to appear as a monolithic whole. This particular eye-consciousness
has arisen because of eye and forms."
"When everything else slips out from their grasp, they grasp
consciousness as their soul, because it is invisible."

### Specific contact, specific feeling (Nandakovāda)
"Tajjaṃ tajjaṃ, bhante, paccayaṃ paṭicca tajjā tajjā vedanā
uppajjanti; tajjassa tajjassa paccayassa nirodhā tajjā tajjā vedanā
nirujjhanti — 'Dependent on each specific condition, venerable sir,
specific feelings arise, and with the cessation of each specific
condition, specific feelings cease.'"
Analayo parallel: Ñāṇamoli (1995: 1123); SĀ 276 ("become truly cool").

### Consciousness and wisdom conjoined (MahāVedalla)
"Viññāṇa and paññā as two conjoined psychological states. They cannot
be separated one from the other, saṃsaṭṭhā no visaṃsaṭṭhā. But they
can be distinguished functionally. Out of them, wisdom is to be
developed, while consciousness is to be comprehended, paññā bhāvetabbā,
viññāṇaṃ pariññeyyaṃ."

### The influxes (āsavā) and why four stages
"Influxes are sometimes reckoned as fourfold... But more often, in
contexts announcing the attainment of arahant-hood, the standard
reference is to three types."
"The term āsavā implies those corrupting influences ingrained in
beings due to saṃsāric habits. They have a tendency to flow in and
tempt beings towards sensuality, existence and ignorance."
"Ignorance or ignoring is itself a habit. There is a tendency in
saṃsāric beings to grope in darkness and dislike light. They have a
tendency to blink at the light and ignore. It is easy to ignore and
forget. This forgetting trait enables them to linger long in saṃsāra."
"Ignorance as a kind of influxes is so powerful that even the keenest
in wisdom cannot attain arahant-hood at once. The wheel of Dhamma has
to turn four times."

### The rickety machine (birth)
"Sometimes we happen to buy from a shop an extremely rickety machine
deceived by its paint and polish, and take it home as a brand new
thing. The very next day it goes out of order... So is our birth with
its unpredictable vicissitudes... This is the price we pay for this
brand new body we are blessed with in this existence."

### The blind men and the elephant (Udāna, Jaccandha)
"Those who touched the elephant's head compared the elephant to a pot,
those who touched its ears compared it to a winnowing basket, those
who touched its tusk compared it to a ploughshare and so forth. The
dogmatic views in the world follow the same trend."

### The Pukkusa episode (conscious and awake, seeing nothing)
Āḷāra Kālāma: five hundred carts pass; "'Venerable sir, did you see
about five-hundred carts passing by?' 'No, friend, I didn't see.'...
'Were you conscious, then?' 'Yes, friend.'"
The Buddha's greater feat: storm at Ātumā, two farmers and four bulls
struck dead; "'Were you conscious (saññī)?' 'Yes, friend.'"
"'It is a wonder, it is a marvel, what a peaceful life those who have
gone forth are leading, so much so that while being conscious and
awake one would neither see nor hear the streaks of lightening and
peals of thunder in the midst of a torrential downpour'."
Analayo parallel: Walshe (1987: 258).

### The Kalahavivāda riddle-verse
"He is not conscious of normal perception, nor is he unconscious, he
is not devoid of perception, nor has he rescinded perception, it is to
one thus constituted that form ceases to exist, for reckonings through
prolificity have perception as their source."
Analayo parallel: Bodhi (2017: 306).
"Perception is the source of all prolific reckonings."

## SECTION 3: WHAT KEEPS THE MECHANISM RUNNING

### The shroud of ignorance
"In the case of the magic show, the very ignorance of the tricks of
the magician is what accounts for the apparent reality of the magic
performance. Once the shroud of ignorance is thrown off, the magic show
loses its magic for the audience."
"The magician's secret stock-in-trade gave rise to the saṅkhāras or
preparations with the help of which the audience created for themselves
a magic show."

### Resting on duality
"The worldling, for the most part, rests on a duality. The world is
steeped in the notion of duality. It grasps either this end, or the
other end. For instance, one may grasp either ignorance, avijjā, or
preparations, saṅkhārā. But here we have neither."
"Hard it is for the world to understand the stance of the arahant
couched in the cryptic phrase nev' idha na huraṃ na ubhayam antare,
'neither here nor there nor in between the two'."

### Individual truths from perception level
"According to one's level of perception, one forms a notion of
reality. To those in the audience the tricks of the magician remained
concealed. It is that ignorance which aroused preparations in them."
"There are no several and various truths, that are permanent in the
world, apart from perception." (Cūḷaviyūha)

### Dogmatism from contact
"Depending on name-and-form, which they grasped, they evolved dogmatic
theories, based on their perceptions, spurred on by sense-contact...
The Buddha had no dogmatic involvement, because he had seen the
cessation of consciousness. Even the mind ceases, and mind-objects fade
away." (Brahmajāla, even with abhiññā)

## SECTION 4: WHAT THE MECHANISM BUILDS

### The magic world of the audience
"The latter came out of the hall as if coming out of the magic world.
He had been amassing graspings proper to a magic world due to his
ignorance of those preparations."
"Being influx-free, they do not go to build up a prepared, saṅkhata.
They do not precipitate an amassing of grasping, upādāna, to bring
about an existence, bhava. This amounts to a release from existence."

### Birth bounded by parents
"Just as one seated on this side of a parapet wall might not see what
is on the other side, what we take as our existence in this world is
bounded by our parents from the point of view of birth. What we take
as death is the end of this physical body. We are ignorant of the fact
that it is a flux of preparations, saṅkhārasantati."

## SECTION 5: HOW TO SEE THROUGH IT

### Dwell on the norm between the links
"To dwell on the law of dependent arising is the way to liberate the
mind from the whole lot of dependently arisen things."
"When one has understood this as a fact of experience, one brings
one's mind to rest, not on the things dependently arisen, but on the
law of dependent arising itself."
"It is an extremely difficult task, because the mind tends to slip
off. The habitual tendency is to grasp this one or the other."

### The chain of attending (link by link)
"When one attends to the fact that the six sense-bases are dependent on
name-and-form, and that they are there only as long as name-and-form
is there, and that with the cessation of name-and-form the six
sense-bases also cease, one is attuning one's mind to the law of
dependent arising, thereby weaning one's mind away from its hold on
dependently arisen things."
"To attend to the specific contact as the cause of feeling is the way
of disenchantment with both feeling and contact."
"When one understands that this existence is dependent on grasping,
arising out of craving, one will not take existence seriously."

### Consciousness comprehended by wisdom
"The development of wisdom is for the purpose of comprehending
consciousness and comprehended consciousness proves to be empty,
essenceless and hollow."
"Disgust, disillusionment and dejection in regard to consciousness is
what accounts for its complete cessation, sooner or later."
"The insight into this cessation brings about the realization that all
what the worldlings take as absolutely true, permanent or eternal, are
mere phenomena arisen from the mind. Manopubbangamā dhammā, mind is
the forerunner of all mind-objects. One comes to understand that all
what is arisen is bound to cease, and that the cessation can occur
here and now."

### Not clinging even to the middle
"Not to cling even to the middle is the ideal of an arahant. That is
the implication of the conclusive statement in the advice to Bāhiya,
nev' idha na huraṃ na ubhayam antarena."
Analayo parallel: Ireland (1990: 20); Anālayo 2018, "The Bāhiya
Instruction and Bare Awareness", IIJBS 19: 1–19.

## SECTION 6: WHAT REMAINS

### The tolerant neutrality of understanding
"He knows that, given ignorance, they are true, and that they are
falsified only when ignorance fades away in one who sees the cessation.
Fortified with that understanding, he does not categorically assert the
sense-data as true, nor does he reprimand those who assert them as the
truth. That is why the Buddha advocates a tolerant attitude in this
discourse."
"This is the typical attitude of an understanding elder to the
questions put by an inquisitive toddler."
"On many such issues, silence happens to be the answer."

### The discerning one's position
"All those who came out of that hall, except this discerning one, were
spectators. He was not one of the audience, because he had an insight
into the magic show from his hiding place on the stage."
"He listens through with equanimity."

## REFUTATIONS

### Parroting the twelve links (missing the wood for the trees)
"The most important normative prefatory declaration is ignored...
This confusion regarding the way of explaining paṭicca samuppāda is a
case of missing the wood for the trees."
"That doesn't mean the ability to rattle off the twelve links by
heart, but the task of bringing the mind to rest on the norm of
paṭicca samuppāda itself."

### The womb reading of the six bases
"In discussing the formula of paṭicca samuppāda, the arising of the
six sense-bases is very often explained with reference to a mother's
womb... But for the Buddha the arising of the six sense-bases was not
a stage in the growth of a foetus in the mother's womb. It was through
wisdom that he saw the six bases of sense-contact arising then and
there."

### The Pukkusa misreadings
"Some interpret this incident as an illustration of the Buddha's
attainment to the cessation of perceptions and feelings. But if it had
been the case, the words saññī samāno jāgaro, 'while being conscious
and awake', would be out of place."
"Nor can this incident be interpreted as a reference to the realm of
nothingness, ākiñcaññāyatana, in the context of the allusion to
Āḷārā Kālāma and his less impressive psychic powers."
"The true import of this extraordinary psychic feat has to be assessed
with reference to the arahattaphalasamādhi... the cessation of the six
sense-spheres, equivalent to the cessation of the world."

### The Tathāgata's neutrality is insight, not tactics
"His neutral attitude was not the result of any lack of knowledge, or
tactical eel wriggling, as in the case of Sañjaya Belaṭṭhiputta."

## PASS 2 FINDS

- **Miss 1:** Manopubbangamā dhammā (mind as forerunner of
  mind-objects) attached to the insight-into-cessation passage — added
  to Section 5.
- **Miss 2:** Voice line had trimmed "of consciousness...from the point
  of view of the worldlings" out of the miss-the-show sentence —
  restored to exact wording.
- The magic show parable in full — the extended scene (stage hiding,
  gadgets, audience, post-show dialogue) is Nāṇananda's own
  composition, not canonical, and is the richest narrative illustration
  in the entire series. Extracted complete.
- The forest/trees argument is a standalone methodological refutation:
  the twelve-link formula is an illustration (yadidaṃ) of the four-line
  norm, not the norm itself. The Bahudhātuka grammatical argument is
  the proof.
- The Assaji verse two-lines-vs-four reading (Upatissa grasped the
  law; Kolita the four links) — a new reading of the conversion scene.
- The Pukkusa/Āḷāra Kālāma storm episode with two misreading
  refutations attached — named teacher (Āḷāra Kālāma) with role.
- The influxes section explains WHY there are four stages — ignorance
  as influx is a habit so deep that wisdom needs four turns of the
  wheel.
- The rickety-machine birth simile — new visual for Section 4.
- Anālayo 2018 IIJBS Bāhiya article — evidence-table addition.
- The "understanding elder to an inquisitive toddler" line — the
  sermon's own metaphor for the tolerant neutrality.

## PASS 3: HOLES AND CONNECTIONS

- **Hole:** The practical instruction "bring the mind to rest on the
  norm between every two links" is stated as extremely difficult but
  the technique for staying there is not given — it connects to the
  signless/awareness practices of Sermon 15.
- **Hole:** The relation between the Pukkusa psychic feat and ordinary
  practice is left at the level of arahattaphalasamādhi — a beginner
  cannot imitate it; note as evidence-not-instruction.
- **Connection to Sermon 25:** Direct continuation — the magic show
  simile imported from Sermon 25's Pheṇapiṇḍa quote and expanded into
  a full parable; Kāḷakārāma's four negations now explained via the
  dialogue.
- **Connection to Sermon 2/3:** The twelve-links-as-illustration
  argument completes the "three lives vs one moment" material — the
  norm is even more basic than the momentary reading.
- **Connection to Sermon 18:** The dog on the plank returns (as
  name-and-form reflection).
- **Connection to Sermon 10:** The "tangle within and tangle without"
  (anto jaṭā bahi jaṭā) recurs at the namarupa step.
- **Connection to VBT pack:** "Always specific and never abstract" IS
  the VBT practice of watching particular experiences rather than the
  generalized "my mind" — consciousness dissolves into conditions when
  tracked concretely.
- **Analayo note:** Anālayo 2018 on the Bāhiya instruction (bare
  awareness) is flagged in-source; Sermon 15's Bāhiya material and
  this one should cross-reference in the final merge.
