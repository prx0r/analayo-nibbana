# SERMON 9 — EXCAVATION

Source: Nibbana-09.pdf (BCBS/Dharma Seed, free). 9183 words.

---

## SECTION 1: WHAT NIBBANA IS

### The deathless as culmination
Samiddhi Sutta: "In what do they culminate? They get merged in the
deathless, venerable sir." Amatogadha = merged in the deathless. The
deathless is a term for Nibbana.

### Deliverance is the essence
"Vimuttisara sabbe dhamma — deliverance is the essence of all things."
"The very emancipation from all this, to be rid of all this, is itself
the essence. Some seem to think that the essence is a heaping up of
concepts and clinging to them. But that is not the essence of this
teaching. It is the ability to penetrate all concepts, thereby
transcending them."

### The gem is transcended by wisdom
"What one has to do now is to see through the gem, to penetrate it, by
viewing it as impermanent, fraught with suffering, and not-self, thereby
arriving at the conviction that, after all, the gem belongs to the
rubbish heap itself."
"If one wins to the wisdom that this gem is something like a piece of
charcoal, to be destroyed in the holocaust at the end of a world period,
one has transcended that gem."

## SECTION 2: HOW THE MECHANISM WORKS

### The relativity principle (SN, Salayatana-vagga)
"When there are hands, monks, a taking up and putting down is apparent;
when there are feet, a going forward and coming back is apparent; when
there are joints, a bending and stretching is apparent; when there is
a belly, hunger and thirst is apparent."
"When there are no hands, a taking up and putting down is not apparent;
when there are no feet, a going forward and coming back is not apparent;
when there are no joints, a bending and stretching is not apparent; when
there is no belly, hunger and thirst are not apparent."
Analayo parallel: SĀ 1166.
"What is implied by all this is that basic principle of relativity...
the understanding of the relativity between the two, which reveals the
emptiness of both."

### The Kimmulaka sutta (AN, Eights)
Eight questions from other sectarians: What is the root of all things?
Origin? Where do they arise? Towards what do they converge? What is at
the head? What dominates? What is transcendence? What is the essence?
Eight answers: Rooted in desire. Born of attention. Arisen from contact.
Converging on feeling. Headed by concentration. Dominated by mindfulness.
Surmountable by wisdom. Yielding deliverance as essence.
Analayo parallel: MĀ 113 (same sequence, slightly different wording).

### The gem in the rubbish heap (the full parable)
"Supposing there is a heap of rubbish and someone approaches it with a
basket to collect it and throw it away. About the rubbish heap, he has
just a unitary notion. But as he bends down and starts collecting it
into the basket, he suddenly catches sight of a gem. Now the gem becomes
the object of his desire and interest."
"It is through attention that the gem came into being... the Buddha
traces its origin in accordance with the norm manopubbagama dhamma,
'mind is the forerunner of all things'."
"Eye-contact with the gem as something special out of all the things in
the rubbish heap. So the gem 'arose' from eye-contact."
"It may be wrong concentration, miccha samadhi, but all the same it is
some kind of concentration. It is now a concentration on the gem."

### The rainbow in the dewdrop
"One keen on seeing a rainbow will have to go on looking at the sky
indefinitely, waiting for a rainbow to appear. But if he is wise enough,
he can see the spectrum of rainbow colours through a dewdrop hanging on
a leaf of a creeper waving in the morning sun, provided he finds the
correct perspective. For him, the dewdrop itself is the meditation
object."
"One can sometimes see the entire Dhamma, thirty-seven factors of
enlightenment and the like, even in a potentially pernicious meditation
object."

### The Brahmajala net
"Just as a clever fisherman would throw a finely woven net well over a
small lake, so that all the creatures living there are caught in it as
they come up, all the possible views in the world are enmeshed or
forestalled by this super-net."
"There is a word occurring all over the discourse, which gives us a
clear answer to what constitutes that fine mesh: 'and that too is due
to contact, and that too is due to contact'. So from this we can see
that contact is the mesh of this net."

### The six consciousnesses (definition from Nidana-samyutta)
"There are these six classes of consciousness — eye-consciousness, ear-
consciousness, nose-consciousness, tongue-consciousness, body-conscious-
ness and mind-consciousness; this, monks, is called consciousness."
"The consciousness mentioned in the formula of dependent arising is not
something like a re-linking consciousness. It is in dependence on name-
and-form, reflected on all six types of consciousness, that the six
sense-bases get established."

### The gem in the pond (Ummagga-jataka)
"The citizens of Mithila came and informed king Videha that there is a
gem in the pond near the city gate. The king commissioned his royal
adviser Senaka... he went and got the people to empty the pond but failed
to find the gem there. Even the mud was taken out and the earth dug up."
"When he went there and had a look around, he immediately understood
that the gem is actually in a crow's nest on a palm tree near the pond.
What appeared in the pond is only its reflection."
"The view of the dogmatists can be compared to Senaka's view. The
discovery of the Buddha that name-and-form is a mere reflection is like
the solution advanced by bodhisatta Mahosadha."

## SECTION 3: WHAT KEEPS THE MECHANISM RUNNING

### Name-and-form as reflection
"The discrimination between an 'internal' and an 'external' is the
outcome of the inability to penetrate name-and-form, to see through it.
There is an apparent duality: I, as one who sees, and name-and-form, as
the objects seen. Between them there is a dichotomy as internal and
external. It is on this very dichotomy that the six sense-bases are
'based'."

### The two views (Itivuttaka)
"Obsessed by two views, monks, are gods and men, some of whom lag behind,
while others overreach, only they do see that have eyes to see."
"It is as if one is trying to outstrip one's shadow by running towards
it out of fun, while the other is trying to flee from it out of fear."

### The sixty-two wrong views
"What it portrays is how the sixty-two wrong views lose their lustre in
the light of wisdom emanating from the non-manifestative consciousness
of the Buddha, which is lustrous on all sides."
"Those heretics who propounded those wrong views, conceived them by
dogmatically holding on to name-and-form."

### The tinted window panes
"Just as someone inside a closed room with tinted window panes sees only
what is reflected on those dark panes, and not beyond, even so, those
'seers' got enmeshed in name-and-form when they proceeded to speculate
on what they saw as their past lives."

## SECTION 4: WHAT THE MECHANISM BUILDS

### The gem is made by attention
"The gem arose out of what earlier appeared as a rubbish heap. It became
the thing for him, and desire was at the root of this phenomenon."
"With the cessation of that concept of a gem as some special thing, a
valuable thing, separate from the rest of the world... the gem ceases
to exist. That itself is the deliverance."

### The perception of impermanence leads to not-self
"Monks, in one who has the perception of impermanence, the perception
of not-self gets established. With the perception of not-self, he
arrives at the destruction of the conceit 'am', which is extinction
here and now."
Analayo parallel: MĀ 57.

### The perception of impermanence exhausts everything
"Monks, the perception of impermanence, when developed and intensively
practised, exhausts all attachments to sensuality, exhausts all
attachments to form, exhausts all attachments to existence, exhausts all
ignorance, exhausts all conceits of an 'am' and eradicates it completely."
Analayo parallel: SĀ 270.

### Mogharaja's void question
"By looking upon the world in which manner can one escape the eye of the
king of death?"
"Look upon the world as void, Mogharaja, being mindful at all times,
uprooting the lingering view of self, get well beyond the range of death."

### Sabbe dhamma nalams abhinivesaya
"Nothing is worth clinging on to." The word abhinivesa is closely
associated with entering into and getting entangled in views of one's
own creation.

## SECTION 5: HOW TO SEE THROUGH IT

### Yoniso manasikara (radical attention) vs ayoniso
"There is a world of difference between them... But from the point of
view of realization, there is just a little difference. That spectrum of
the sun's rays in the dewdrop disappears with a very little shift in
one's perspective."

### The Nagasamala episode
"Once, on my begging round, I happened to look up to see a dancing woman,
beautifully dressed and bedecked, dancing to the rhythm of an orchestra
just on the middle of the highway. Just then, radical attention arose
from within me, the perils were manifest, and dejection took place, then
my mind got released."
"If a train of thoughts had got started up about that gem, even through
a wrong concentration, at whatever moment radical attention comes on the
scene, complete reorientation occurs instantaneously."

### The four postures (continued from Sermon 8)
"In whatever way his body is disposed, so he understands it."
"A meditator becomes aware, while attending to his postures, that he is
merely enacting a dumb show."

## SECTION 6: WHAT REMAINS

### The deathless
"They get merged in the deathless, venerable sir."
"Everything has the potentiality to yield the deathless, provided
radical attention is ushered in."

### The Dhammapada verse on the arahant
"Let one put wrath away, conceit abandon, and get well beyond all fetters
as well, that one, untrammelled by name-and-form, with naught as his
own — no pains befall."

## REFUTATIONS

### The commentary on the Kimmulaka sutta
"They have narrowed down the meaning of the set of answers recommended
by the Buddha by limiting its application to wholesome mental states.
The occurrence of such terms as chanda, sati, samadhi and panna had
probably led them to believe that the entire questionnaire is on the
subject of wholesome mental states. But this is a serious underestimation."

### The chanda = skilful reading
"The term chanda is one that has both good and bad connotations. In
such contexts as chandaraga and chandajam agham, it is suggestive of
craving as the cause of all suffering."
"The way of practice to be followed for the abandonment of desire is
the four bases of success... The brahmin is puzzled: 'that one abandons
desire by desire itself is an impossibility'."

### Taṇha used to abandon taṇha
"Depending on craving one should abandon craving." (Analayo: SĀ 564.)
"The kind of craving meant here is not something crude. It is specifically
explained there that it is the longing arising in one for the attainment
of arahanthood on hearing that someone has already attained it."

## PASS 2 FINDS

- The gem in the rubbish heap parable is the complete mechanism of how
  concepts are born from attention — one of the best parables in the
  series.
- The Nagasamala episode — the dancing woman — is the practice of yoniso
  manasikara in a single story. Even wrong concentration can be redirected
  by radical attention.
- The rainbow in the dewdrop — "the dewdrop itself is the meditation
  object" — is the key insight for the whole early-suttas approach: any
  object can yield the Dhamma if seen correctly.
- The Brahmajala net — "contact is the mesh" — is the specific answer to
  what holds the sixty-two wrong views together.
- The Kimmulaka chanda argument — using craving to abandon craving — is
  the middle-path technique applied to the very fuel of samsara.
- The two views (lag behind / overreach) is the Itivuttaka summary of
  the eternalism-nihilism problem.

## PASS 3: HOLES AND CONNECTIONS

- **Hole:** The relationship between the gem in the rubbish heap (Sermon
  9) and the gem in the pond (Ummagga-jataka, also Sermon 9) is
  established but the distinction between them could be clearer. The
  rubbish heap gem is made by attention; the pond gem is a reflection
  in consciousness.
- **Hole:** The perception of impermanence is established as leading to
  not-self, but the practice method for developing it is only hinted at
  (seven suns, world destruction). The maranasati practices would fill
  this gap.
- **Connection to Sermon 8:** The rope/cord simile (grasping in the
  middle) is the mechanism; the gem in the rubbish heap is the result.
  The Dhammapada "even oneself is not one's own" connects both.
- **Connection to Sermon 6:** The dog on the plank (looking creates the
  reflection) is the same as the gem in the pond (the pond reflects the
  gem). Both show that the object is created by the looking.
- **Connection to Sermon 7:** The Brahmajala net and the sixty-two views
  are the dark cinema screen. The light that dissolves them is the
  anidassana vinnana.
- **Connection to VBT pack:** The gem in the rubbish heap maps to the
  concept-deconstruction practice — see the gem as charcoal. The Nagasamala
  episode maps to the Bahiya practice — even a dancing woman can be the
  object of radical attention.
