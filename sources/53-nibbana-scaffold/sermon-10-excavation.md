# SERMON 10 — EXCAVATION

Source: Nibbana-10.pdf (BCBS/Dharma Seed, free). 8115 words.

---

## SECTION 1: WHAT NIBBANA IS

### Bhuta vs bhava/vibhava (the Buddha's word choice)
"The Buddha has interposed this word bhuta between the dichotomous
terms bhava and vibhava. In the contemporary society, these two terms
were used to denote the existence and the destruction of a soul."
"The term bhuta, on the other hand, is not amenable to such a usage. It
has the passive sense of something that has become. Like that reflection
mentioned earlier, it conveys the idea of being produced by causes and
conditions."
"Itivuttaka: born, become, arisen, made up, prepared, unstable. These
words are suggestive of some artificiality."

### Bhavanirodha = cessation of the process, not destruction of a soul
"From the point of view of the Dhamma the cessation of existence amounts
to a stopping of the process of becoming, by the removal of the causes
leading to it, namely ignorance and craving. It is, in effect, the
cessation of suffering itself."
"Those who held on to the annihilationist view entertained the hope
that their view itself entitled them to their cherished goal. But it
was in vain, because the ignorance, craving, and grasping within them
created for them the five groups of grasping, again and again."

### The carrot before the donkey
"A donkey drags a wagon when a carrot is projected towards it from the
wagon. The journey of beings in samsara is something like that."
"Bhava is always bent forward, so much so that we get an infinite
progression."

### Asmi is more basic than atthi
"The Buddha pointed out that asmi, or 'am', is more basic than the
usage of 'is' and 'is not'. The most elementary concept is asmi, or
'am'. Hence the term asmimana, the conceit 'am'."
"It is like the central peg from which all measurings and surveyings of
the world start, since the word mana in asmimana also means 'measuring'."

### The grammar of existence
"If we say 'there is something', someone will pose the question 'where
is it?' It should be either here or there or yonder. Now, if it is here,
how does that place become a 'here'? That is where I am."
"'I am', 'you are', and 'he is'. One can speak of the existence of
something relative to a viewpoint represented by 'am' or 'I am'."
"This conceit 'am' is, as it were, the origin of the whole world, the
origin of the world of grammar."

## SECTION 2: HOW THE MECHANISM WORKS

### Upadaya = the absolutive of upadana (relativity)
"Upadaya is the absolutive form of upadana, it has the implication of
something relative... This holding on creates a certain relationship,
which may be technically termed a relativity. The two stand relative to
each other."
"The prefix upa in upadana conveys the sense of proximity or nearness...
So the totality of existence, from the point of view of Dhamma, is
dependent on a holding on, or a grasping on."

### The mirror simile (Punna Mantaniputta, Khandha-samyutta)
"It is with dependence, friend Ananda, that the notion 'am' occurs, not
without dependence. With dependence on form... on feeling... on
perception... on preparations... on consciousness does the notion 'am'
occur."
"Just as a woman or a man, youthful and fond of adornment, in looking
at her or his facial image in a mirror or in a bowl filled with pure,
clear, clean water, would be seeing it with dependence and not without
dependence."
Analayo parallel: SĀ 261.
"It is when one looks into a mirror that one suddenly becomes self-
conscious... Even if one already has no such notion, the moment one looks
into a mirror one is suddenly reminded of it, as if to exclaim: 'Ah,
here I am!'"

### The twenty modes of personality view
"As regards form, it is fourfold: He regards form as self, or self as
possessing form, or form as in self, or self as in form. It is the same
with the other four groups. In this way, the personality view is
altogether twenty-fold."
"All this comes about due to the ignorance that name-and-form is only a
reflection, like that facial image."

### Consciousness as double-acting
"The magical illusion created by consciousness is so complete that it
is capable of playing a dual role, as in double acting. Because it
reflects, like a mirror, consciousness itself is grasped, just as one
grasps the mirror. Not only the reflection of the mirror, but the mirror
itself is grasped."

### Adhivacana and patigha contact (Mahanidana catechism)
"Phassa, or contact, is a sort of hybrid, carrying with it the
implications of both adhivacanasamphassa and patighasamphassa. That is
to say, it partakes of the character of name, as suggested by
adhivacanasamphassa, as well as that of form, indicated by
patighasamphassa."
"Adhivacana, synonym, nirutti, nomenclature, and pannatti, designation,
are part and parcel of linguistic usage."
"Nirutti emphasises the explanatory or expository function of language,
adhivacana its symbolic and metaphorical character, while pannatti brings
out its dependence on convention."
"Form, or rupa, has a striking quality, while name, or nama, has a
descriptive quality. Phassa, or contact, is a hybrid of these two."

### The carpenter's planks
"A carpenter might speak of two planks touching each other as if they
can actually touch and feel. The concept of touch, even when it is
attributed to inanimate objects, is the outcome of attention, in this
case the attention of the carpenter."
"Wherever the carpenter's interest went, his attention discovered and
picked up the thing."

### What am I touching?
"If I ask what I am touching now, one might say that I am touching the
palm leaf fan in my hand. But suppose I put away the fan and ask again
what I am touching now, one might find it difficult to answer...
Consciousness follows in the wake of attention. Whatever my attention
picks up, of that I am conscious."

### Rupa etymology: ruppati = affected
"And what do you call rupa? It is affected, that is why it is called
rupa. Affected by what? Affected by cold, affected by heat, affected by
hunger, affected by thirst, affected by contact with gadflies,
mosquitoes, wind, sun and serpents."
Analayo parallel: SĀ 46. "Since it can resist and can break, it is
called the bodily form aggregate of clinging."
"What is known as the form-group is one vast wound with nine apertures.
This wound is affected when it is touched by cold and heat."

### The tangle within and tangle without (Jata Sutta)
"Where name and form, as well as resistance and perception of form, are
completely cut off, it is there that the tangle gets snapped."
"The deepest point of the entire formula of paticca samuppada is
traceable to the interrelation that obtains between name and form on
the one hand, and between name-and-form and consciousness on the other."

## SECTION 3: WHAT KEEPS THE MECHANISM RUNNING

### The two views (Itivuttaka, continued)
"Gods and men delight in existence, they are attached to existence...
When Dhamma is being preached to them for the cessation of existence,
their minds do not reach out towards it."
"Being troubled, ashamed, and disgusted of existence as such, some
delight in non-existence."

### The mirror creates the self
"Whether one has a liking or a dislike for what one sees, one gets the
notion 'this is me'."

### The grammatical framework
"There is a certain deep significance in the word yathabhuta. In
contradistinction to the two words bhava and vibhava, the word bhuta
has some peculiarity of its own."

---

## SECTION 4: WHAT THE MECHANISM BUILDS

### The perception of form
"It is due to that 'striking against', or resistance. Perception of
form arises, for instance, when gadflies and mosquitoes land on this
body."
"We are only trying to measure and gauge the four great primaries with
this human frame. We can never ever comprehend fully the gamut of these
four great primaries. But we are trying to understand them through this
human frame in a way that is meaningful to our lives."

### Kalahavivada: the wrong conclusion
"Having seen the existence and destruction of material forms, a man in
this world comes to a conclusion." The conclusion: absolute existence
and absolute non-existence. "The Buddha has pointed out that such
absolute views are a result of an incorrect understanding about form."

---

## SECTION 5: HOW TO SEE THROUGH IT

### Yathabhuta nana dassana (knowledge and vision of things as they are)
"It is by offering a knowledge and vision of things as they are, in
place of those two views of existence and non-existence."
"He made known to the world the true knowledge and vision that name-and-
form is merely an image reflected on consciousness."

### The practice: see the reflection as reflection
"The eternalist, because of his narcissistic self-love, gets attached
to his own self image and lags behind... It is like the narcissistic
attempt to embrace one's own image in water out of self love."
"The annihilationist view leads to an attitude of escapism, like that
of one who is obsessed by his own shadow. One cannot outstrip one's
own shadow."

---

## SECTION 6: WHAT REMAINS

### The tangle snapped
"Where name and form, as well as resistance and perception of form, are
completely cut off, it is there that the tangle gets snapped."
"The true purpose should be the release of one's mind from this
perception of form."

## REFUTATIONS

### The materialist rupa analysis
"Materialistic philosophers went on analysing matter, until they ended
up with an atomism and grasped a heap of concepts. The analysis of
matter thus precipitated a grasping of a mass of concepts. Whether one
grasps a pole or a mole, it is a grasping all the same."
"The real problem at issue is not that of form, but of the perception
of form."

### The absolute existence/non-existence views
"The two extreme views are a result of an incorrect understanding about
form... Due to a misconception about the perception of form, the world
inclines towards the two extreme views."

## PASS 2 FINDS

- The mirror simile (Punna Mantaniputta) is the most precise explanation
  of how the self-sense arises — the "Ah, here I am!" moment when you
  look in a mirror. This is the dependent origination of asmimana in
  one image.
- The rupa etymology (ruppati = affected) is a major philological move —
  Nanananda shows that rupa means "that which is affected" not "matter."
  This is the foundation of the whole early-suttas reading of form.
- The adhivacana/patigha contact — the hybrid of naming and resistance —
  is the precise mechanism of how contact arises from name-and-form.
- The carpenter's planks — touch attributed to inanimate objects is the
  outcome of attention — connects the Kimmulaka "born of attention" to
  the Mahanidana contact catechism.
- The twenty modes of personality view — the four per aggregate — is
  the complete catalogue of self-view. All twenty are the mirror error.
- The "vast wound with nine apertures" line on the body is one of the
  most visceral images in the series.

## PASS 3: HOLES AND CONNECTIONS

- **Hole:** The practice for seeing through the twenty modes is only
  implied (the mirror simile) — the actual satipatthana method for
  seeing the self-image as reflection is not worked through here.
- **Hole:** The relationship between the adhivacana/patigha contact and
  the practice of bare sensing (Bahiya) is established but not connected
  explicitly.
- **Connection to Sermon 9:** The gem in the pond (reflection) is the
  same as the mirror simile here. The dog on the plank (Sermon 6) is
  the same insight.
- **Connection to Sermon 3:** The Mahanidana catechism (consciousness +
  nama-rupa) is continued with the contact catechism.
- **Connection to Sermon 1:** The "nama is name" thesis is extended here
  — adhivacana is the same as the naming quality of nama.
- **Connection to VBT pack:** The mirror simile maps to the body-as-
  consciousness practice — the self you see in the mirror is a reflection,
  not a thing. The adhivacana/patigha contact maps to the inner-outer
  spaces practice.
