# SERMON 14 — EXCAVATION

Source: Nibbana-14.pdf (BCBS/Dharma Seed, free). 7754 words.

---

## SECTION 1: WHAT NIBBANA IS

### The Dhatuvibhanga grand finale (sage stilled)
"The tides of conceiving do not sweep over one who stands upon these
foundations, and when the tides of conceiving no longer sweep over him
he is called a sage at peace."
"Conceiving is a disease, conceiving is a tumour, conceiving is a dart.
By overcoming all conceivings, bhikkhu, one is called a sage at peace."
"And the sage at peace is not born, does not age, does not die; he is
not shaken and does not yearn. For there is nothing present in him by
which he might be born."
Analayo parallel: MĀ 162. Tibetan parallel also noted.

### The Udana: not-born, not-become
"Monks, there is a not-born, a not-become, a not-made, a not-compounded.
If that were not, there would be no stepping out here from what is born,
become, made and compounded."
"The terms ajatam, not-born, abhutam, not-become, akatam, not-made, and
asankhatam, not-compounded, are all epithets for Nibbana."

### The island (Kappa, revisited)
"Owning naught, grasping naught: the isle is this, none else besides.
Nibbana — that is how I call that isle, wherein decay is decayed and
death is dead."
"The ideal of owning naught and grasping naught is itself Nibbana,
and nothing else."

### The Bahiya instruction (the tersest discourse)
"In the seen there will be just the seen, in the heard just the heard,
in the sensed just the sensed, in the cognized just the cognized."
"When you are not by it, you are not in it. And when you are not in it,
you are neither here, nor there, nor in between. This itself is the end
of suffering."
"No sooner had the Buddha finished his exhortation, the ascetic Bahiya
attained arahant-hood then and there."

## SECTION 2: HOW THE MECHANISM WORKS

### The elements meditation (Dhatuvibhanga)
"That which is the internal earth element, and that which is the external
earth element, they are both just the earth element itself. And that
should be seen as it is with right wisdom: 'this is not mine', 'I am
not this', 'this is not my self'."
Analayo parallel: MĀ 162.
"It is this first principle that is truly important and not any kind
of atomic theory. This resolution of the internal/external conflict has
in it the secret of stopping the samsaric vortex."

### The vortex of reiterated becoming
"It is due to the very discrimination between an 'internal' and an
'external' that this samsaric vortex is kept going. In the case of a
vortex, what is found inside and outside is simply water. But all the
same there is such a vehement speed and activity."

### The mirror and the three views
"Just as by looking at a mirror one may like or dislike the image
appearing on it, these three points of view give rise to various pervert
notions. All this because of the perpetuation of the distinction between
an internal and an external."

### The three perversions
"Sanñavipallasa, cittavipallasa and ditthivipallasa give rise to a mass
of concepts of an imaginary nature. The entire mass of suffering...
are basically of a mental origin."

### The tree-sellers analogy (standpoint)
"If five men trading in buds, leaves, flowers, fruits and wood respectively
are asked whether the tree is too young or too old, the answers given
might differ according to the individual standpoint. It turns out to be
a difference of viewpoint."

### Atammayata (non-identification)
"This term tam-maya needs to be clarified. It is derived from tad maya,
literally 'made of that' or 'of that stuff'. When one has cravings,
conceits and views about something, he practically becomes one with it
due to that very grasping."
"The principle of non-identification has been recommended by the Buddha
even with regard to the attainment of the sphere of neither-perception-
nor-non-perception."
"That ardent sage who has touched the extinction of birth... is aloof
in regard to all phenomena."

## SECTION 3: WHAT KEEPS THE MECHANISM RUNNING

### The tides of imaginings
"Steadied whereon the tides of imaginings no longer occur in him."
"The idea behind this occurrence of tides of imaginings is quite often
represented by the concept of asava, influx."

### Consciousness dwelling
"The form element, householder, is the abode of consciousness. The terms
oka, niketa and nivesana are synonymous, meaning 'abode', 'home', or
'dwelling place'."
"The nature of consciousness in general is to abide or dwell in. That
nonmanifestative consciousness, however, has got rid of the tendency to
abide or dwell in."

### The three kinds of feeling
"The sage observes these three kinds of feelings, pleasant, unpleasant
and neither-unpleasant-nor-pleasant, as they arise and cease dependent
on specific contacts, thereby gradually bringing the mind to equanimity."
"He understands it to be impermanent, uninvolved, unrejoiced."

## SECTION 4: WHAT THE MECHANISM BUILDS

### Space as sankhata
"This mode of contemplation of space betrays the fact that space is
also something made up, or prepared, sankhata. Whatever is prepared,
sankhata, is thought out and mind made."
"The Buddha proclaimed that there is only one asankhata, unprepared,
that is Nibbana. But later philosophers confounded the issue by taking
space also to be asankhata."

### Form and space as picture and background
"Form and space are relative to each other like a picture and its
background. A picture is viewed against its background, which is relative
to it."

### Consciousness as discrimination
"It cognizes, it cognizes, that is why consciousness is called."
"Now that the five elements have been totally removed, the residual
function of consciousness amounts to a discrimination between the three
grades of feelings."

## SECTION 5: HOW TO SEE THROUGH IT

### The elements meditation (practice)
"The internal earth element and the external earth element are both
just the earth element itself... 'This is not mine', 'I am not this',
'this is not my self'."
"Having seen thus with right wisdom, he becomes dejected with the earth
element, he detaches his mind from the earth element."

### The Bahiya training (the tersest practice)
"In the seen there will be just the seen... Thus should you train
yourself."
"The basic principle in this training seems to be the discipline to
stop short at bare awareness."

### The Talaputa aspiration
"When shall I weigh as equal all these Limitless things both internal
and external, twigs, grass, creepers and these aggregates?"
"It is at the stage of arahant-hood that the internal and the external
appear alike."

### The Adhimutta connection
"When one sees through wisdom, the world to be comparable to grass and
twigs."

## SECTION 6: WHAT REMAINS

### The unshakeable deliverance
"The deliverance in Nibbana is called akuppa cetovimutti, the unshakeable
deliverance of the mind. All other deliverances of the mind, known to
the world, are shakeable."
"The arahant Thera Venerable Adhimutta fearlessly made his declaration
to the bandits."

### The arahant and birth
"The sage has no postulate of existence. Since there is no existence,
there is no birth. Because there is no birth, there is no decay."
"Jarā implies both growth and decay. It is after setting a limit that
we speak of a process of 'decay', after 'growth'. That limit varies
according to our individual standpoint."

## REFUTATIONS

### The commentator on idha ("here")
"The commentator pays little attention to the word idha, 'here', in this
passage. The fact that there is a possibility here and now of stepping
out from the state of being born, become, made and compounded, surely
deserves emphasis."
"Most of the scholars emphasize the opening word atthi, 'there is', to
prove that Nibbana is some form of reality absolutely existing somewhere."

### Space as asankhata
"Later philosophers confounded the issue by taking space also to be
asankhata. They seem to have ignored its relation to the mind in
regarding causes and conditions as purely external things."

## PASS 2 FINDS

- The Bahiya sutta appears here in full — the tersest discourse in the
  canon. Bahiya attains arahantship on hearing it. This is the practice
  that the whole series has been building toward.
- The elements meditation (internal = external) is the specific practice
  for resolving the internal/external split — the root of the samsaric
  vortex.
- Atammayata (non-identification) is a precise term for the practice
  — "made of that" — you become one with what you grasp. Don't identify,
  even with the formless attainments.
- The tree-sellers analogy (standpoint determines the answer) connects
  to Sermon 4's tree-seller and the Kosiya rice-cakes.
- The space-as-sankhata point is a major philosophical move — space is
  prepared, thought out, mind made. Only nibbana is asankhata.
