# SERMON 16 — EXCAVATION

Source: Nibbana-16.pdf (BCBS/Dharma Seed, free). 8616 words.

---

## SECTION 1: WHAT NIBBANA IS

### The asset-less (Mucalinda-vagga)
"Touched by pain in village or in forest, think not in terms of oneself
or others. Touches can touch one, because of assets. How can touches
touch him, who is asset-less?"
"Upadhi in a deeper sense stands for the fivefold grasping groups. The
worldling imagines those groups of grasping as things grasped and
deposited."

### Relinquishment through understanding (not willpower)
"It is not so much a deliberate giving up of assets, as a sequential
liquidation. The vanity of all these assets has to be seen through by
the light of wisdom."
"In explaining the phenomenon of relinquishment with reference to the
cinema, we described how the assets accumulated in the minds of the
audience are automatically abandoned when the cinema hall gets lit up."

### The three temporary awakenings
1. Cinema: lights on, scenes fade, then dark again, influxes return.
2. Devalued currency: notes become paper, but one still runs after valid
   notes.
3. Dream: waking from a dream, but going back to sleep brings more dreams.
"The awakening in Nibbana is not of such a temporary character. Because
all the influxes that lead one into the samsaric slumber are made extinct."

### The anantarika samadhi (the immediate concentration)
"That pure concentration, which the Supremely Awakened One extolled,
that concentration which the Noble Ones call 'immediate', there is no
concentration comparable to it."
"This incomparable and extraordinary concentration has given rise to
many problems concerning the concept of Nibbana."

### The three faculties
1. Anannatannassamitindriya: "I shall know what has not been fully
   known" — knowledge of extinction.
2. Annindriya: the final knowledge of certitude.
3. Annatavindriya: the knowledge that prompts "unshakeable is my
   deliverance."

### The Sandha sutta: musing not dependent on anything
"He muses not dependent on earth, water, fire, air, the sphere of
infinite space... whatever is seen, heard, sensed, cognized... and yet
he does muse."
"The devas along with Indra, Brahma, and Pajapati worship the excellent
thoroughbred person from afar: 'We ourselves do not understand what you
meditate in dependence on.'"
Analayo parallel: SĀ 926.

### The four negations resolved
"Not percipient through perception, not percipient through disturbed
perception, not altogether without perception, not percipient of what
has vanished: form vanishes for one who has so attained."
"This verse points to the arahattaphalasamadhi. The vacant gaze is
neither conscious, nor unconscious, nor non-conscious, nor devoid of
consciousness."

### The vacant gaze (appatittham, appavattam, anarammanam)
"The vacant gaze is not established anywhere. It has no existence and
it is object-less."
"It is a gaze that sees the cessation of the object, a gaze that
penetrates the object."

### The birds in the sky
"Those who have no accumulations, and understood fully the subject of
food, and whose feeding ground is the void and the signless — their
track is hard to trace, like that of birds in the sky."
"Gocara means 'pasture'. Their true pasture is the arahattaphalasamadhi.
Once they are well within this pasture, neither gods nor Brahmas nor
Mara can find them."

### Nandiya's challenge to Mara
"The monk whose mind is always bright, and gone to the fruit of
arahant-hood, should you dare to challenge that monk, O Blackie, you
only come to grief."

## SECTION 2: HOW THE MECHANISM WORKS

### Six narrow beams (revisited)
"The worldling discerns the world with the help of six narrow beams of
light, namely the six sense-bases. When the superior lustre of wisdom
arises, those six sense-bases go down."
"The cessation of the six sense-bases does not mean that one does not
see anything. What one sees then is voidness."

### Vibhuta = gone, not manifest
"The verb vibhuta seems to have a negative sense... The perception of
earth is gone for him."
"The Culaniddesa paraphrases the term with the words vigata, atikkanta,
samatikkanta, vitivatta — gone, transcended, fully transcended."

### The theme verse as the arahant's object
"Herein, Ananda, a monk is thus conscious: 'This is peaceful, this is
excellent, namely the stilling of all preparations, the relinquishment
of all assets, the destruction of craving, detachment, cessation,
extinction.'"
"This does not mean that the arahant in his samadhi goes on reciting
the formula. It means that the arahant reverts to the realization he
has already won."

## SECTION 3: WHAT KEEPS THE MECHANISM RUNNING

### The worldling's horizon
"There is a horizon at the furthest end of our range of vision. Standing
by the seaside, one gazes upon a horizon where the earth and sky meet.
The arahant's range of vision has no such horizon. That is why it is
called ananta, endless."

### Papanca as transaction
"The cause of all conceptual proliferation is contact. The arahants
understood this by their insight into the fact that the seen, the heard,
the sensed and the cognized are simply so many collocations of conditions
which come together for a moment due to contact, only to break up."

---

## SECTION 4: WHAT THE MECHANISM BUILDS

### The world as six beams through a curtain
"The world of the six sense-bases reveals itself to be no more than six
narrow beams of light, appearing through a solidly thick curtain, namely
the darkness of delusion."

### The sun, moon, and stars need darkness
"The sun, the moon and the stars shine precisely because of the presence
of darkness. In the non-manifestative consciousness there is absolutely
no darkness for them to shine forth."

---

## SECTION 5: HOW TO SEE THROUGH IT

### Reverting to the realization
"The arahant reverts to or re-attains the realization he has already won
through the lustre of wisdom. That is what one has to understand by the
saying that the arahant attends to Nibbana as his object."

### The three deliverances
"Animitta (signless), appanihita (undirected), sunnata (void) — the
arahant is now looking at the object with a penetrative gaze. That is
why it is not possible to say what he is looking at."

---

## SECTION 6: WHAT REMAINS

### Mara cannot find the arahant
"Even gods and Brahmas are unable to find out the object of the arahant's
mind when he is in the phalasamapatti. Mara can never discover it."

### The end of all
"When the arahant passes away, at the last moment of his life span, he
brings his mind to this arahattaphalasamadhi. Then not even Mara can
trace him. There is no possibility of a rebirth and that is the end of
all."

## REFUTATIONS

### The commentary on vibhuta
"The commentator paraphrases it by pakata, which means 'clearly manifest'.
This interpretation seems to distort the meaning of the entire passage.
The commentator seems to have missed the true import."

### The commentary on gati (birds in the sky)
"For the commentary gati is the place where the arahant goes after death.
But taken in conjunction with the simile used, gati obviously means the
path, padam, taken by the birds in the sky. It is the path they went by
that is referred to."

## PASS 2 FINDS

- The three temporary awakenings (cinema, devalued notes, dream) vs the
  permanent awakening (nibbana) is the clearest distinction yet.
- The Sandha sutta (musing not dependent on anything, yet does muse) is
  the most precise description of the arahant's concentration.
- The vacant gaze resolved: the four negations of the Kalahavivada verse
  point to arahattaphalasamadhi, not to a formless attainment.
- The birds-in-the-sky gati = path, not destination. The commentary got
  this wrong.
- Nandiya's challenge to Mara is the most vivid declaration of
  fearlessness in the series.

## PASS 3: HOLES AND CONNECTIONS

- **Hole:** The sa-upadisesa vs anupadisesa Nibbana-dhatu is mentioned
  but not fully discussed — that's Sermon 19's job.
- **Connection to Sermon 15:** The Bahiya conclusion verse is resolved

  as the six beams going down.
- **Connection to Sermon 7:** The anidassana vinnana is the vacant gaze.
  The Kevaddha verse and the Sandha sutta are parallel descriptions.
- **Connection to VBT pack:** The vacant gaze IS the signless practice
  at its deepest. The birds-in-the-sky is the observer-inquiry practice
  — the path leaves no trace.
