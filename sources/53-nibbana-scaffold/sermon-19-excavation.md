# SERMON 19 — EXCAVATION

Source: Nibbana-19.pdf (BCBS/Dharma Seed, free). 7717 words.

## SECTION 1: WHAT NIBBANA IS

### The oil lamp simile (Dhatuvibhanga)
"Just as an oil lamp burns depending on oil and the wick, and when
that oil and the wick are used up, if it does not get any more of
these, it is extinguished from lack of fuel."
Analayo parallel: MĀ 162.
"When he feels a feeling limited to the body, he understands 'I feel
a feeling limited to the body'... he understands 'on the breaking up
of this body, before life becomes extinct, even here itself, all that
is felt, not being delighted in, will become cool'."

### The two Nibbana-elements defined in brief
"Sa-upadisesa: the arahant is free from fresh graspings, lust, hate
and delusions do not flare up. But so long as he has to bear the burden
of this organic combination, the arahant has to experience certain
afflictions."
"Anupadisesa: at the last moment of the arahant's life, even this
organic body that had been grasped has to be abandoned."

### Anupada parinibbana (the arahattaphalasamadhi itself)
"Anupada parinibbana refers to the arahattaphalasamadhi as such, for
which even other terms like anupada vimokkha are also applied."
"Friend, it is for the sake of perfect Nibbana without grasping that
the holy life is lived under the Exalted One." (Rathavinita, Punna
Mantaniputta)
Analayo parallel: MĀ 9.

### The floral design (sankhata becomes asankhata)
"Saṅkhata, or the prepared, is like a floral design. This prepared
floral design, which is bhava, is made up with the help of the glue
of craving, the tangles of views and the knots of conceits. If one
removes the glue, disentangles the tangles and unties the knots, the
saṅkhata itself becomes asankhata, then and there."

### Dabba Mallaputta's self-cremation (full)
"He went up into the sky and, sitting cross-legged, aroused the
concentration of the fire element and, rising from it, attained
parinibbana. As his body thus miraculously self-cremated burnt in the
sky, it left no ashes or soot."
"Body broke up, perceptions ceased, all feelings cooled off, preparations
calmed down, consciousness came to an end."
"Just as in the case of a fire blazing like a block of iron in point of
compactness, when it gradually calms down, no path it goes by can be
traced."

## SECTION 2: HOW THE MECHANISM WORKS

### The assaddho pun verse (Dhammapada)
"Assaddho akataññū ca, sandhicchedo ca yo naro, hatāvakāso vantāso,
sa ve uttamaporiso."
Surface meaning: "That faithless ungrateful man, who is a housebreaker,
who is hopeless and greedy of vomit, he indeed is the man supreme."
Deeper meaning: "That man who has outgrown faith, as he is a knower of
the unmade, who has sundered all shackles to existence and destroyed
all possibilities of rebirth, who has spewed out all desires, he indeed
is the man supreme."

### General Siha's giving fruits (the knowledge/faith principle)
"Those four fruits of giving, visible here and now, I do not believe
out of faith, because I myself know them to be so. But about rebirth
after death, I do not know, and here I go by faith."
"Where there is knowledge born of personal experience, there is no
need of faith. Faith is displaced by knowledge of realization. It is
where one has no such experiential knowledge that faith comes in."

### The Naga verse (giving up the body)
"The one who has abandoned lust, hate and delusion and is influx-free,
that elephant of a man, on giving up his body, will attain full
appeasement, being influx-free."

### The Vedana-samyutta verses
"A disciple of the Buddha, mindful, concentrated, comprehending clearly,
understands feelings and the origin of feelings, where they finally
cease... With the destruction of feelings, that monk is hungerless and
perfectly extinguished."

## SECTION 3: WHAT KEEPS THE MECHANISM RUNNING

### The arahant's after-death state is assured here
"Aranthants have already directly realized, well in time, their after
death state. Not only have they gone through the experience of
extinguishment here and now, but they are also assured of the fact that
this extinguishment is irreversible even after death."

### The sanditthika/samparayika principle
"Where there is knowledge born of personal experience, there is no need
of faith. Faith is displaced by knowledge of realization."

## SECTION 4: WHAT THE MECHANISM BUILDS

### The tortoise and frog parables refuted
"In order to determine what sort of a thing land is, the fishes go on
asking the tortoise a number of questions based on various qualities of
water. To each question the tortoise has to reply in the negative."
"Those negative answers are compared to the strings of negative terms
found in the sutta passages dealing with arahattaphalasamadhi."
"Such facile explanations contradict the deeper teachings on the
cessation of existence, dependent arising and not self."

### The Buddha's response to annihilation charges
"The Buddha did not in any way incline towards eternalism in order to
defend himself. Instead, he drew attention to the three signata and
the four noble truths and solved the whole problem."
"Formerly as well as now, O monks, I point out only a suffering and a
cessation of that suffering."

## SECTION 6: WHAT REMAINS

### The Naga on the island
"When faced with death, or the pain of death, it gives protection.
It provides shelter. It is the island within easy reach. It is the
refuge and the resort. It is the security, and above all the deathless."
"This deathlessness they experience in this very world."

### The Anuruddha verses on the Buddha's death
"There were no in-breaths and out-breaths, for that steadfastly such-like
one of firm mind. With a mind fully alert, he bore up the pain. The
deliverance of the mind was like the extinguishment of a torch."
"Peace comes first and the passing away later."

## REFUTATIONS

### The commentary on anupadisesa as a place
"There is no justification whatsoever for taking anupadisesa Nibbanadhatu
as a place of eternal rest for the arahants after death. The deathlessness
of Nibbana is to be experienced in this world itself."

### The commentary on the arabbha term
"The commentary says the Buddha passed away with the idea 'I will go to
that state of peace'. But according the sutta proper, peace comes first
and the passing away later."

## PASS 2 FINDS

- The floral design (sankhata → asankhata by removing glue/tangles/knots)
  is the most precise mechanism for how cessation happens in one moment.
- The assaddho pun verse is the most playful — the surface reading sounds
  blasphemous, the deep reading describes the arahant.
- General Siha's giving fruits is the most practical: faith is displaced
  by knowledge where you have it, needed where you don't.
- The tortoise and frog parables are the modern attempts to defend nibbana
  as a place — Nanananda demolishes them.
- Dabba's self-cremation with no ashes is the ultimate symbol of
  extinction — nothing left to venerate.

## PASS 3: HOLES AND CONNECTIONS

- **Hole:** The relationship between the oil lamp's "last feeling" and
  the practice of attending to feeling is established but not worked
  through. The satipatthana feeling contemplation is the practice.
- **Connection to Sermon 18:** The two Nibbana-elements are continued.
  The embers going out is the oil lamp running out of oil.
- **Connection to Sermon 17:** The twig-fire simile is the same as the
  oil lamp. The arahattaphalasamadhi is the unshakeable deliverance
  that the arahant resorts to at death.
- **Connection to VBT pack:** The floral design practice IS the VBT
  concept-deconstruction practice — remove the glue and the design
  becomes unprepared.
