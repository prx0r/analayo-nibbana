# SERMON 18 — EXCAVATION

Source: Nibbana-18.pdf (BCBS/Dharma Seed, free). 7275 words.

---

## SECTION 1: WHAT NIBBANA IS

### The lotus simile (water on a lotus leaf)
"Just as a drop of water on a lotus leaf, or water that taints not the
lotus petal, so the sage unattached remains, in regard to what is seen,
heard and sensed."
"The distance between the mundane and the supramundane is the same as
that between the lotus leaf and the drop of water on it."

### The Bahuna ten things
"Detached from form, feeling, perceptions, preparations, consciousness,
birth, decay, death, pains, defilements, the Tathagata dwells with a
mind unrestricted. Just as a blue lotus, born in the water, rises well
above the water and remains unsmeared."

### The two Nibbana-elements (Itivuttaka, full)
"The Nibbana element with residual clinging: the arahant's five sense
faculties still remain, he experiences likes and dislikes. That
extirpation of lust, hate and delusion in him is the Nibbana element
with residual clinging."
"The Nibbana element without residual clinging: in him, here itself,
all what is felt will cool off, not being delighted in."
"One is of relevance to the here and now, with residual clinging, yet
with tentacles to becoming snapped. The other is of relevance to the
hereafter, for in it surcease all forms of becoming."

### The embers going out (Naga sutta)
"As a huge blazing fire, with no more firewood added, goes down to reach
a state of calm. Embers smouldering could be reckoned, so long as they
last, as almost 'extinguished'."
"To the extent that they are no longer hungering for more fuel and are
not emitting flames, they may as well be reckoned as 'extinguished'."

### Dabba Mallaputta's self-cremation
"Using his jhanic powers, he simply employs the internal fire element
to cremate the body he has already discarded. Outwardly it might appear
as an act of self-immolation. But this is not so."
"This is the Buddha's extraordinary solution to overcoming death — a
solution that completely outwits Mara."

## SECTION 2: HOW THE MECHANISM WORKS

### The Nalaka riddle (not going twice, not going once)
"They go not twice to the farther shore, nor yet is it to be reckoned
a going once."
"The arahant has the ability to detach himself from the world from
time to time and re-attain to arahattaphalasamadhi. From the worldling's
point of view, the arahant has come back to the world."

### The Sariputta sense-faculty statement
"The Exalted One sees forms with the eye, but there is no desire or
attachment in him, well freed in mind is the Exalted One."
"He takes his food experiencing the taste, though not experiencing any
attachment to the taste."

### The Chandikaputta refutation
"Even if many forms cognizable by the eye come within the range of
vision, they do not overwhelm his mind; his mind remains unalloyed,
steady and unmoved, he sees its passing away."

## SECTION 3: WHAT KEEPS THE MECHANISM RUNNING

### The snake slough (revisited)
"That monk forsakes the hither and the thither, even as the snake its
skin that doth wither."
"The skin has served its purpose, but now it is redundant."

### Oraparam (this shore and the farther shore)
"It stands for the duality implicit in 'internal' and 'external', 'one's
own' and 'another's', as well as 'this shore' and 'the farther shore'."

### The banknote analogy
"Concepts current in the world, like banknotes in transaction, are
reckoned as valid so long as cravings, conceits and views bound with
them are there. They are no longer valid when these are gone."

---

## SECTION 4: WHAT THE MECHANISM BUILDS

### The raft simile
"The Dhamma is comparable to a raft, which is for crossing over and
not for grasping. Well knowing the Dhamma to be comparable to a raft,
you should abandon even the good things, more so the bad things."

### The Uragasutta overreaching/lagging
"Speculation about the future is an overreaching and repentance over
the past is a lagging behind. To transcend both these tendencies is to
get beyond all proliferation."

---

## SECTION 5: HOW TO SEE THROUGH IT

### The lotus practice (untainted in the midst)
"Just as a blue lotus, born in the water, rises well above the water
and remains unsmeared — so the Tathagata dwells detached from ten
things with a mind unrestricted."

### The taste practice
"He takes his food experiencing the taste, though not experiencing
any attachment to the taste."

---

## SECTION 6: WHAT REMAINS

### The cooling off
"In him, here itself, all what is felt will cool off, not being
delighted in."
"This cooling off happens just before death, without igniting another
spark of life."

### The Nalaka paradox resolved
"The arahant has no such distinction [between this shore and the
farther shore]. Once gone beyond, the emancipated one has no more use
of these concepts."

## REFUTATIONS

### The commentary on samparayika
"Samparaya means after the breaking up of the aggregates. The implication
is that it refers to the arahant's after death state. Are we then to
conclude that the arahant gets half of his Nibbana here and the other
half hereafter?"
"If Nibbana is the cessation of existence, there is nothing left for
the arahant to experience hereafter. There is absolutely no question of
a hereafter for the arahant."

### The commentary on the Nalaka riddle
"The commentary sidetracks by resolving it into two different problems.
It comprehends the whole idea of not going twice. Only then is the
paradox complete."

## PASS 2 FINDS

- The two Nibbana-elements in full (Itivuttaka) is the definitive
  statement: with residual (five senses intact, defilements extinct)
  and without residual (all feelings cool off).
- The embers going out (Naga sutta) is the precise mechanism: no more
  fuel, flames stop, embers smoulder, then ashes. The living arahant
  is the embers.
- Dabba Mallaputta's self-cremation is the most dramatic moment in
  the series — the arahant using jhanic fire to cremate his discarded
  body, leaving no ashes.
- The lotus practice (untainted in the midst) is the practice image
  for the arahant's daily life — senses working, nothing sticking.

## PASS 3: HOLES AND CONNECTIONS

- **Hole:** The relationship between the two Nibbana-elements and the
  practice path is established but not worked through. The signless
  practice is the sa-upadisesa element.
- **Connection to Sermon 17:** The twig-fire simile is the embers going
  out. The ayatana and the two elements are the same insight from
  different angles.
- **Connection to Sermon 16:** The vacant gaze and the anantarika
  samadhi are the arahattaphalasamadhi that the arahant reverts to.
- **Connection to VBT pack:** The lotus practice IS the VBT body-as-
  consciousness practice — senses working, nothing sticking. The taste
  practice IS the VBT bare-sensing practice.
