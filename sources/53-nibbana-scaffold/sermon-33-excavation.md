# SERMON 33 — EXCAVATION

Source: Nibbana-33.pdf (BCBS/Dharma Seed, free). 7183 words.
(Final sermon of the series: the MahāSaḷāyatanika cure-half, the
mirage, the two ends and the middle — the series synopsis.)

---

## SECTION 1: WHAT NIBBANA IS

### The cure-half of the MahāSaḷāyatanika (knowing and seeing)
"Monks, knowing and seeing the eye as it actually is... one does not
get lustfully attached... for him, who is not lustfully attached, not
fettered, not infatuated, contemplating danger, the five aggregates of
grasping get diminished for the future and his craving... is abandoned,
his bodily stresses are abandoned... his bodily fevers are abandoned...
and he experiences bodily and mental happiness."
Analayo parallel: Ñāṇamoli (1995: 1138); SĀ 305.

### The path perfected through the six bases
"The view of a person such as this is right view... his bodily action,
his verbal action and his livelihood have already been purified
earlier. Thus this noble eightfold path comes to fulfilment in him by
development."
"These two things, namely serenity and insight, occur in him yoked
evenly together." (Yuganaddhā.)

### The four knowledges (the net result)
"He comprehends by direct knowledge those things that should be
comprehended... abandons... ignorance and craving; develops... serenity
and insight; realizes... true knowledge and deliverance."
Paraphrase of the four knowledges: the things to be comprehended are
the five aggregates of grasping; to be abandoned, ignorance and
craving; to be developed, serenity and insight; to be realized, true
knowledge and deliverance.

### The series synopsis: the two ends and the middle
"The comments we have presented here... could even be offered as a
synopsis of the entire series of thirty-three sermons. All what we
brought up in these sermons concerns the question of the two ends and
the middle."

### The closing aspiration
"May it conduce to the attainment of that peaceful and excellent
Nibbāna!"
"Let a myriad arahant lotuses, unsmeared by water and mud, bright
petalled and sweet scented, bloom all over the forest hermitage pond."

## SECTION 2: HOW THE MECHANISM WORKS

### The mirage simile (the gap can never be reduced by running)
"A deer which sees a mirage in a plain from a distance in the dry
season has a perception of water in it. Impelled by that imagining, it
runs towards the mirage... But there is something that the deer is not
aware of, and that is that this gap can never be reduced by running."
"So long as there are two ends, there is a middle. This is a maxim
worth emphasizing. Where there are two ends, there is a middle."
"All these three factors are integral in this perceptual situation.
That is why the gap can never be done away with."

### The arahant stops at the seen (Bahiya recap)
"The emancipated one... stops short at the seen, true to the aphorism
diṭṭhe diṭṭhamattaṃ... He does not go on imagining like that deer,
taking his stand on perception. He does not imagine a thing seen or
one who sees."

### The Chabbisodhana statement (each term explained)
"Friends, with regard to the seen, I dwell unattracted, unrepelled,
independent, uninvolved, released, unshackled, with a mind free from
barriers."
"Mentally he neither approaches nor recedes, though he may appear to do
both physically, from the point of view of the worldling — anupāyo
anapāyo, unattracted, unrepelled. It is the same with regard to the
term anissito, independent. He does not resort to the mirage with the
thought 'Ah, here is a good reservoir'. Appaṭibaddho, uninvolved, he is
not mentally involved in the mirage. Vippamutto, released, he is
released from the perception of water in the mirage, from imagining
water in it. Visaṃyutto, unshackled, he is not bound by it.
Vimariyādikatena cetasā, with a mind free from barriers. What are these
barriers? The two ends and the middle."
Analayo parallel: Ñāṇamoli (1995: 903).

### The mirage seen back (approach/recede paradox)
"The more one approaches it, the farther it recedes, the more one
recedes from it, the nearer it appears. So in regard to the mirage of
percepts... the arahant neither approaches nor recedes."

### Three levels of knowledge (Mūlapariyāya recap)
"The untaught worldling is bound by sense-perception and goes on
imagining according to it... the disciple in training (sekha), since
he has a higher knowledge of conditionality... trains in resisting from
the tendency to imagine. An emancipated one, the arahant, has fully
comprehended the mirage nature of perception."

### Consciousness is the gap itself
"What is called eye-consciousness is the very discrimination between
eye and form. At whatever moment the eye is distinguished as the
internal sphere and form is distinguished as the external sphere, it is
then that eye-consciousness arises. That itself is the gap in the
middle, the intervening space."
"We mentioned that a carpenter, fixing up a door by joining two
planks, might speak of the contact between the two planks... The
concept of touching between the two planks came up because the
carpenter's attention picked up the two planks as separate and not as
one board."

### Craving as glue and seamstress
"The attempt to tie up the two ends by ignoring the middle is
upādāna or grasping. That is impelled by craving, taṇhā."
"It is sometimes called lepa or glue... the gap is continually sought
to be glued up and ignored."
"The verb sibbati or saṃsibbati... sewing and weaving both...
upādāna, grasping or holding on, is an attempt to tie up two ends with
the help of taṇhā."

### The Tissametteyya verse (beyond the seamstress)
"He who, having known both ends, with wisdom does not get attached to
the middle, him I call a great man, he has gone beyond the seamstress
in this world."
Analayo parallel: Bodhi (2017: 330).

### The Isipatana symposium (six interpretations)
Question: "What, friends, is the one end, what is the second end, what
is in the middle and who is the seamstress?"
1. Contact / arising of contact / cessation of contact — craving.
2. Past / future / present — craving.
3. Pleasant / painful / neither feeling — craving.
4. Name / form / consciousness — craving.
5. Six internal bases / six external bases / consciousness — craving.
6. Sakkāya / arising of sakkāya / cessation of sakkāya — craving.
The Buddha: "What you all have said is well said from some point of
view or other. But that for which I preached that verse in the
Metteyyapañha is this" — his ratification = the first (contact).
Analayo parallel: Bodhi (2012: 951–952) (AN Sixes).

### The six as meditation topics
"It is beneath this middle, the cessation of contact, that ignorance
lurks... when what is in the middle is understood, there is
emancipation... If one rightly understands this middle dispassion sets
in, leading to disenchantment, relinquishment and deliverance."

### Touch after touch (Dvayatānupassana)
"Be it pleasant or unpleasant, or neither... all what is felt, knowing
it as 'pain', delusive and brittle, touch after touch, seeing how it
wanes, that way he grows dispassionate therein, by the extinction of
feeling it is that a monk becomes hungerless and fully appeased."
"The seeing of cessation comes only with the insight knowledge of
seeing the breaking up, bhaṅgañāṇa."
Analayo parallel: Bodhi (2017: 952).

### Present preparations (Bhaddekaratta)
"Let one not trace back whatever is past, nor keep on hankering for
the not yet come... But whoever sees that which rises up, as now with
insight... Neither 'drawing in' nor 'pushing on', that kind of stage
should the wise cultivate."
"To reflect on past preparations is relatively easy... It is the
present preparations that are elusive and difficult to muster."
Analayo parallel: Ñāṇamoli (1995: 1045); MĀ 165.

### The counterparts chain (Dhammadinnā–Visākha)
"Unpleasant feeling is the counterpart of pleasant feeling... ignorance
is the counterpart of neither-unpleasant-nor-pleasant feeling...
knowledge is the counterpart of ignorance... deliverance is the
counterpart of knowledge... Nibbāna is the counterpart of
deliverance."
"The neutrality and indifference of equanimity has beneath it
ignorance... When ignorance is displaced, knowledge surfaces."
Analayo parallel: Ñāṇamoli (1995: 402); MĀ 210.

### Release from BOTH form and formless (Dvayatānupassana)
"Those beings that go to realms of form, and those who are settled in
formless realms, not understanding the fact of cessation, come back
again and again to existence. Those who, having comprehended realms of
form, do not settle in formless realms, are released in the experience
of cessation, it is they that are the dispellers of death."
"Sages like Āḷāra Kālāma tried to escape form, rūpa, by grasping the
formless, arūpa. But only the Buddha could point out that one cannot
win release from form by resorting to the formless. Release from both
should be the aim."
Analayo parallel: Bodhi (2017: 288).

### The dog on the plank (consciousness as the reflective water)
"Why does that dog keep on looking at the dog it sees in the water...
Because it is unaware of the reflexive quality of the water.
Consciousness is like that water which has the quality of reflecting on
its surface. What is there between the seen dog and the looking dog as
the middle is consciousness itself."

### The sixth interpretation (sakkāya)
"The term sakkāya... referring to the five aggregates of grasping,
literally the 'existing body'... sat kāya... suggestive of the tendency
to take the whole group as existing, giving way to the perception of
the compact, ghanasaññā. The arising of this notion... is chandarāga or
desire and lust."

### The seamstress's qualifications
"Ponobhavikā nandirāgasahagatā tatratatrābhinandinī... She is the
perpetrator in re-becoming... she delights now here now there."
"Craving draws in with upādāna, grasping, while conceit binds and views
complete the entanglement. That is how existence gets stitched up."

### Knots and rest
"If instead of drawing in the thread to complete the knot it is drawn
out, all what is woven will be undone immediately."
"Existence is a formation of knots and stress. Cessation is an
unravelling of knots and rest."

### The Suddhaṭṭhaka verse
"They fabricate not, they proffer not, nor do they speak of a 'highest
purity', unravelling the tangled knot of grasping, they form no desire
anywhere in the world."
Analayo parallel: Bodhi (2017).

## SECTION 3: WHAT KEEPS THE MECHANISM RUNNING

### Ignorance of the middle
"These interpretations show us that ignoring the middle is a common
trait in the worldling. It is there that ignorance lurks."

### The deer's imagining
"Impelled by that imagining, it runs towards the mirage... The gap is
ignored... What impels one to ignore that gap is craving."

### Beings delight in existence
"Beings... taking delight in existence, bhavarāmā, lusting for
existence, bhavaratā, and rejoicing in existence, bhavasammuditā,
Nibbāna came to be apprehensively misconstrued as tantamount to
annihilation."

## SECTION 4: WHAT THE MECHANISM BUILDS

### The knots stitched
"Craving stitches up... ignoring the middle... Craving draws in with
upādāna, while conceit binds and views complete the entanglement."

### The imagined gap
"The gap, the interstice between them, is something imagined."

## SECTION 5: HOW TO SEE THROUGH IT

### Look at the middle
"Our special attention should be directed to what lies in the middle,
the cessation of contact."
"The six explanations... One can make use of these six as meditation
topics."

### Touch after touch
"Touch after touch, seeing how it wanes, that way he grows dispassionate
therein."

### Present preparations in insight
"In deep insight meditation the attention should be on the present
preparations."

### Yoked evenly together
"These two things, namely serenity and insight, occur in him yoked
evenly together."

## REFUTATIONS

### Ekayano as "the only way"
"Some are tempted to interpret the four foundations of mindfulness as
'the only way'. We have pointed out... that such a conclusion is
unwarranted. Ekāyano does not mean 'the only way', it means 'directed
to one particular destination', that is, to Nibbāna."

### The commentators scared of cessation
"Even commentators were scared of the prospect of a cessation of
existence and tried to explain away Nibbāna through definitions that
serve to perpetuate craving for existence."

### Escaping form by the formless (Alara Kalama)
"Sages like Āḷāra Kālāma tried to escape form, rūpa, by grasping the
formless, arūpa... one cannot win release from form by resorting to
the formless. Release from both should be the aim."
