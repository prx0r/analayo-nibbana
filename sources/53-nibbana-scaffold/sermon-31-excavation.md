# SERMON 31 — EXCAVATION

Source: Nibbana-31.pdf (BCBS/Dharma Seed, free). 9315 words.

---

## SECTION 1: WHAT NIBBANA IS

### The Dhamma as a raft — for crossing, not grasping
"Monks, I shall preach to you the Dhamma comparable to a raft for
crossing over and not for grasping."
"Monks, when you know the Dhamma to be similar to a raft, you should
abandon even good states, how much more so bad states."
Analayo parallel: Ñāṇamoli (1995: 228); MĀ 200.

### The aim restated: three reframes
"The aim of this holy life or this path of practice is non-grasping
instead of grasping; non-identification, atammayatā, instead of
identification, tammayatā; assetlessness, nirupadhi, instead of
assets, upadhi."

### The effortless sequence ends at deliverance
"Thus, monks, mere phenomena flow into other phenomena, mere phenomena
fulfil other phenomena in the process of going from the not beyond to
the beyond."
"In the last analysis, it is only a question of phenomena. There is no
'I' or 'mine' involved."
Analayo parallel: Bodhi (2012: 1340) (Cetanākaraṇīya).

## SECTION 2: HOW THE MECHANISM WORKS

### The raft parable (full)
"Suppose a man in the course of a long journey, saw a great expanse of
water whose near shore was dangerous and fearful and whose further
shore was safe and free from fear. But there was no ferry boat or
bridge going to the far shore."
"Suppose I collect grass, sticks, branches and leaves and bind them
together into a raft, and supported by the raft and making an effort
with my hands and feet I were to get safely across to the far shore."
Wrong response: "Suppose I were to hoist it on my head or load it on
my shoulder and then go wherever I want." — "No, Venerable Sir."
Right response: "Suppose I were to haul it on dry land or set it adrift
in the water and then go wherever I want. Now it is by so doing that
that man would be doing what should be done with the raft."

### Even a purified view not to be possessed (MahāTaṇhāsaṅkhaya)
"Monks, purified and cleansed as this view is, if you adhere to it,
cherish it, treasure it and treat it as a possession, would you then
understand the Dhamma... as similar to a raft...?" — "No, Venerable
Sir!"
"If you do not adhere to it... would you then understand...?" — "Yes,
Venerable Sir!"
Analayo parallel: Ñāṇamoli (1995: 352); MĀ 201.

### Opanayika: leading onward (Udāyi)
"The Dhamma has been well understood by me, Venerable Sir, and that
path has been obtained which, when developed and cultivated, will lead
me onwards to such states as I go on dwelling in the appropriate way."
"The Dhamma has the intrinsic quality of leading onward whoever is
dwelling according to it so that he attains states of distinction
independent of another's intervention."
Analayo parallel: Bodhi (2000: 1588).

### No act of will needed (Cetanākaraṇīya)
"To one who is virtuous, monks, who is endowed with virtue, there is
no need for an act of will like: 'let remorselessness arise in me'; it
is in the nature of Dhamma... that remorselessness arises..."
(→ free from remorse: gladness; → glad: joy.)
"In this way, the Buddha outlines the entire course of training leading
up to knowledge and vision of deliverance... an almost effortless
flow."
Analayo parallel: Bodhi (2012: 1340).

### The six qualities as one chain (svākkhāta → paccattaṃ → opanayika)
"An ill-preached doctrine only promises a goal attainable in the next
world, the well-preached Dhamma points to a goal attainable in this
world itself. Therefore we have to understand the full import of the
epithet svakkhāta in relation to... sandiṭṭhika."
"The meaning of the term sandiṭṭhika is 'visible here and now, in this
very life', as far as the results are concerned... diṭṭheva dhamme
sayam abhiññā sacchikatvā, 'having realized by one's own higher
knowledge in this very life'."
"Since the results are attainable here and now, it does not involve an
interval in time... timeless, akālika... Nibbāna-examination, on the
other hand, yields results then and there and produces the certificate
immediately."
"If the result can be seen here and now, without involving time, there
is good reason for the challenge: 'come and see!' If the result can be
seen only in the next world, all one can say is: 'go and see!'"
"It is not the Buddha who says: 'come and see!', it is the Dhamma
itself that makes this challenge."
"Those who took up the challenge right in earnest have proved for
themselves the realizable nature of the Dhamma, which is the
justification for... paccattaṃ veditabbo viññūhi."
"The inviting nature of the Dhamma leads to personal experience and
that highlights the opanayika quality of leading onwards."

### The deity and Samiddhi (akālika clarified)
"Enjoy, monk, human sensual pleasures, do not abandon what is visible
here and now and run after what takes time!"
"It is not the case, friend, that I abandon what is visible here and
now in order to run after what involves time. On the contrary, I am
abandoning what involves time to run after what is visible here and
now."
"What comes after death is kālika, involving time. It may come or may
not come, one cannot be certain about it. But of what is visible here
and now, in this very life, one can be certain."
Analayo parallel: Bodhi (2000: 98); SĀ 1078.

### The seven tool-kits (37 factors)
"Therefore, monks, whatever dhammas I have preached with higher
knowledge, you should cultivate, develop and practice thoroughly...
They are the four foundations of mindfulness, the four right
endeavours, the four bases for success, the five faculties, the five
powers, the seven factors of enlightenment, and the noble eightfold
path."
"It is as if the Buddha is entrusting to the monks a toolkit before his
departure... an assemblage of seven tool-kits. Each of these seven is
well arranged with an inner consistency."
Analayo parallel: Walshe (1987: 253); DĀ 2; Waldschmidt 1944: 116.

### Satipatthana as foundation (first tool-kit)
"It seems more natural to associate it with the word paṭṭhāna,
'foundation', as the basis for the practice."
"Upaṭṭhitasati... 'this Dhamma is for one who is attended by
mindfulness, not for one who has lost it.'"
"So here we have a basis for the exercise of mindfulness beginning
with a gross object, gradually leading on to subtler objects...
There is a subtle imperceptible interconnection between these four
foundations themselves."
"the colour-light system of the mind in response to feelings, the
alternations between a lustful mind, sarāgaṃ cittaṃ, a hateful mind,
sadosaṃ cittaṃ, and a deluded mind, samohaṃ cittaṃ, as well as their
opposites."
Analayo parallel: Anālayo 2003, Satipaṭṭhāna: the Direct Path,
p. 29f.

### Satipatthana → right endeavours (the second tool-kit links)
"And he also understands how there comes to be the arising of unarisen
sensual desire, and how there comes to be the abandoning of arisen
sensual desire."
"Understanding of the hindrances is the pre-condition for this right
endeavour... With mindfulness and full awareness one sees what is
happening. But that is not all. Right endeavour has to step in."
"Proper attention is actually the basis for right endeavour. Even when
a machine is out of order, there is a need for tightening or loosening
somewhere. But first of all one has to mindfully scan or scrutinize
it."
Analayo parallel: Ñāṇamoli (1995: 151, 153); MĀ 98; Anālayo 2013,
Perspectives on Satipaṭṭhāna, p. 174ff.

### The four right endeavours as ascending effort
"For the non-arising of unarisen evil unskilful mental states he
arouses a desire, makes an effort, puts forth energy, makes firm the
mind and endeavours..." (four times — unarisen-evil, arisen-evil,
unarisen-skilful, arisen-skilful.)
"Chandaṃ janeti refers to the interest or the desire to act.
Vāyamati suggests effort or exercise. Viriyaṃ ārabhati has to do with
the initial application of energy. Cittaṃ paggaṇhāti stands for that
firmness of resolve or grit. Padahati signifies the final all out
effort or endeavour."
Analayo parallel: Ñāṇamoli (1995: 636).

### The rock simile (toppling the mass of suffering)
"Suppose there is a rock which we want to get out of our way... we put
some kind of lever underneath it... we at least give our shoulders an
exercise... we heave slowly slowly... it looks as if the rock is
precariously balanced... So we grit our teeth and make a firm resolve...
With one deep breath... we push it away with all our might."
"One can substitute for it the gigantic mass of suffering to make it
meaningful in the context of the Dhamma."

### The four-factored effort
"Verily let my skin, sinews and bones remain, and let the flesh and
blood dry up in my body, but I will not relax my energy so long as I
have not attained what can be attained by manly strength, by manly
energy, by manly exertion."
Analayo parallel: Ñāṇamoli (1995: 584); MĀ 195.

### The four bases for success (iddhipāda)
"The basic sense of iddhi is 'success' or 'proficiency'. For instance,
samiddhi means 'prosperity'... a 'base for success'."
"In the case of the bases for success, it is said that even one would
do, as the others fall in line."
Temperaments: "Venerable Raṭṭhapāla... belonged to the chanda-category,
and Venerable Mogharāja had vīmaṃsa as his forte." (Commentarial.)
"If by relying on desire, monks, a monk gets concentration, gets
one-pointedness of mind, this is called desire-concentration."
"Vīmaṃsā, or investigation, is paññā, or wisdom, in disguise."
Analayo parallel: Bodhi (2000: 1729–1730) (Chandasutta).

### The five faculties (pañcindriya)
"Faith, saddhā; energy, viriya; mindfulness, sati; concentration,
samādhi; and wisdom, paññā."
"Saddhā, or faith, is chanda, desire or interest, in disguise."
"Where is the faculty of faith to be seen? In the four factors of
stream-entry." — confidence born of understanding in Buddha/Dhamma/
Saṅgha + virtues dear to the Noble Ones.
"Mindfulness... stands in the middle of the group of leaders without
taking sides. In fact, its role is the preserving of the balance of
power... the balancing of faculties."
"So in this tool-kit of faculties, sati is the spanner for tightening
or loosening, for relaxing or gripping."
"And where, monks, is the faculty of concentration to be seen? In the
four absorptions." Or exceptionally: "having made release its object."
"Where is the faculty of wisdom to be seen? In the four noble truths."
(In insight: "knowledge of rise and fall, udayatthagāmini paññā.")
Analayo parallel: Bodhi (2000: 1670–1671, 1693); SĀ 646.

### Sāriputta's chain of faculties (Indriyasaṃyutta)
"It could indeed be expected of a noble disciple who has faith that he
will dwell with energy... that he will be mindful... that he will gain
concentration, will gain one-pointedness of mind, having made release
the object... that he will understand thus: 'This saṃsāra is without a
conceivable beginning... But the remainderless fading away and
cessation of ignorance... the stilling of all preparations... the
destruction of craving, detachment, cessation, extinction.' That
wisdom of his, Venerable Sir, is his faculty of wisdom."
Analayo parallel: Bodhi (2000: 1693).

## SECTION 3: WHAT KEEPS THE MECHANISM RUNNING

### Cherishing even the purified view
"If you adhere to it, cherish it, treasure it and treat it as a
possession..."

### Chasing the time-involving
"Enjoy, monk, human sensual pleasures... run after what takes time!"
"Sensual pleasures are time involving, fraught with much suffering,
much despair, and that more dangers lurk in them."

## SECTION 4: WHAT THE MECHANISM BUILDS

### The sequence itself (the built path-structure)
"A long sequence of mental states is interconnected in a subtle way,
according to the principle of relativity, leading onwards as far as
final deliverance itself."
"In this sequential progression, we see an illustration of the quality
of leading onward, opanayika."

## SECTION 5: HOW TO SEE THROUGH IT

### Scan first, then act
"First of all one has to mindfully scan or scrutinize it... But based
on that scrutiny, the four right endeavours play their role in regard
to unskilful and skilful mental states."

### Specialize by temperament
"One comes to know one's strongpoint, where one's forte lies...
One might recognize chanda, desire or interest, as one's strongpoint...
Another might discover that his true personality emerges in the thick
of striving... One who belongs to the wisdom category is never tired of
investigation."

### The tool-kits taken up together
"These seven tool-kits are interconnected. From the satipaṭṭhāna
tool-kit, the sammappadhāna tool-kit comes out as a matter of course."

## REFUTATIONS

### The bare-attention-only tendency
"There is at present a tendency among those who recommend
satipaṭṭhāna meditation to overemphasize the role of attention. They
seem to assert that bare attention or noticing is all that is needed...
probably the attempt to specialize in satipaṭṭhāna in isolation,
without reference to the rest of the thirty-seven participative factors
of enlightenment. These seven tool-kits are interconnected... That is
why bare attention is not the be all and end all of it."

### Akālika corrupted to "eternal"
"Unfortunately, however, the term akālika also suffered by much
commentarial jargon. Meanings totally foreign to the original sense
came to be tagged on, so much so that it was taken to mean 'true for
all times' or 'eternal'."

### Iddhi as psychic power
"It is easy to mistake it as a base for psychic power. But the basic
sense of iddhi is 'success' or 'proficiency'."

### Satipatthana etymology dispute
"The term satipaṭṭhāna has been variously interpreted by scholars,
some with reference to the term paṭṭhāna and others connecting it with
upaṭṭhāna."

  endeavours, bases, faculties) — powers, factors of enlightenment and

  the faculties. Note for the merge: the tool-kit tour is unfinished
  by design (series ends at 33).

  cittānupassanā but only sketched — no extended treatment.
- **Connection to Sermon 30:** Relay-of-chariots relativity continues
  as the six-qualities chain and the effortless sequence; pragmatism/
  relativity named again as the twin principles.
- **Connection to Sermon 27:** The exam/certificate simile (Sermon 1)
  returns inside akālika.
- **Connection to Sermon 1 (Rādha chain):** the effortless sequence
  (virtue→regret→joy...) is another chain ending at nibbana like
  rivers in the sea.
- **Connection to Sermon 4:** Satipaṭṭhāna as the first tool-kit is
  the scaffold Section 5's core; this sermon's inter-toolkit argument
  answers "is attention enough?" (no).
- **Connection to VBT pack:** The scan-then-act technique and the
  temperament specialization map to the VBT practice-notes; the
  bare-attention refutation is directly relevant to VBT positioning.
