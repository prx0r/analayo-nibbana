## SECTION 1: WHAT NIBBANA IS

### Nibbana = the three deliverances
"It is on rising from that attainment [of saññāvedayitanirodha], which
is like a deep freeze, that one makes contact with the three deliverances,
the signless, animitta, the desireless, appanihita, and the void, sunnata."
"It is through this unique vision of the Signless, the Desireless, and
the Void, that the Buddha arrived at the state of stilling of all
preparations."

### Cessation of perception and feeling is NOT nibbana
"In view of the fact that Nibbana is called the stilling of all
preparations, one might sometimes conclude that the attainment of the
cessation of perceptions and feeling is in itself Nibbana. But it is on
rising from that attainment... that one makes contact with the three
deliverances."

## SECTION 2: HOW THE MECHANISM WORKS

### The painter simile (Second Gaddulasutta)
"Just as a dyer or a painter would fashion the likeness of a woman or
of a man, complete in all its major and minor parts, on a well planed
board, or a wall, or on a strip of cloth, with dye or lac or turmeric
or indigo or madder, even so the untaught worldling creates, as it were,
his own form, feelings, perceptions, preparations, and consciousness."
"What the Buddha wants to convey... is the insubstantiality and the
vanity of those five groups. It brings out their compound and made-up
nature."

### The five similes (Pheṇapiṇḍūpamasutta)
"Form is like a lump of foam, feeling like a water bubble, perception
like a mirage, volitions like a plantain trunk, and consciousness like
an illusion, so explained the Kinsman of the Sun."
"These five similes bring out the insubstantiality of the five grasping
groups. Their simulating and deceptive nature is indicated by the
similes. Not only the magic show, but even the other similes, like the
mass of foam, are suggestive of simulation, in giving a false notion of
compactness."

### The Khajjaniyasutta definition of sankharas
"They prepare the prepared — that is why they are called preparations.
And what is the prepared that they prepare? They prepare, as a prepared,
form into the state of form, feeling into the state of feeling,
perception into the state of perception, preparations into the state of
preparations, consciousness into the state of consciousness."
Analayo parallel: SĀ 46.

### Sankhara = preparation, intention, imagination
"The two terms abhisankhatam abhisancetayitam are often found in
juxtaposition, as if they are synonymous. Abhisankhatam means 'specially
prepared', and abhisancetayitam means 'thought out' or 'intended'. Here
we see the relationship of sankharas to intention. The preparation is
done by means of intentions."
"It is, in the last analysis, something constructed by imagination. All
of these five groups are thought-constructs."

### The three kinds of sankhara (singular form)
Vibhangasutta (Nidana-samyutta): "What are preparations? There are these
three preparations: body-preparation, speech-preparation, and
mind-preparation."
"The term is given in the singular. In the majority of instances it is
found in the plural number, but here in the definition of the term the
singular is used."

### Dhammadinna's definitions (Culavedallasutta)
"In-breaths and out-breaths are bodily, these things are bound up with
the body, that is why in-breaths and out-breaths are a body-preparation."
"First having thought and pondered one breaks into speech, that is why
thinking and pondering are a speech-preparation."
"Perception and feeling are mental, they are bound up with the mind,
that is why perception and feeling are a mind-preparation."
Analayo: MĀ 210 does not have the definition; the Tibetan parallel does.

### The iceberg analogy
"Just as in the case of an iceberg floating in the ocean, the greater
part is submerged and only a fraction of it shows above the surface, so
also the deeper nuances of this term are rather imperceptible. Beneath
our heap of body actions, verbal actions, and mental acts of willing or
intentions lies a huge mountain of activities."

### The back-stage simile
"If one can get the opportunity to see either a rehearsal or the
back-stage preparations for a drama, one would be able to see through
the drama. If one can steal a peep into the back-stage make-up
contrivances of actors and actresses, one would see how ugly persons can
become comely and the wretched can appear regal. One would then see what
a 'poor show' it is."
"In the case of a film show or a drama, the preparations remain as
preparations so long as there is that darkness of ignorance."

### The Cetanāsutta (AN, Sancetaniyavagga)
Section 1: "When the body is there, due to bodily intention, there arises
inward pleasure and pain... all conditioned by ignorance."
Section 2: "Either he himself prepares that bodily preparation... Or
else others prepare for him... Either he, being fully aware... Or else
he, being fully unaware."
Section 3: "In all these cases, ignorance hangs on. But with the
remainderless fading away and cessation of ignorance, that body is not
there, owing to which there can arise for him inward pleasure or pain...
That field is not there, that site is not there, that base is not there."

### The three characteristics of craving
"Craving has three characteristics: ponobhavika (leads to re-becoming),
nandiragasahagata (accompanied by delight and attachment), and
tatratatrabhinandi (delighting here and there)."
"Ponobhavika... is craving that connects up one moment of existence with
another. One who is seeing a film show connects up the first scene with
the second, in order to understand the latter."
"Craving in particular is like a glue. In fact, a synonym for it is
lepa, which means a 'glue'. Another synonym is visattika, an 'adhesive'
or a 'sticky substance'."

### The Kevaddha verse (anidassana vinnana)
"Non-manifestative consciousness, endless and all-lustrous, in which
earth, water, fire, and wind do not find a footing... where long and
short, fine and coarse, pleasant and unpleasant, as well as name-and-form
are held in check in a way complete. It is by the cessation of
consciousness that all these are held in check."
"The Buddha recommended a restatement of the riddle: 'Monk, that is not
the way you should put it.' The question is not about a cessation of
the four primaries somewhere in the world. The correct way to put it,
is to ask where the four great primaries do not find a footing."

### The dog on the plank
"Sometimes we see a dog, crossing a plank over a stream, stopping half
way through to gaze at the water below. It wags its tail, or growls, or
keeps on looking at and away from the water, again and again. Seeing its
own image in the water, it imagines that to be another dog. So it either
wags its tail in a friendly way, or growls angrily, or else it keeps on
stealing glances out of curiosity — love, hate, and delusion."
"The dog thinks that it is looking because it sees a dog. But what is
really happening? It is just because it is looking that it sees a dog.
If the dog had not looked down, it would not have seen a dog looking up
at it from below, that is to say — its own image."
"Here lies the secret of Dependent Arising."

### Dvayatanupassana: name-and-form as illusion
"Just see the world, with all its gods, fancying a self where none
exists, entrenched in name-and-form it holds the conceit that this is
real."

## SECTION 3: WHAT KEEPS THE MECHANISM RUNNING

### The film reel with no spectator
"If a film reel is played at a time when there is no spectator, no film
show will be registered anywhere, because there is no mind to put
together. It merely flashed on the screen. But if someone had been there
to receive it, to contact with his sense-bases... then there comes to be
a film show."
"Film producers and dramatists think that the production of the film
and the drama is solely their work. But in the last analysis, it is the
audience that gives the film and the drama the finishing touch."

### Sakkayaditthi (personality view) = the dog's error
"We tend to think that every object in the world exists in its own
right. But then this is what is called sakkayaditthi, the 'personality
view', which carries with it the self-bias. It is such a view that made
the dog imagine that there is another dog in the water."

### Paticca = the special word
"The term paticca samuppada is not to be found in any other philosophical
system. The special significance of the term lies in the word paticca."

### Dukkha = the five grasping groups
"Sankhittena pancupadanakkhandha dukkha — in short, the five grasping
groups are suffering."
"Upavana, I have declared that suffering is dependently arisen.
Dependent on what? Dependent on contact."

### Pre-Buddhistic ascetics failed
"They went on calming down the bodily activities to subtler and subtler
levels. They calmed down the in-breaths and out-breaths, they managed
to suppress thinking and pondering by concentration exercises, but
without proper understanding. It was only a temporary calming down."
"Once they reached the level of neither-perception-nor-nonperception,
they had to face a certain problem. The very designation of that level
of attainment betrays the dilemma they were in."
Pancattayasutta: "Perception is a disease, perception is a boil,
perception is a dart, but not to have perception is to be deluded, this
is peaceful, this is excellent, that is, neither-perception-nor-non-
perception."
"They thought that these are only states of a soul. Therefore, like the
present day Hindu Yogins following the philosophy of the Upanisads, they
thought that breathing is just one layer of the self."
"The 'kernel' of self was supposed to have around it the four rinds,
annamaya, pranamaya, sanjnamaya, and vijnanamaya."

## SECTION 4: WHAT THE MECHANISM BUILDS

### The five groups as painted artefacts
"The worldling creates his own form, feelings, perceptions, preparations,
and consciousness — as a painter fashions a likeness on a board."
"Form like foam, feeling like bubble, perception like mirage, volitions
like plantain trunk, consciousness like an illusion."

### Sakkayaditthi = self-bias
"It is such a view that made the dog imagine that there is another dog
in the water. It imagined that the dog is there, even when it is not
looking."

### The winding and rewinding
"The entire world, or samsaric existence in its entirety, is a process
of winding and rewinding. Where the winding ends and the rewinding
begins is a matter beyond our comprehension."
"We go on heaping up karmic actions, but when the time comes to
experience their consequences, we do not regard them as mere results of
karma, but superimpose an 'I' on that experience."
"This is like a tangled skein. Sometimes, when an unskilled person tries
to disentangle a tangled skein while disentangling one end, the other
end gets entangled."

### Dancing as madness
"This, monks, is a form of madness according to the noble one's
discipline, namely dancing. Now what is the nature of a madman? He is
jumpy. From the standpoint of Dhamma, dancing is a form of jumpiness.
In fact, all preparations are that. It shows a nervous stress as well
as a nervous release. It is an endless series of winding and unwinding."

## SECTION 5: HOW TO SEE THROUGH IT

### Insight vs tranquillity
"What tranquillity meditation does, is to temporarily calm them down and
derive some sort of happiness. That too is necessary from the point of
view of concentration, to do away with restlessness and the like, but
it does not dispel ignorance. That is why, in insight meditation, one
tries to understand preparations for what they are by dispelling
ignorance. The more one sees preparations as preparations, ignorance is
dispelled, and the more one dispels ignorance, the preparations lose
their significance as preparations."

### The back-stage view
"If one sees these back-stage preparations with wisdom, one would be
disenchanted."

### The sign has to leave with the desire
"The sign stands for the notion of permanence and it accounts for the
deceptive nature of preparations... So that sign has to leave together
with the desire, for the Desireless Deliverance to come about."

## SECTION 6: WHAT REMAINS

### This, in other words, is the state of Nibbana
- "Now all these are related to ignorance. Therefore, at whatever point of time this ignorance ceases completely in someone, then for him there is no consciousness of a body, though from an outside point of view he appears to have a body. He may use words, he may speak, but for him there is nothing substantial in linguistic usage."
- "He seems to be making use of a mind, mindobjects also come up, but he does not regard it as a unit. Therefore, inwardly, no pleasures and pains come up."
- "With the cessation of ignorance comes the cessation of preparations. Thereby all pleasures and pains cease. This, in other words, is the state of Nibbāna."
- "It appears, then, that this discourse gives us a clue to the state of Nibbāna."
- The Cetanasutta formula itself (the remainderless fading away; that body,
  field, site, base not there) is filed in section 2; this is its payoff
  passage - what life looks like from inside when the unit stops.

### It removes the field, the ground, the base
- "Since all the instances mentioned earlier are accompanied by ignorance, the utter fading away and cessation of that very ignorance prevents, as it were, the crystallization of that body, speech, and mind, due to which inward pleasure and pain can arise. In other words, it removes the field, the ground, the base and the provenance for the arising of inward pleasure and pain."
- Dual membership: the formula is section 2 material; this exposition - the
  cessation removing the provenance of inward pleasure and pain - is what
  remains.

### Preparations cease to be preparations (Upatissa and Kolita)
- "It is such a situation of non-enjoyment that we happened to mention in the previous sermon with reference to the witnessing of a hill-top festival by Upatissa and Kolita. They had a flash of insight due to the light of wisdom that came from within, not due to any illumination from outside. Because of it, those preparations ceased to be preparations. From this we can understand that the term saṅkhāra becomes meaningful only against the background of ignorance."
- The hill-top festival insight was introduced in the previous sermon; here
  its meaning is stated: saṅkhāra is saṅkhāra only against the background of
  ignorance - drop the background and the preparations stop being
  preparations.

### The winding ends
- "Where the winding ends and the rewinding begins is a matter beyond our comprehension. But one thing is clear all these comes to cease when craving and grasping are abandoned."
- The VOICE bucket carries the first half as a one-liner; the full sentence is
  the sermon's closing statement on what stops.


## REFUTATIONS

### The pre-Buddhistic tranquillity approach
"The kind of tranquillity meditation followed by the pre-Buddhistic
ascetics... could never bring about a stilling of all preparations. Why?
Because the ignorance underlying those preparations were not discernible
to their level of wisdom. In the least, they could not even recognize
their saṅkhāra nature."

### The commentary on the Kevaddha verse
"The commentary advances several interpretations to this verse. Being
unable to give one definite meaning, it suggests several." Nanananda
proposes his own interpretation based on the mode of analysis developed
in the sermons.

### The sankharas as kamma
The Khajjanīyasutta definition is NOT about kamma. "There is no explicit
mention of karma here." Sankharas here mean the basic activities:
breathing, thinking, perceiving — not moral actions.

## PASS 2 FINDS

- The dog on the plank is one of the best similes in the entire series —
  "it is just because it is looking that it sees a dog." This is the
  dependent origination of perception in one image.
- The three characteristics of craving (ponobhavika, nandiragasahagata,
  tatratatrabhinandi) are mapped to the cinema: connecting scenes,
  delighting, glue. This is a complete practical teaching on craving.
- The Khajjaniyasutta definition — "they prepare the prepared" — is the
  most precise definition of sankhara in the series.
- Dhammadinna's definitions (breathing = body-preparation, thinking =
  speech-preparation, perception/feeling = mind-preparation) are the
  most practical — they tell you exactly what to watch in meditation.
- The iceberg analogy — the submerged part is the real sankhara work.
  Breathing, thinking, perceiving are all sankharas with ignorance hidden
  in them.
- The Kevaddha verse is introduced here but not fully worked through —
  that's Sermon 7-8's job.

## PASS 3: HOLES AND CONNECTIONS

- **Hole:** The Kevaddha verse (anidassana vinnana) is introduced but
  not explained. Nanananda says "we shall not translate, at the very
  outset, the first two lines" — that's a deliberate deferral to Sermons
  7-8.
- **Hole:** The relationship between the three sankharas (breathing,
  thinking, perceiving) and the practice of stilling them is established
  but not worked through. That's the satipatthana practice, Sermon 5.
- **Connection to Sermon 5:** The cinema simile is continued and deepened.
  The three characteristics of craving are the glue that holds the movie
  together. The back-stage view is the insight meditation.
- **Connection to Sermon 4:** The two bulls / conjoined pair is the same
  insight as the dog on the plank — the illusion of separation.
- **Connection to Sermon 3:** The Cetasutta (intention, construction,
  latency) is the same three-fold structure as the three sankharas.
- **Connection to Sermons 7-8:** The anidassana vinnana is the signless
  consciousness where the four elements find no footing. That's the next
  sermon's topic.
- **Connection to VBT pack:** The dog on the plank maps to the "body as
  consciousness" practice — seeing that the self you're looking for is
  your own reflection. The three sankharas map to the VBT breath and
  gap practices — watching breathing, thinking, perceiving as preparations.
