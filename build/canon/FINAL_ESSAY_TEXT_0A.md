# THE ESSAY — seed (0) + A (rung A, analayo-only listen)

## 1. What is Nibbana?

PLAIN: Nirvana is when everything the mind makes comes to a stop, the letting go of everything carried, the thirst ends, nothing pulls you any more, stopping, going out, like a flame with no fuel.

TEXT: This is peaceful, this is excellent, namely the stilling of all
preparations, the relinquishment of all assets, the destruction of
craving, detachment, cessation, extinction.

## 2. Why don't I see Nibbana right now?

PLAIN: You do not see Nirvana right now because you imagine a seer and a world, and neither is there.

TEXT: Due to the very ignorance of the fact that it is seeing because it is looking, the dog imagines another dog, there, in the water. What is called maññanā is an imagining of that sort.

TEXT: Only a looking is there, nothing worth looking at. Only a seeing is there, nothing to be seen. Apart from the bare act of hearing, there is nothing to be heard.

TEXT: There is no seer, apart from the phenomenon of seeing, according to the Tathāgata... For the worldling, the bare act of seeing carries with it a perception of 'one who sees'.

## 3. What keeps it going?

PLAIN: Thirst keeps it going: impelled by that making it into something, it runs towards the mirage. The gap is ignored. What impels one to ignore that gap is thirst.

TEXT: Impelled by that imagining, it runs towards the mirage... The gap is
ignored... What impels one to ignore that gap is craving.

## 4. How is the sense of self constructed?

PLAIN: The sense of self is constructed like this: when one looks into a mirror one suddenly becomes self-conscious. Even with no such notion, the moment one looks one is reminded of it, as if to exclaim: 'Ah, here I am!'

TEXT: It is when one looks into a mirror that one suddenly becomes self-
conscious... Even if one already has no such notion, the moment one looks
into a mirror one is suddenly reminded of it, as if to exclaim: 'Ah,
here I am!'

## 5. How is Nibbana attained?

PLAIN: Nirvana is only attained now: in seeing, just the seeing, with no I added. Then it will not carry you. Nothing to stand on, nowhere to land — neither here, nor there, nor in between. This itself is the end of suffering.

TEXT: In the seen there will be just the seen... Then you will not be by it. And when you are not by it, you are not in it. And when you are not in it, you are neither here nor there nor in between. This itself is the end of suffering.

## 6. What is Nibbana like?

PLAIN: like a flame with no fuel.

TEXT: I am grown cool, extinguished as I am.

## A1. What is Nibbana?  (beats [1, 3, 6, 11])

- "Khayamatta na nibbanam"
- "To project Nibbana into a distance and to hope that craving will be destroyed only on seeing it, is something like trying to build a staircase to a palace one cannot yet see."
- "To think that the destruction of craving is not sufficient is like trying to give water to one who has already quenched his thirst."

- "What is the use of a well, if water is there all the time, having cut craving at the root, in search of what should one wander?"

- Samsara or reiterated existence is itself a debt, which one can never pay off. When one comes to think of kamma and its result, it is a debt that keeps on gathering an interminable interest.
- Even from this debt the arahants have won freedom by destroying the seeds of kamma, by rendering them infertile.

## A2. Why don't I see Nibbana right now?  (beats [1, 2, 10])

- Where the winding ends and the rewinding begins is a matter beyond our comprehension. But one thing is clear all these comes to cease when craving and grasping are abandoned.

- In order to ascertain whether a banana trunk is pith-less, it is not
necessary to go on removing its bark, layer after layer, from top to
bottom. We only have to take a sharp sword and cut the trunk in the
middle, so that the cross-section will reveal to us its pith-less nature.
Similarly, if we cut in the middle the banana trunk of preparations with
the sharp sword of wisdom, its internal structure as revealed by the
cross-section will convince us of the essence-less nature of the group
of preparations.

## A3. What keeps it going?  (beats [1, 4, 16, 17])

- Gods and men delight in existence, they are attached to existence...
When Dhamma is being preached to them for the cessation of existence,
their minds do not reach out towards it.

- Dependent on eye and forms, friend, arises eye-consciousness; the concurrence of the three is contact; because of contact, feeling; what one feels, one perceives; what one perceives, one reasons about; what one reasons about, one proliferates; what one proliferates, owing to that, reckonings born of prolific perceptions overwhelm him in regard to forms cognizable by the eye relating to the past, the future and the present.

- Like a promontory, it juts out into the ocean of samsara. When it comes to rebirth, it is craving that bridges the apparent gap.

## A4. How is the sense of self constructed?  (beats [1, 2, 5, 6, 7, 8])

- It is when one looks into a mirror that one suddenly becomes self-
conscious... Even if one already has no such notion, the moment one looks
into a mirror one is suddenly reminded of it, as if to exclaim: 'Ah,
here I am!'

- He declares that he has seen well in advance 'the barb on which
mankind is hooked impaled'. The barb is none other than the dogmatic
assertion, 'I know, I see, it is verily so'.

- The magical illusion created by consciousness is so complete that it is capable of playing a dual role, as in double acting. Because it reflects, like a mirror, consciousness itself is grasped, just as one grasps the mirror. Not only the reflection of the mirror, but the mirror itself is grasped.

- The primary significance of papañca is traceable to the linguistic medium. We have already shown how the network of grammar spreads as soon as the peg 'am' is driven down to earth, as it were. The reality in the first person in grammar beckons a second and a third person to complete the picture.

- Just as a dyer or a painter would fashion the likeness of a woman or of a man, complete in all its major and minor parts, on a well planed board, or a wall, or on a strip of cloth, with dye or lac or turmeric or indigo or madder, even so the untaught worldling creates, as it were, his own form, feelings, perceptions, preparations, and consciousness.

## A5. How is Nibbana attained?  (beats [1, 4, 5, 15])

- With the idea of sharpening the razor, if one grabs it tightly and
rubs it on the sharpening stone, it will only become blunt. Paramasana,
grasping, grabbing, is something like that.

- What tranquillity meditation does, is to temporarily calm them down and
derive some sort of happiness. That too is necessary from the point of
view of concentration, to do away with restlessness and the like, but
it does not dispel ignorance. That is why, in insight meditation, one
tries to understand preparations for what they are by dispelling
ignorance. The more one sees preparations as preparations, ignorance is
dispelled, and the more one dispels ignorance, the preparations lose
their significance as preparations.

- Animittañca bhāvehi, mānānusayamujjaha — 'Develop the signless, too, and give up the latency to conceit!'

## A6. What is Nibbana like?  (beats [1, 2, 4, 11, 14, 15, 16])

- Now, for the arahant, the whole world appears like a chessboard.

- It is as if the unending game of chess with all its vicissitudes has
ended in a peaceful draw.

- All those who came out of that hall, except this discerning one, were
spectators. He was not one of the audience, because he had an insight
into the magic show from his hiding place on the stage.

- Just as in the case of a fire blazing like a block of iron in point of compactness, when it gradually calms down, no path it goes by can be traced.

- Our entire saṃsāric existence is a chess game between the organic, upādiṇṇa, and the inorganic, anupādiṇṇa. The four elements within this body, the grasped par excellence... and the four elements as nutrition and atmosphere are always in conflict in their game of chess.

- For whom there is neither a farther shore, nor a hither shore, nor both, who is undistressed and unfettered, him I call a Brahmin.
